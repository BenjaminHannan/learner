# 8a size ladder: the 10M result and the shape probe (2026-10-08, 4 AM ET)

Spec: `8A-SPEC-2026-10-07.md` (PR #50, mirrored at `design/8a-bigger-is-better-2026-10-07.md`). Code: build branch
`claude/project-thread-f1to6a` at 50ee171632 (PR #51). Every run: one rented RTX 5090 per seed, 24,000 updates of 256
rows, the same 60M-piece pool (38% our questions, 62% web fill-in rows), the same global caps (addendum G).

## Plain summary for Ben

We made our model (B2) about three times bigger and it did not get better. Over six tries it went from 72.3% to 72.8%
on the test; we needed about +3 points. The ordinary transformers trained on exactly the same practice did get better
with size: the step-writing one went up about 3.7 points and the plain language-model one about 15.6.

Looking question type by question type: B2 is already almost perfect on the arithmetic stories its built-in calculator
handles, so there is nothing left to gain there. What it gets wrong are "spot the rule" questions (continue a pattern,
work out a rule from examples, order people by age). The plain transformers slowly get better at those with size; B2
does not, whichever way we grew it. Our best guess is that B2's fixed calculator steps and its one-shot answer writer
cannot express those answers, so extra size has nowhere useful to go.

We also tried growing B2 two other ways (a much deeper reader, and wider instead of deeper). The deeper reader was flat
and broke the letter-code questions. Wider was slightly better than deeper (about +1 point) but nowhere near +3.

So today's B2 does not pass the "bigger is better" test. Running the 30M size (about $42, about 14 hours) cannot make it
pass any more. The decision for you is whether to run 30M anyway, or hold it and get outside opinions first (recommended).

## 5. What it means (ranked; labels as CLAUDE.md asks)

1. **B2 is saturated where its calculator works and cannot express the rest** (suggested; best fit). Shown: near 100%
   on the calculator types at both sizes; flat on the rule and pattern types where PT gains. Untested: whether the op
   set (add, subtract, multiply, divide, mod, min, max, compare) and the parallel one-shot GEN answer writer can express
   those answers at all. Astra's prompt asks for that trace from the code.
2. **The extra size went into a part that is not the bottleneck** (suggested, weakened). Depth, a deep reader and width
   all gave under +1.6.
3. **Plain models only gain because they start lower** (suggested, weakened). PT at 10M (69.97) is still below B2 at 3M,
   but B2 is flat even on the types where it is below PT.
4. **A training problem at 8 blocks x 12 loops** (untested; gradient norms not checked). W (3 blocks, lower learning
   rate) gained a little more but did not fix it.

## 7. What this changes in the roadmap

- 8a does not pass (mark 1), so today's B2 does not meet 8c's entry ("8a pass"). The roadmap's "proved wrong" line for
  8a (3M-to-30M under +2) is not formally reached without 30M, but no shape we tried is on track for +3 a step.
- 8c is built only on B3 after B3 shows the 3M-to-10M gain under 8a's marks (unchanged). This result raises the bar for
  B3's design: whatever B3 is, its 3M-to-10M step is now the deciding test, and PT shows that a plain transformer on
  this pool does scale (+3.8 at this step).
- One candidate for the next single change (suggested, not sealed): keep what scales (the plain step model) and give
  it the exact calculator as an outside tool, which is the direction of Ben's 2c2 ("the calculator works just as well
  outside"). Marks would be sealed in their own dated addendum before anything runs.
