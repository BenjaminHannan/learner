# Speed study, third GPU run: cheap wins, accum 8 and 16, and what accum 4 / 8 do to early learning (three rented RTX 4090s, 10-14 6:43 PM to 10-15 12:30 AM ET)

Marks were fixed before the run in `PREREG-b3g2-speed-ab.md` (Amendments 1 to 5 are all dated before the result files they concern were read; the Result log at its end has the unedited tables). Raw outputs: `gpu-run-3-box1/`, `gpu-run-3-box2/`, `gpu-run-3-box3/`. Tables made by `gpu_run_3_tables.py` and `gpu_run_3_pairs.py` from those files. All figures are on a rented 4090, never on the PC's 5070 Ti. Every dollar figure is wall time x the rate the instance really billed, not the box's own dollar lines (they used the rate given at creation).

## Plain version
- **None of the cheap speed-ups is worth adopting.** On a steady box, three interleaved rounds each (accum 4, B3G2 3M): removing host syncs (`nosync`) -0.1 %, TF32 +0.1 %, padding the prompt width (`pad64p`) +2.2 %, padding all three widths (`pad64`) -2.0 % (slower), turning the fused optimizer off (`unfused`) +0.2 % in the earlier box's steady rounds. The bar was 5 %. Fused AdamW is already on in `train.py`, so there is nothing to switch on there.
- **Accum 4 is the fastest setting; bigger micro-batches than that cost speed.** Same box, same minute: accum 8 takes 1.45x as long as accum 4 (3.26 s vs 2.24 s per update), accum 16 takes 2.7x (6.01 s). My prediction that neither would pass the 1.3x-faster mark was right.
- **The gate-3 result stands, and accum 8 shows the same pattern at about half the size.** The first 300 updates of accum 4 sit above accum 32 by 0.21 (seed 400) and 0.25 (seed 401) in mean loss; accum 8 sits 0.11 above. The registered fail line is 0.06. Almost all of it is in the first 150 updates (the loss falls from about 17 to 4 there); in the last 50 updates the gaps are small (accum 4: +0.023 and +0.040; accum 8: +0.018; the line is 0.05). One accum-4-vs-accum-4 pair (seed 401 vs seed 400) differs by 0.04, so this statistic has noise of about that size (one pair only). So: the gap is bigger than seed noise at accum 4 (about 5x), and at accum 8 about 2.7x (shown); whether it matters for a 24,000-update result is untested.
- **Money:** one 24,000-update B3G2 3M run on a 4090 at $0.4563/h: accum 4 about 15 h, $6.8 to $7.0; accum 8 about 21.7 to 22.8 h, $9.9 to $10.4; accum 16 about 40 h, $18.3; accum 32 about 75 h, $34. The three boxes of this sweep cost about $3.0 together.
- **One of my own tools was wrong and is fixed.** The profiler section printed "GPU busy 99.8 %, GPU-bound" because it counted torch's per-step range marker as a kernel. Corrected from the saved tables (subtraction, not re-measured): real kernel time is about 1.4 s per update, 39 to 41 % of the profiled wall time (the profiler slows the update; against the unprofiled 2.25 s it would be at most about 63 %), mean kernel 18.7 microseconds, 70,600 launches per update. So the GPU is not saturated; the update is made of very many tiny kernels (launch-bound, suggested). Nothing in the A/B timings, equivalence checks or pair runs used this section.

Why `nosync` did nothing (suggested, untested): the corrected profile says the GPU is busy only about 40 to 63 % of the time, but the idle time is the CPU launching 70,600 small kernels per update, not the CPU waiting on the GPU, so removing the waits does not make the launches faster. The premise I wrote in the pre-registration (the GPU idled 79 % of the time at accum 32, from the passive sampling) was a measurement, but the reading that syncs were the cause was my guess.

## Box 1's slow stretch, and what box 3 says about it
Box 1 (US host) ran about 2.5x slower for about 65 of its 113 bench minutes (A 5.5 s/update instead of 2.22; `nosync` round 3, all of `pad64p` and `pad64`, `tf32` rounds 1 and 2), then went back by itself. Box 3 (NL host) had a host monitor writing every 10 s (GPU clock, power, temperature, throttle reasons, load, CPU steal; `gpu-run-3-box3/hostmon.csv`) and **had no slow stretch**: the median GPU clock was 2,685 MHz (the 210 MHz lines are idle moments), the median power 185 W, the temperature 27 to 43 C, and CPU steal was 0 in all 299 samples; all four arms were steady (A spread 1.008 to 1.019). So the monitor shows what a healthy box looks like but cannot explain box 1's stretch; the cause stays **unexplained** (hypotheses, untested: a busy neighbour on that host, CPU or GPU throttling there). Rule used: read only normal-speed rounds, arms with fewer than 3 are "regime-confounded" and were repeated on box 3 (all four decided there).

Reading (shown = from these tables; suggested = my interpretation):
- **Shown:** the gap to accum 32 is ordered accum 4 (+0.21, +0.25) > accum 8 (+0.11) > 0. Between accum 4 seeds the difference of means is 0.04 and the first window differs by 0.24 (accum 4 vs accum 32 differs by 0.85 and 1.09 in that window), so the accum-4 gap is several times the seed difference seen here. Accum 8's first-window gap (+0.37) is 1.5x the seed difference seen between the two accum-4 runs.
- **Shown:** the gaps to accum 32 shrink with training. Windows 201-250 and 251-300: accum 4 seed 400 +0.058 and +0.023, accum 4 seed 401 +0.108 and +0.040, accum 8 +0.010 and +0.018.
- **Suggested, untested:** the gap is an early-learning effect of sharing one loop length K across more rows per micro-batch (accum 4: 64 rows share one K; accum 32: 8 rows), and it closes by itself. Whether anything is left at update 24,000 is not known; no accum-32 run of this model has finished (G2f died at update about 500).
- **Caveats:** one seed for accum 8 and accum 32, two for accum 4; the accum-8 pair crosses two hosts (box 3 NL vs box 1 US; same code, same data, same seed; GPU rounding differs); 300 updates is an early probe, and the first 100 updates carry most of the difference; "diff of means" is dominated by the loss falling from 44 to 6.

Verdict (mine, Ben decides): accum 8 with `--soft-trace-a`. The deviation is disclosed above; the accum 8 gap is the smallest of the cheap recipes and the speed gain over accum 32 is about 3.4x.
