STYLE SHEET FOR GRADING GRAMMAR (exp 241, M1). Sealed by the director on 2026-09-22 before any sweep was seen.

You grade one reply at a time: GRAMMATICAL or UNGRAMMATICAL, with a short reason.
- A reply is UNGRAMMATICAL if ANY sentence in it breaks any rule below, or is not standard written English.
- Grade grammar only. A stiff but correct sentence ("Tarno's city is Vell.") is GRAMMATICAL.
- Never judge whether a fact is true. Never judge the names or values themselves: fillers are made-up words and may look odd.

House rules. Breaking any of these is an error.
1. Possessives
   - A singular name takes 's, including names ending in s, x or z ("Tomas's", "Brix's").
   - A bare apostrophe after a singular name ("Tomas' job") is an error.
   - A plural common noun ending in s takes a bare apostrophe ("the parents' house"). "parents's" is an error.
   - An "of" form or a verb form that avoids the possessive is fine.
2. Lists: "A and B", "A, B and C". No Oxford comma: "A, B, and C" is an error. No empty slots ("people: .", "A, , B", "and ."). No stray separators.
3. Numbers in running text
   - Counts 0–9 are words ("three sisters"); 10 and up are digits.
   - Digits inside a value (a date, a year, a phone number, an address, a code) are fine.
   - A bare zero count ("0 people", "zero people") is an error. Say "no one", "nobody", "none" or "I don't know anyone".
4. Agreement
   - Count and noun must agree ("one person", "two people"). "1 people" and "2 person" are errors.
   - Subject and verb must agree ("X lives", "they live"; "does X live", not "does X lives").
   - Verb forms must be right ("was born", not "is born" for a birth; "has worked", not "has work").
5. Articles: "a" before a consonant sound and "an" before a vowel sound, by sound ("an hour", "a university", "an MBA"). No missing article where English needs one ("X is teacher" is an error). No doubled article.
6. Sentence form
   - Each sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with . ? or !
   - No doubled words ("the the"), no missing words, no run-on sentences joined only by a comma (comma splices).
   - Brackets and quotes must be balanced.
   - A question is phrased as a question and ends with "?".
7. Typography: no space before punctuation, no doubled punctuation ("..", "?.", ".,"), no double spaces, no leading or trailing space.
8. No machine text: raw keys, underscores or placeholders ("date_of_birth", "{name}", "<X>", "None", "null", "[]") are errors, unless the user's own words contained them.
9. "(worked out backwards)" must follow the statement it qualifies, inside or right after the same sentence. A dangling, doubled or orphaned one is an error.
10. Pronouns: "I/me/my" for the assistant and "you/your" for the user are fine. Singular "they" is fine. Pronoun choice is not a grammar error unless it breaks agreement or has no clear referent in the same reply.

Output for each reply: id, GRAMMATICAL or UNGRAMMATICAL, and a reason naming the rule number (or "general" for other standard-English errors) plus the exact offending words.
