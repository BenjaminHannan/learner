# When I would stop, and what I would try instead

20 September 2026. Stopping advice only; simulator design, budgets and numerical thresholds belong to the separate `design/v3/20-*` specification and preregistration. No experiments run here.

**Cheapest reason to stop spending:** the baseline-only calibration shows that an ordinary GRU or transformer already meets the preregistered success limits at the smallest tested discovery budget, including transfer, the genuinely different held-out mechanism family, and no-hidden-variable controls. Its reliability across all scheduled seeds must also leave no worthwhile advantage to pursue. I would then shelve the proposed concept machinery for this benchmark before building it. Saturation on the single-scalar case alone only rejects that case as a discriminator; it does not reject the broader direction.

**Cheapest direct reason to abandon the proposed mechanism:** if calibration leaves headroom, run one bounded comparison against an ordinary fixed-state learner with the same information, objective, adaptation rights and total compute. If the uncertainty interval rules out the preregistered minimum useful gain, and there is no preregistered transfer or reliability advantage, I would stop this implementation line. A noisy tie or one failed seed is inconclusive. This rejects the claim that explicit concept machinery earns its cost, not the possibility of machine invention.

Report every scheduled seed, apply the advance definition of “never started,” and include those failures in the primary decision. Keep machine-specific outcomes visible; never select whichever machine makes a seed look best. Unresolved execution instability prevents a clean architectural verdict, although it can justify pausing investment.

**What I would try instead:** if the remaining bottleneck is insufficient evidence, use an ordinary learner and investigate choosing experiments whose outcomes separate its competing explanations. The target would be fewer observations needed for reliable discovery, counting the computation spent choosing experiments. This is established [active causal experimental design](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/agrawal19b.html), not a new invention mechanism or a claim of superiority over transformers; competing models must receive the same action choices. Its selection signal is disagreement between explanations, rather than the previously proposed rate of improvement on practice questions. If calibration reveals no evidence-acquisition bottleneck either, I would park this branch instead of adding another module without a measured problem to solve.
