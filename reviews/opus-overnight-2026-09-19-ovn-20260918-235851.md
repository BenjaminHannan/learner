## The short version (for Ben)

### What we learned

- **The model's memory cards can hold only one kind of information.**
  - When the model stores a fact such as "Mira's colour is blue", it squeezes the line into one summary on a
    card. A useful card needs two things: *who it is about* ("Mira, colour"), so the right card can be found,
    and *what it says* ("blue"), so the answer can be read off it.
  - Tonight's measurements show the single summary ends up keeping only one of the two.
  - The model is not misreading the sentence: every word is still there in its reading of the line. The loss
    happens only when the line is squeezed into the card.
  - Training decides which side wins, and so does luck: two runs with identical settings went opposite ways.
- **That one flaw explains three separate failures:**
  1. The model never learned to pick the right card among similar ones. The cards had forgotten who they were
     about.
  2. When it searched for cards itself, it often found the right card but could not read the answer. The card
     had forgotten the answer.
  3. Results flipped between random seeds.
- **Chaining two lookups works only for practised combinations.** The model can learn "look up Mira's friend,
  then look up the friend's colour" for combinations it practised. For a new combination of steps it already
  knows, it finds the first card but almost never the second.
  - The reason: at the second step it asks for the **wrong kind of fact**.
  - It always asks for a kind it practised in that position. It knows how to ask "what is X's shoe size?" as a
    first question, but never learned to ask it as a second one.
- **Some ideas did not help:**
  - making the "thinking" step subtract its shared drift;
  - checking only the last thinking step's answer during training;
  - letting the answer-writer see the question.

  The first didn't restore reading, and none of the three taught the model to choose among cards.

### What improved

- **Milestone 2 is closed.** Both repairs let the model copy an answer from a supplied card: 1024/1024 on the
  untouched test, on two seeds. The repaired models also passed the change-the-fact checks.
- **Copying from a supplied card now works on the new ladder task too:** 512/512, 323/323 and 189/189 on test.
- **Searching and then reading works on one seed, with the right training schedule.** Start with a long phase
  where the model only reads, then 4000 steps. The whole pipeline then answered:
  - 356/512 one-step questions;
  - 154/323 practised two-step questions (test).

  With the default schedule it answered only 32/512 (validation). The long reading phase on a second seed (at
  1500 steps) failed: 27/512 even when handed the right card. So this is not a reliable fix.
- **The cause is now measured, not guessed.** That points to a cheap, specific fix.

### What remains uncertain

- Whether giving each card two summaries (a "who/what" slot and an "answer" slot) removes the race. This is
  untested.
- Most results come from one seed. The one replication, seed 1, came out opposite.
- Whether building the second search question from the question's own relation (candidate B) fixes new
  combinations. The failure is now located: the second search asks for the wrong relation. The fix is untested.
- Everything is a tiny toy (32-number vectors, made-up words). Retention of learned skills was not tested at all.

### Best next step and why

**Give each card two separate summaries.**
- The "who/what" summary is used for finding the card; the "answer" summary is used for reading it.
- Compare three versions, each on at least two seeds with the same training:
  - the current single summary;
  - two summaries;
  - a plain average of the line.

**Why this one:**
- It costs 33 extra numbers.
- It targets the exact flaw we measured.
- Every other plan depends on cards keeping both kinds of information: choosing among facts, chaining lookups,
  and the gold-card arm of the planned solvability comparison (D-gold).

## 2. The ladder task (new, `premonition/toy_ladder.py`)

- **World.** 6 people, drawn from 16 entity ids per visit, so every visit has fresh bindings. Each person has 3
  attributes with 16 possible values, and one "friend" link.
- **Gap.** A filler block of at least 128 tokens separates the facts from the questions. That is beyond the
  reader's 64-token window, so facts can reach the answer only through memory.
- **Questions.**
  - 1-hop: "ENT REL?".
  - 2-hop: "ENT LINK REL?", meaning the friend's attribute.
  - Relation 2 is **never asked as 2-hop in training**. Held-out 2-hop questions are therefore *familiar steps
    in a new combination*.
  - No unseen operation was tested tonight.
- **Supplied-card sets (gold-evidence diagnostic).**
  - 1-hop gets 4 mirror cards: the gold card, the same person with another relation, and another person with the
    same relation and with another relation.
  - 2-hop gets 6 cards:
    - the gold link and the gold attribute;
    - the asker's own attribute (a 1-hop shortcut);
    - a decoy chain: a decoy link, the decoy's attribute, and a card for the decoy chain's start.
- **Shortcut audit.** The best question-blind rule on validation scores 53.7% on 1-hop and 53.4% on 2-hop.
  - An earlier design allowed 90.6% ("object of any link"), and a later one about 87%.
  - Both were redesigned before any training.
- **Chance.** 1/16 = 6.25%.
- **Length and steps.**
  - A 2-hop question is one token longer than a 1-hop question and needs two facts.
  - "Read 2" isolates length plus step count without decoys: the same 2-hop question with only its two gold
    cards. It succeeds (341/341, 323/323 on test). So the extra token and the second fact are not the problem;
    choosing among decoys is.

**The smallest task that separates reading from combining.**
- The 2-hop question with its two gold cards, against the same question plus a mirror decoy chain.
- 4 cards are enough: the gold link, the gold attribute, the decoy link and the decoy attribute. By symmetry,
  every question-blind rule then scores ≤ 50% (a design property, not measured tonight).
- The gold pair alone is solved by "copy the only attribute card", so it tests reading, not combining.
- The smaller 1-hop pair (the gold card plus a card differing only in the person) separates reading from
  *choosing*, which combining needs first.

  So the question knows *whom* it asks about, but the cards no longer say whom they are about.

    - Which fact survives depends on the schedule, and also on the seed: the same schedule gave opposite

    - The information is lost in the pool, not in the reader.

**Shortlist, ranked** (each with a plain example, mechanism, prior art, costs, privileges, competitor, and

3. **Register-only Think** (facts stay read-only). Known elsewhere as Perceiver IO latents; untested; it needs
   (1) first.

- Rejected: row-centred Think; final-loop-only supervision; the gate as a reasoning enabler; the question-aware
  read alone; the milestone-2 guess "several cards cause the own-retrieval failure" (refuted).
- Postponed: early_ans 0 had no effect; TST (a throughput method, identical to patch-level training, not our
  bottleneck); continued-learning pilots; normalisation tricks.
