This is about one system only: a pretrained 3.7B chat model that learns a simulated user's facts by nightly fine-tuning ("sleep"). Please do not mix in advice for other kinds of models (for example small from-scratch models with an explicit card memory); that is a separate project.

## 1. The setting

- **Model:** K2-Horizon-3.7B (a pretrained chat model with a "think" block it can open before answering), bf16. Sleep trains a LoRA adapter (rank 16, alpha 32, all attention and MLP projections, 34M trainable parameters), lr 2e-4, batch 16 cards + 8 replay texts.
- **The week:** 7 simulated days of chat between a user ("Sam") and the assistant, ~2,100 turns. Sam mentions invented people, places and things (nonsense names such as "Kizibra", "Foperukin") in passing while asking for help with other tasks. 264 facts: 64 said once, 100 said on 2 days, 100 said on 4 days; plus 30 facts that change mid-week and 20 routines.
- **Each night**, after that day's chat:
  1. The base model extracts subject-relation-object facts from the day's messages. A fact becomes "eligible" for sleep once it has been mentioned on 2 or more days.
  2. **NREM:** for each eligible fact, the base model writes ~16 paraphrases and question-answer cards ("Where does Kizibra's mentor live?" style is NOT here; these are single facts: "Who mentors Kizibra?" -> "Vebribon"). Training steps on these cards are mixed with a KL penalty that keeps the model's outputs on generic text close to the original model.
  3. **REM ("two-step cards"):** pairs of eligible facts that share a name (for example "Vebribon mentors Kizibra" + "Vebribon lives in Foperukin") are given to a writer model, which thinks and then writes ONE question that needs both facts ("Where does Kizibra's mentor live?"); the answer is the far end ("Foperukin"). Each pair is tried in both directions, twice per cycle, 4 cycles per night, until it has one kept card. A card is kept only if (a) it parses as a single question, (b) it names the start, not the shared middle name and not the answer, (c) it is not one of the test questions, and (d) the base model, shown the relevant chat lines and the question, writes the answer within its token limit. Kept cards are trained as plain question -> answer (no think block) on that night and every later night.
  4. A "downscaling" step shrinks the adapter's small singular values a little (synaptic homeostasis idea).
  5. **Morning gate:** if ARC-Easy accuracy (a general-knowledge benchmark) on a validation slice drops more than 3 points below its pre-sleep score, the whole night's training is undone.
- **Test after day 7 (weights only, no notes, think block closed):** forward recall of the 2- and 4-day facts (200 questions), reverse, **two-hop (78 questions such as "Where does the mentor of Kizibra live?")**, changed facts, routines; ARC-Easy 500; and a "diary" condition where the model reads retrieved notes.
- **Goal:** two-hop >= 75 of 78, weights only, no thinking at test time, without hurting the rest (recall C2+C4 >= 93.5%, ARC-Easy within 5 points, diary within 5 points).

## 2. Results so far

**History (shown):** without two-step cards, two-hop was 3.8% (3/78). Arm B (the recipe above, with 2x the REM practice steps) reached **50.0% (39/78)**, p < 1e-4 vs 3.8%. Single-fact recall was 96.5% in a similar recipe whose night 7 was kept; in B it fell to 82.5% because the gate undid night 7 (see below). Asking B to think before answering made two-hop worse (38.5%). On new wordings of the single-fact questions, recall matched the usual wording (no sign of memorising exact strings).

**The gate's decisions in B (shown):** the gate used 100 ARC validation questions. Pre-sleep score 0.90; night scores 0.84, 0.86, 0.82, 0.88, 0.93, 0.89, 0.86. Nights 1, 2, 3 and 7 were undone. ARC-Easy on the 500-question test set after night 6 was 89.2 vs 89.4 before sleep, so suggested: most undos were noise.

**Two-step cards kept per night in B (shown):**

| night | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cards kept / samples written | 6/138 | 15/346 | 32/612 | 0/1072 | 78/1920 | 96/1586 |
| writer's thinking tokens per sample | 453 | 442 | 327 | 17 | 390 | 431 |
| night undone by the gate | yes | yes | no | no | no | yes |

On night 5, 1064 of 1072 samples never closed their think block, at 17 tokens each. The writer was the model as trained so far (adapter on). Suggested: night 4's training broke the writer's thinking. In an earlier generation-only probe with the untrained base model as writer (different, development week; one night): of 537 samples, 72 were kept, 244 failed check (b), 210 failed check (d), 9 were test questions, and 2 failed to parse. In the rejected examples, the checker often named the answer only after a lead-in and was cut off at its 20-token limit. Some pairs were one fact extracted twice ("Glisemu" / "Glisemu as a mentor"). And capped thinking often ran on after the question on the same line.
