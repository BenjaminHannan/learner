# Literature that changes a design choice

All linked pages were opened during this task. **Established** below means demonstrated/described in that source's setting; **your inference** means Astra's proposed transfer. None establishes that an approximately 80k-parameter from-scratch toy will learn latent roles or answer-only retrieval. No literature claim substitutes for the local paired controls.

1. **Established:** Pointer Networks produce distributions over input positions for structured outputs. **Your inference:** preserve discrete token locations when literal copying is the hypothesis, but supervised pointing results do not establish role discovery from answer loss. With the new local role readouts, pooled querying comes first. [Vinyals et al., Pointer Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03134).

2. **Established:** SCAN evaluates systematic generalisation under controlled compositional splits and exposes failures of tested sequence models. **Your inference:** reserve whole question/evidence arrangements, not just new random worlds; positional success cannot identify a latent role rule. This does not prove that increasing this toy to eight relations would fix learning. [Lake and Baroni, Generalization without Systematicity](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lake18a.html).

3. **Established:** TP-Transformer uses learned role/filler interactions and reports task-trained role structure in a substantially different model/task setting. **Your inference:** latent role structure is possible without supplying every linguistic role label, but head semantics here remain non-identifiable until tested against competing positional rules. This motivates diagnostic head assignment and role-decoy layouts, not a tensor-product architecture as the next build. [Schlag et al., Enhancing the Transformer with Explicit Relational Encoding](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06611).

4. **Established:** straight-through estimators are heuristic gradient estimators for discrete computations; score-function approaches provide a different bias/variance tradeoff. **Your inference:** require hard-forward verification and separate direct value gradients from score gradients before interpreting the existing ST screen. A biased estimator can work, and its failure is not an impossibility proof. [Bengio et al., Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3432).

5. **Established:** REALM models retrieval as a latent choice, approximates marginalisation over top-ranked documents, warm-starts retrieval with an inverse-cloze task, and starts its encoder from pretrained BERT. It includes a null document. **Your inference:** candidate-marginal answer likelihood is a credible retrieval-credit diagnostic, but REALM is not evidence for this toy's random-initialization cold start. A soft mixture of document values is not the same objective as marginalising answer likelihood over discrete documents. Exact enumeration can be expensive and does not dominate every optimization route at fixed compute. [Guu et al., REALM, paper body](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/guu20a/guu20a.pdf).

6. **Established:** XTDB distinguishes valid time from system time for bitemporal queries. **Your inference:** represent “true at effective tick t” and “believed using observations through s” separately, including late observations and retrospective corrections. Adopt the semantics, not a dependency on that database. [XTDB time documentation](https://docs.xtdb.com/about/time-in-xtdb.html).

7. **Established:** Cassandra's tombstone documentation explains how previously deleted replicated data can reappear if deletion information disappears before stale replicas are reconciled. **Your inference:** correction authority must outlive stale answers in weights/replay/diaries or force unavailability. Neural weights are not Cassandra replicas; a successful finite weights probe is not a safe analogue of complete replica repair. This changes the guard-release policy. [Apache Cassandra, Tombstones](https://cassandra.apache.org/doc/latest/cassandra/managing/operating/compaction/tombstones.html).

8. **Established:** Dark Experience Replay stores logits sampled through the optimization trajectory and replays them; its experiments report benefits, including with non-final teacher outputs. **Your inference:** do not assert that its only possible benefit is image-class similarity or that non-final logits must be useless. Nevertheless, changing fact truth, stale targets and stored-logit bytes create additional design obligations here. Defer it until ordinary replay and direct cards-to-weights practice have been measured. [Buzzega et al., Dark Experience for General Continual Learning](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/b704ea2c39778f07c617f6b7ce480e9e-Paper.pdf).

9. **Established:** Prabhu and colleagues evaluate continual-learning methods under explicit computational budgets and find strong replay-based baselines in their vision setting. **Your inference:** charge maintenance, extra forwards, replay generation and model snapshots before claiming an advantage. Their result does not show replay is universally optimal, nor that village consolidation cannot improve on it. [Computationally Budgeted Continual Learning: What Does Matter?](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Prabhu_Computationally_Budgeted_Continual_Learning_What_Does_Matter_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf).

10. **Established:** product-key memory uses structured keys to retrieve a small subset of a large value table; the paper studies query normalization and capacity/utilization. Author code exposes uniform subkey initialization, normally initialized values and query batch normalization. **Your inference:** if a pilot is later justified, reproduce these controls at a small size, count table and optimizer state, and separate routing from value changes. This is not an exact identity/version store. [Lample et al., Large Memory Layers with Product Keys](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/9061-large-memory-layers-with-product-keys.pdf), [author memory implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/XLM/main/xlm/model/memory/memory.py), [author initialization utilities](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/XLM/main/xlm/model/memory/utils.py).

11. **Established:** the sparse-memory fine-tuning preprint studies continual adaptation with large sparse memory components and selective updates in pretrained systems. **Your inference:** fixed-routing/frozen-core controls are worthwhile if PKM reaches this project, but scale, pretrained knowledge and task differences preclude transplanting its success claim to the toy. [Continual Learning via Sparse Memory Finetuning, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15103v1).

12. **Established:** the fast-weight PKM preprint investigates inference-time memory updates for long-context use. **Your inference:** fast inference writes would violate the current weights-only read-only evaluation contract unless explicitly designated a separate state source. Long-context retrieval success does not establish persistent correction authority, sleep consolidation or replay superiority. Defer this route. [Fast-Weight Product Key Memory, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00671v1).

**Suggested — literature boundary.** No reviewed source settles role learning in this exact from-scratch model, the utility of score-only ST at the proposed budget, or whether this persistent village needs anything beyond version-correct replay. Those decisions require the local experiments; references justify controls and caution, not a preselected winner.
