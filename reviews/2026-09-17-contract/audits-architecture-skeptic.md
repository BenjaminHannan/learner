STRONGEST ATTACK: The 2-hop test (Episode B, and the four-arm 'adaptive retrieval versus extra computation' comparison) can be passed or failed by write-time pre-composition, and the proposal's own reverse-query requirement makes that shortcut available in BOTH teaching orders. The writer is specified as "input (x_teach, z_T)", where z_T is the workspace after T recurrent steps that read W. When "e7 is red" has been taught and "e5's friend is e7" arrives, the reasoner can retrieve e7's colour during those T steps and the writer can store "e5 -> friend-colour red" under e5's key; the 2-hop query "what colour is e5's friend" then needs one constant read, so arm (a) (x-only key, the draft's constant-read design) matches arm (b). The proposal's only defence is "order conditions", but the substrate is also required to answer "reverse" queries, which means the writer must learn to store the reverse binding e7 -> is-friend-of e5; once it does, the reverse order ("e5's friend is e7" first, then "e7 is red") is pre-composable too, because at the second teaching the reasoner can look up e7, find e5 via the reverse binding, and write the composed fact. The stated decision rule, "If (c) closes the gap, extra computation alone suffices and the retrieval claim fails", does not name what follows when (a) closes the gap, so the arm comparison can neither confirm nor refute workspace-conditioned retrieval. Episode D compounds this: "practice is log-verified entailment" generates exactly the composed facts that turn 2-hop into 1-hop, and nothing visible sequences the retrieval-arm test before practice. The cheap kill is a control arm in which the writer's z_T is computed with W masked during teaching (writer sees x_teach only); pre-composition is impossible there, and the (a)-vs-(b) gap under masking is the number that actually bears on adaptive retrieval.
ERRORS:
- "It has the same bytes if capped at 96 entries." The proposal's own table gives the cache 96 × (48 + 1) = 4,704 float32 = 18,816 bytes against 36,864 for each substrate; the cache has the same entry count at half the bytes, not the same bytes.
- "Sham write: writer enabled but v replaced by zero → chance." For substrate (i) with W initialised to zero, the delta update with v = 0 is W' = W + (0 − Wk)k^T = W(I − kk^T), which is identically zero on a zero W; the sham arm is the removal arm under another name and provides no independent control for the delta bank (it is a genuine control only for (ii) and (iii)).
- "a Wilson 95% interval at p = 0.9 has half-width about 1.3 points at n = 2,000 (deduction)." The arithmetic is right for independent trials, but the ~10 queries per episode share one W, one set of teachings and one writer trajectory, so the effective unit is the episode (n = 200 per condition per seed); a cluster-honest interval at p = 0.9 is roughly four points, and the stated thresholds (≥ 90%, ≥ 75%, ≥ 70%) are separated from each other by less than that.
- "halt an arm when validation query accuracy improves by less than one point over three consecutive ten-minute runs, or after twelve runs. Expect plateaus" — the stopping rule halts inside precisely the plateau the next sentence predicts (R13 extra 6; R9 C1), so a plateaued arm is recorded as converged rather than as undertrained; the two sentences contradict each other.
- "the composition result may be entirely explained by θ having learned a 30-way program classifier plus six executors" versus "induced from demonstrations over a program space with never-seen elements": a 30-way classifier over ordered pairs requires every pair to have appeared in meta-training, which contradicts held-out pairs; if the pairs are genuinely unseen, the classifier is two 6-way identifications, which is the weaker (and cheaper) reading and should be stated as the expected mechanism, not a risk.

STRONGEST ATTACK: The meta-training stopping rule contradicts the proposal's own convergence warning and will manufacture uninterpretable nulls. It says "halt an arm when validation query accuracy improves by less than one point over three consecutive ten-minute runs, or after twelve runs", while two paragraphs later it says to "Expect plateaus" citing Kirsch et al.'s ~35k-step plateau during which unseen-task accuracy is flat or worsening, and grokking's 10^5-10^6 step delays. An accuracy-based <1-point-per-30-minutes rule halts exactly inside such a plateau. The arms this hits are the ones that carry the research claims: arm (b) on 2-hop queries (the reasoner must learn to re-key from z, a within-episode learning-to-learn behaviour that sits behind the plateau) and Episode C's held-out-pair composition (a SCAN-style 'around right' split where basic seq2seq gets ~51% error and only meta-learning over many episodes closes it, R3 C4). The plan then classifies "nothing passed PC3" as "uninterpretable, not negative", so the most probable outcome of the ~36 GPU-hour plan is a pile of uninterpretable results. Compounding this, the 36-hour figure covers only 6 configurations x 3 seeds x 12 runs; it omits PC0, PC1 and PC2 training (each its own training regime per substrate, ~7 extra configurations, ~40 more hours), the parameter-fitting baseline (per-episode fine-tuning over 200 test episodes), the N95 sweep to N=128, and the pre/post-restart evaluation passes. Realistic cost is 2-3x the stated figure, or 5x at the 'five preferred' seeds.
ERRORS:
- Sham-write control is wrong for the delta substrate. "Sham write: writer enabled but v replaced by zero -> chance" and "Unrelated items must stay within +/-3 points of the sham-write control": for a delta bank with beta=1 and unit key, v=0 gives W' = W + (0 - Wk)k^T = W(I - kk^T), an active erasure of every stored component along k. On a fresh W=0 this is a no-op, so the chance claim holds, but as the interference/damage baseline it damages earlier items whose keys overlap k, inflating baseline damage and masking real interference. For (iii) MLP_mem an SGD step toward a zero target is likewise destructive. The sham must be a skipped write (beta=0) or v := Wk, not v := 0.
- Byte accounting is internally inconsistent. The baseline paragraph says the explicit cache "has the same bytes if capped at 96 entries", but the table gives Cache 96 x (48+1) = 4,704 floats = 18,816 bytes versus 36,864 bytes for each substrate; that is half, not equal. Either cap the cache at ~188 entries (which raises its capacity above the substrates') or state that it is a half-byte baseline. Minor: (iii) MLP_mem 48*96 + 96*48 = 9,216 omits the 144 bias parameters (9,360 floats, 37,440 bytes) unless biases are explicitly excluded.
- Inference unit mismatch. "a Wilson 95% interval at p = 0.9 has half-width about 1.3 points at n = 2,000" is arithmetically right (1.96*sqrt(0.09/2000)=0.013), but the plan then says "treat the seed as the unit for arm comparisons" with "Three meta-training seeds minimum". At n=3 seeds the per-query Wilson interval is irrelevant to the arm comparison, and "replicated in every seed" is a sign test with p=1/8 under a no-direction null. The 2,000-query interval also assumes independent queries; queries within one episode share the same W and encoder state, so the effective n is closer to the 200 episodes for any W-level error.
- N95 is measured out of the writer's training distribution. PC2/PC3 train and are thresholded at N=12, PC1 at N<=32, yet "N95 is the largest N in {8,...,128} with >= 95% recall". A learned key/writer that never saw >12 sequential writes in meta-training will show interference at N>12 for length-generalisation reasons, so N95 confounds substrate capacity with the writer's episode-length generalisation. Either meta-train with N sampled up to the ceiling or report N95 only for PC1 (fixed keys) as the substrate number.
UNSUPPORTED:
- "With theta frozen and z cleared, any above-chance recall can come only from W (deduction)." This is a deduction only if entity-answer bindings are re-randomised every episode (MANN-style label shuffling, R4 C2; Chan et al. R13 extra 2). If the same bindings recur across meta-training episodes, theta can store them in-weights and the frozen-theta recall is not attributable to W. The visible excerpt does not state re-randomisation; if section 3 does, this is fine, otherwise it is a leak.
- "Larimar's near-100% up to 512 edits at memory size K = 512 then 82% at 1024 is the calibration shape to expect." Larimar uses a pseudo-inverse/least-squares Kanerva write with a 6B LM encoder; a 96x96 delta bank with learned keys, one SGD step per write and no replay has no published result of that shape. This is a hypothesis, not a calibration.
- "Method threshold: >= 70% exact match on discriminating held-out-pair queries (hypothesis)": the proposal offers no positive control isolating the executors (given the program identity as an explicit token, can frozen theta execute it on novel 4-symbol operands?). Without it, a sub-70% result cannot be attributed to induction, execution, or memory, so the 70% figure is not diagnosable.
- "That is 6 configurations x 3 seeds x <= 12 runs ~ 36 GPU-hours plus evaluation, over a few days": arithmetic is right (216 runs x 10 min = 36 h) but the scope is wrong; PC0-PC2 training, the parameter-fitting baseline, the N95 sweep and restart evaluations are not costed.
- "a difference >= 15 points ... supports workspace-conditioned retrieval": no power justification is given for the 15-point margin, and with 3 seeds and from-scratch 10-minute training the between-seed variance (cf. Banino et al. on ACT 'great variance across seeds') could exceed it for training reasons alone.
GRAFT:
- The log-poisoning check: overwrite the audit log with wrong answers after teaching and require 100% agreement of scored answers with and without poisoning. Cheap, decisive against silent retrieval from records.
- The cross-episode W swap (score episode-1 queries with W2 -> chance, with W1 -> pass) and rollback-to-pre-correction-snapshot tests; together with removal they make 'W carries the information' a causal claim rather than a correlation.
- The signed-damage treatment: paired per-item correct->incorrect and incorrect->correct flip counts, signed mean like GEM's BWT, and the explicit derivation E[max(0, eps)] = sigma/sqrt(2*pi) showing why clipped damage is biased upward.
- Dropping the RL heads, skip head and target-answer embedding from the writer in favour of a bi-level outer loss on post-clearing queries plus an auxiliary support loss (meta seq2seq's 99.95% -> 5.43% without support loss). This removes the RL-variance risk entirely from milestone one.
- The four-arm adaptive-retrieval design (key from x vs (x,z); T=6 vs T=12; four parallel reads) at equal parameters and bytes, with the explicit rule that if arm (c) closes the gap the retrieval claim fails.
- The explicit table of what counts as falsification versus broken component, and the refusal to report ten-minute nulls without PC0-PC3 as negatives.
- Scoring correction and all headline numbers only after clearing and process restart, with the pre-restart numbers reported as a leak diagnostic.
MISSING:
- No positive control for the 2-hop re-keying path specifically: a teacher-forced second hop (oracle intermediate entity injected into the key) would separate 'reasoner cannot learn to re-key' (undertrained) from 'adaptive retrieval not needed' (hypothesis fails). The ladder PC0-PC3 diagnoses the facts path only.
- No executor positive control for Episode C (program identity given explicitly; test execution on novel operands), so a method-threshold failure is not diagnosable as induction vs execution vs memory, contrary to the brief's demand to separate an undertrained component from a wrong hypothesis.
- No paraphrase positive control: the held-out-template hypothesis (>= 75%) can fail purely because a from-scratch GRU encoder over a ~50-token vocabulary does not generalise to an unseen word order, and PC0 is thresholded on 2-hop and composition, not on the held-out template. The number of meta-training templates that makes 'held-out template' meaningful is not stated in the visible excerpt.
- Uncertainty estimates at the unit the plan itself declares (the seed) are absent; the stated 1.3-point interval is at the query level. A rule for excluding seeds that fail PC3 before arm comparisons is not given, so training-variance failures and hypothesis failures are conflated by 'replicated in every seed'.
- The budget omits several required stages (PC0-PC2 training per substrate, parameter-fitting baseline, N95 sweep, restart passes), so the brief's request to say what can and cannot be established within the initial budget is only partly met.
- Sections 1-3 and the component-visibility table were not in the excerpt provided to me; I cannot confirm that binding re-randomisation per episode, the exact teaching representation, or Episode D's verification source are specified, and the deduction 'recall can come only from W' depends on the first of these.
VERDICT: I would adopt this as milestone one with amendments, because its control structure (log-poisoning, W swap, rollback, signed damage, four retrieval arms, no RL writer) is the strongest of the designs I have seen and nothing in it quietly requires a pretrained LM, large data, or more than a few GPU-days. Convergence is plausible inside chained ten-minute runs for the facts, paraphrase and correction path (meta seq2seq reached 99.95% in under an hour on a Titan X with 10k episodes, and these nets are under 1M parameters), but it is doubtful for arm (b) 2-hop and for Episode C's held-out-pair composition, which sit behind the learning-to-learn plateau and a SCAN-style split, and the proposal's own accuracy-plateau stopping rule would halt training exactly there. I would change four things: replace the stopping rule with a validation-loss criterion plus a minimum run count and a memorise-first curriculum; redefine the sham write as a skipped write (v := Wk or beta = 0) since v := 0 is an erasure on a delta bank; add executor and teacher-forced-second-hop positive controls so Episode C and 2-hop failures are diagnosable; and re-cost the plan honestly at roughly 80-100 GPU-hours with PC0-PC2 and the baselines included, either sampling N up to the capacity ceiling during meta-training or reporting N95 only for the fixed-key substrate. With those fixes, the facts-plus-2-hop-plus-program-pointer result is a real step beyond storing 24 numbers, and the proposal is right to say plainly that it is not procedure acquisition.
