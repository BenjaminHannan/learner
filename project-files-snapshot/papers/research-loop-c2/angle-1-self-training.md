# Angle 1: iterated self-training / expert iteration for program synthesis (literature pass)

Date: 2026-10-07. Papers only: no code run, no GPU, no repo file edited. Nothing below has been run on B2, so labels grade how close the evidence is, not whether it will work for us. Cards are for the small card experiments (B2, C2); village-model claims are not made.

## How to read
- **shown** = program synthesis with an executor, close to ours. **suggested** = other domain (LM reasoning, games, RL, theorem proving), plausible bridge. **untested** = the mechanism differs enough that transfer is a guess.
- Read tags: **[M+R]** = I read the method and results sections of the PDF text (appendices only in part); **[partial]** = some sections; **[abs]** = abstract/intro only, numbers come from there. No paper is "full text, every page".
- All 40 citations were checked against the arXiv API on 2026-10-07 (title, authors, date). Venues are given only where the PDF header shows them. The one 2026 paper (TD-Grokking) is abstract/intro only and unreplicated.
- Scale warning: model sizes run from small purpose-built nets (CrossBeam, PQT, DeepCoder, DreamCoder's recognition nets; sizes not stated in the text I read), LambdaBeam's stated 13M and Lee et al.'s 14M, through 125M-250M (GPT-Neo, CodeT5+, Stream of Search), up to 540B-class LMs. None is a ~3M looped controller. Treat sizes of effect as direction only.

## What the papers agree on (read first)
1. The common loop is sample, keep what a checker accepts, fine-tune, repeat (STaR, ReST-EM, CodeIt, SOAR). It mostly sharpens what the model can already do sometimes. STaR says it "eventually fails to solve any new problems" because failures give no signal; Mind the Gap and Yue say the same without a new information source.
2. Three ways out of ~0%: (a) an external expert or search supplies the targets (ExIt, DreamCoder, Polu, Stream of Search, Searchformer); (b) hindsight relabelling turns every executed program into a correct example of what it actually computes (CodeIt, SOAR, MINIMO, HER); (c) a ladder of easier items or hints (Polu, Lee, STaR rationalization, NuRL).
3. Relabelling is not automatically good. SOAR: uniform hindsight data (32.38) lost to correct-only (34.67); half-best/half-worst (36.46) won. CodeIt: no relabelling 24 vs 49 solved (policy only).
4. The checker is a proxy. Programs that fit the examples but are the wrong rule are the main contamination (STaR at temperature, ReST-EM on rationalization, MAPO, MeRL, PQT). Shortest-program and held-out-example checks are the usual defences.
5. Solution diversity collapses: RFT at 33B had 88.7 correct paths per question but only 2.78 distinct; Mind the Gap pass@k at large k falls each round; SOAR diversity falls on solved tasks. Fixes: dedupe, cap per problem (ReST-EM: 10), keep some bad-but-different examples (SOAR).
6. Rounds: 2-3 big rounds for large LMs on small problem sets (ReST-EM, SoS: 3; Haluptzok: 2; Mind the Gap: saturates in 2-3), up to 100 small rounds for CodeIt. Plateau and regression after round 2 is common (ReST-EM APPS).
7. From base vs continue: STaR, ReST-EM, Polu, SOAR re-train the base model each round on accumulated data (less drift; ReST-EM: same in-domain score, better transfer than continuing). CodeIt and Lee continue training; online ExIt aggregates all data.
8. Mixing ratios seen: CodeIt 10k train + 90k buffer per round; Lee newest round = 50% of batches; HER 4-8 relabelled per real item (more than 8 hurt); DreamCoder 50/50 replay and fantasy; ReST keeps the original data in the mix.

## Bootstrapping a ~0% family: what was actually done
- External expert targets: Polu (difficulty ladder, expert iteration closes top level where sampling stays 0), SoS and Searchformer (traces from classical solvers), DreamCoder (search in wake, net in sleep). Yue 2025 and Mind the Gap are the negative mirror: self-sampling alone is bounded.
- Hindsight relabelling: CodeIt (24 to 49 policy-only solved), SOAR (best with half-best/half-worst), MINIMO, HER. Selection and dedupe matter.
- Curriculum: Polu, Lee (+1 digit per round), STaR (stage-wise gains per length).
- Hints: STaR rationalization (2-digit addition <1% to 32% in one iteration), but ReST-EM reports more right-answer-wrong-reason programs from it, and NuRL found the gold answer itself hurt.
- Partial credit: Ni (prefixes whose intermediate values match), MAPO (buffer), TD-Grokking (abstract only).

## Hypothesis cards (independent; run one at a time against the same fixed baseline night, 3 seeds, pass marks fixed beforehand)
**Step 0, free, do first (reads existing logs, changes nothing).**
(a) Per-rule pass@32: fraction of the 32 samples that fit all 3 examples, per kind and per affine rule. Near 0 on most affine rules means self-sampling cannot bootstrap it (Yue, Mind the Gap): use cards 2, 6, 7, 8. A few percent while greedy is ~2% means a sharpening problem: cards 1, 3, 4, 5 are the ReST-EM regime.
(b) Weights (my arithmetic, assumes the five kinds count equally; unchecked against the code): using midpoints of your ranges, (2+94+13+94+8)/5 is about 42%, matching your 38-44%. With square and last_digit at ~95%, 70% needs the three weak kinds to average ~53%. sq_plus and double_add have 7 rules each: nearest wins. Affine (49 rules) is the hurdle.
(c) Spurious rate (evaluation only, never fed to training): among programs that fit all 3 examples, the share whose output on fresh x differs from the true rule.
(d) Budget (my arithmetic): a night is ~45-65 TFLOP. 5 GPU-hours = 18,000 s, so 100 nights (CodeIt ran 100 rounds) is ~5,500 TFLOP, needing ~0.3 TFLOP/s effective. Check measured throughput first.
(e) Noise: if DEV has 256 questions split evenly, each kind has ~51; SE is up to ~7 points per kind. Use the holdout, 3 seeds, and demand effects of 2x SE.

**Card 1, relabel every sampled program (hindsight).** Change: run every valid sampled program on the 3 example inputs, write a new prompt with the realized outputs, train to emit it. Weight by the share of original examples matched; drop duplicates by output on ~20 probe inputs; drop constants/identity; strip dead steps; keep half best-matching, half worst-matching, <=50 per question. Mechanism: every sample becomes a correct example of the function it computes, so B2 gets signal on building constants and chaining ops from its own attempts, no answers read. Source: CodeIt, SOAR, MINIMO, HER. Expected (untested): positive for sq_plus/double_add; affine uncertain; CodeIt's policy-only solved count doubled with relabelling (24 to 49), SOAR's uniform version lost 2.3 points to correct-only, so selection decides the sign. My guess: single-digit points on weak kinds after 1-3 nights. Cost: no extra sampling; fine-tune grows with examples (cap to +10-20 TFLOP/night, assumes cost linear in examples). Wrong if: after 3 nights the mean of affine, sq_plus, double_add on the holdout is not >= 5 points above filter-only, or any old skill drops > 2 points.

**Card 2, search programs as expert targets, shortest, cross-checked.** Change: for all 1,024 pool questions take the shortest program from the offline symbolic search that fits all 3 examples AND still fits the third when found from the other two; train on (prompt, program) mixed with self-sampled fits and old-skill replay. Mechanism: apprentice imitates an expert that reaches the zero-sample questions; shortest = DreamCoder MAP target; leave-one-out screens spurious programs. Source: ExIt, DreamCoder, Polu, Searchformer, Yue 2025. Expected: largest effect on affine if B2 can imitate 7-step constant-building at all; size depends on how many pool questions the search covers (unknown). Cost: CPU search; no extra sampling; usual fine-tune. Wrong if: after one night affine DEV rises < 5 points while the model's greedy program still fits < 20% of the pool prompts it was trained on (imitation or format limit, not data), or the Step 0(c) spurious rate of search programs exceeds ~30%.

**Card 3, difficulty ladder plus learnability budget.** Change: bucket pool questions by length of their shortest search program (steps to build A and B from 1/2/10/100); unlock the next bucket when training-fit rate of the current one >= 70%; sample ~8 per question for kinds fitting >= 90%, ~64 for 1-50%, ~16 for 0% unless search gives a program. Mechanism: effort goes where the success variance is. Source: Polu, Lee, Foster, ReST-EM (medium and hard gain most). Expected: affine gains sooner per night; saturated kinds stop eating budget; Foster's 3x is RL and unproven here. Cost: same total sampling (reallocated), CPU for bucketing. Wrong if: same number of nights, holdout not >= 5 points above the unordered run, or square/last_digit fall > 2 points.

**Card 4, distinct-solution cap plus canonical targets.** Change: keep <= 4 semantically distinct fitting programs per question (try 1, 4, 10), each the shortest after dead-step removal; equal examples per kind. Source: RFT, ReST-EM (cap 10), DreamCoder, PQT (K=10), SOAR diversity result. Expected: small, protective (within +-3 points) mainly for rare kinds. Cost: ~0 (CPU). Wrong if: DEV change < 1 SE (~3 points) and distinct programs per question do not rise.

**Card 5, retrain from the practised checkpoint on cumulative deduplicated data.** Change: night n fine-tunes the original practised checkpoint (not last night's) on old-skill replay + all deduplicated data from nights 1..n, newest night at 50% of batches, relabelled:real <= 8:1, total examples capped so cost stays flat. Mechanism: avoids compounding errors and drift; accumulated data avoids collapse. Source: STaR, ReST-EM Fig. 7, Polu, SOAR (from base) vs CodeIt, Lee (continue); Gerstgrasser, Shumailov. Expected: neutral over 1-3 nights, better old-skill retention and C2 by night 5+ (guess). Cost: flat with the cap, else grows ~n x. Wrong if: over 5 nights old skills and C2 are no better than continuing from last night.

**Card 6, partial-prefix credit.** Change: for samples that do not fit, compute each step's value vector on the 3 example inputs; if it equals a step of any known fitting program, train on the prefix up to it (no end token). Source: Ni et al. (prefix credit added +3.0 pass@100 on GSM8K over fully-correct-only, small GPT-Neo), MAPO, TD-Grokking (abstract). Expected: raises the rate of correct constants (3 = 2+1, 7 = 10-2-1) in next-night samples, hence affine yield. Cost: CPU matching, +10-20% examples. Wrong if: fraction of next-night samples whose first k steps match a correct prefix does not rise vs control.

**Card 7, dreams from discovered programs.** Change: sample or lightly mutate (change a constant, swap an op) programs the system itself found, run them on fresh random x to make new 3-example prompts, mix 50/50 with replay. Guard: library comes from search/sampling on the pool, not hand-written rule templates, or "never practised" is broken. Source: DreamCoder, CodeIt (without mutated-program data: 59 to 20 cumulative solved), CrossBeam/LambdaBeam random tasks. Expected: coverage of rule variants beyond the pool; risk of drift to artificial rules. Cost: free data; fine-tune grows with examples. Wrong if: C2 holdout does not move while old skills do.

**Card 8, DAgger step: complete the model's own wrong prefixes.** Change: for pool questions where B2's greedy program does not fit, search for a completion of B2's own prefix (else shortest program); train with loss only on the steps after the prefix. Source: DAgger, CrossBeam (on-policy contexts beat off-policy), ExIt. Expected: raises greedy first-try specifically (the scored quantity); size unknown. Cost: CPU search from prefix; usual fine-tune. Wrong if: greedy fit rate on the pool is not >= 3 points above Card 2 alone over 2 seeds.

## Not found / limits
- No paper at ~3M parameters or with a looped controller. LambdaBeam (13M, search policy) and Lee et al. (14M, self-training on arithmetic) are nearest.
- No paper compares hindsight relabelling against an external-search expert on the same task with numbers; the choice between cards 1 and 2 is open.
- Spurious-fit rates are reported qualitatively (STaR, ReST-EM, PQT), not as numbers I could quote.
- TD-Grokking (2026), Foster, Yue, Invisible Leash, TTT and LOLS were read at abstract/intro level only.

## Summary for Ben (high-school senior)
1. The standard trick is "try lots of answers, keep the ones that check out, study them, repeat." It works, but mostly it sharpens things the model already gets right sometimes. For rules where it is right almost never (affine), the papers say it gets stuck.
2. Three ways papers get unstuck: get answers from an outside expert (our symbolic search), relabel each failed try as a correct example of whatever it actually computed, or practise easy versions first and climb.
3. Traps: a program can pass all 3 examples and still be the wrong rule; the model can keep recycling one easy program; and the gains usually fade after 2-3 rounds unless new information comes in.
4. If the five kinds count equally, 70% needs the three weak kinds to average about 53%. sq_plus and double_add (7 rules each) are the nearest wins; affine (49 rules) is the real hurdle.
5. First, free: count, rule by rule, how often any of the 32 tries fits all 3 examples. That tells us which family of fixes can work.
6. Then try one change at a time. My top picks: put the search's shortest programs in as targets (with a hold-out check), relabel every try, and order the practice questions from easy to hard.
