I checked the RESULT files (plain_tf-3M, 4 seeds, 24k updates).
- The most measurable weakness of plain_tf is the answer split on numeric families: state_update 0.5, chain_ops 2.8, story_chain3 9.0, var_chain 10.5, table_calc 13.
- It also fails at copying: copy_word on vocab 30.5, object_track on answer 2.8.
- Because pooled-5 includes the answer and vocab splits, an exact executor or a pointer moves pooled-5 by construction. The answer-only latent loops (RRL, TRM, StreamPonder) have no comparable lever, and the repo evidence on looping is negative (tf2x4; sandwich 8 rounds = 4 rounds).

Attribution:
- The three step-supervised designs (Ledger, Abacus, TWS) gain mainly from steps supervision. Every row has a steps field, but numeric intermediate values exist only for the arithmetic families: 23k rows with 2 or more steps, about 69k with any program.
- Each of these should put a supervision-matched plain_tf (writes steps) in its first screen, not defer it to confirmation. Only TWS does.
- Ledger's executor is a hand-coded tool, so C1' (plain_tf + steps + calculator) is the honest ceiling test.

Ideas worth grafting:
- Abacus's place-tied per-slot register readout, used as Ledger's GEN path for non-program numbers (seq_next, fewshot).
- RRL and TWS's content-free pointer is already in Ledger as the WORD path. Keep its keys content-free.
- Abacus's register-interchange lesion, scored against a counterfactual parsed from steps, is the best causal test proposed. Ledger's opswap lesion is its executor counterpart.

Fairness:
- TWS uses 1.62x the FLOPs and does not screen n_loops=2.
- RRL and Abacus screen against old calibration seeds; a fresh twin costs one run slot.
- TRM-text's per-family counts are slightly off: list_index is 19-23 of 40 and verify_claim 24-31, not 19-21 and 24-26.

No design addresses the held-out families. All predict 0-5%, except a possible unit_convert gain through Ledger's MUL. Beating plain_tf at the same size on new kinds of task is untested by all six designs.

Checked against the files:
- The per-family plain_tf figures that designs quote at 24k match RESULT.json. For example, chain-5 by seed is 36.5/42/36/36, and TWS's chain-4 figures (8.1/40/27.5/47.5) also match.
- The brief's "chain_ops 2-8%, state_update 8-10%" are 8k-update numbers. At 24k they are chain_ops 5-12.5% and state_update 32.5-52.5%. TRM-Text and wildcard correctly flag this.
- The README gotcha says dev-only symbols map to UNK. That is stale: data.py now gives every printable ASCII character its own id (108 ids), and the designs assume this correctly.
- Step-coverage checks on train: chain_ops, chain_story2 and var_chain parse 100% with the result regex. state_update parses 2,792/5,106 and story_chain3 1,944/4,095. TWS and wildcard report this right; RRL does not.

Cross-design grafts:
1. Wildcard's per-round value targets plus register interchange fit almost unchanged onto TRM-Text's answer slots across supervision steps. The result has a conv reader, a linear talker and causal proof the loop transforms state. That is probably the best fit for Ben.
2. RRL's content-free pointer (keys are position only) is the right copy mechanism for every linear-talker design. It also suits Ben's "act on things never seen" Minecraft goal.
3. Ledger's opswap and noexec lesions show what proof of the core looks like. An exact executor is better held back as a later tool arm, after the latent designs are measured.
4. Every transformer-reader design (Ledger, wildcard) should swap to a local or context-free reader. Otherwise the reader can do the binding, and Ben said he would rather not have one.
5. Every screen needs a fresh paired plain_tf twin. RRL and wildcard compare against the old calibration seeds, which CALIBRATION.md forbids for verdicts.
6. Designs using steps supervision must run the same-information plain_tf control in the screen, not later.

None of the six addresses held-out families or few-example learning. All expect 0-5% there and say so. Ben should hear plainly that this north-star goal needs task diversity or episodic training, not a new architecture.

Harness facts every design must handle (checked in the code):
