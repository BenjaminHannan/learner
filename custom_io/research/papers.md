# Papers for custom readers and talkers (Sonnet search, 2026-10-05)

Four Sonnet searchers (looped reasoners, custom readers, custom talkers, fair evaluation), then Sonnet verifiers fetched every arXiv abstract. Rows marked FAILED CHECK had a wrong id, a different title, or a claim the abstract contradicts; do not rely on them.

## Synthesis: looped-reasoners

1. Published from-scratch winners use almost no reader or talker. HRM, TRM, URM and the 13.6M model of 2609.39967 embed cells, recurse a small block, and read answers with a per-position linear head in one parallel pass. The question x is re-fed every step (TRM, Recall, DEQ, Abacus), and the carried state includes the current answer y. In the best studies the hierarchy is not needed (ARC Prize analysis, Ge et al. 2510.00355, TRM, 2609.39967); what matters is outer-loop deep supervision, full backprop through a recursion, and state stability. Smaller wins under scarce data (2 layers beat 4).

2. What generalises to new compositions, with evidence: weight sharing across depth (2604.07822: vanilla fails systematic composition, recurrent succeeds, via grokking); recurrence beats unshared depth at equal FLOPs (URM: 40.0 vs 23.75 on ARC-1); equal-size dense controls collapse OOD (7% vs 71%, 2609.39967); keeping the input visible and training so the loop can run on indefinitely (Recall). Answer-only supervision extrapolated better in 2603.21676, but hints helped in CLRS. Which applies to Premonition is untested.

3. Caution on 'new task kinds'. HRM, TRM and VARC reach new tasks through a learned per-task ID or token plus augmentation and test-time training. Blank ID gives 0% (2512.11847); training only on the eval tasks keeps 31% of 41% (ARC Prize). None is zero-shot. That is the project's 'mode signal' finding in published form. Closest from-scratch alternatives: infer a task latent from demos and search it (LPN: 7.75% to 15.5% OOD) and in-context looped learners (2311.12424). Both are shown only on grids or numeric function classes.

4. Reader quality is the bottleneck on text. In 2603.21676 the unstructured-text case was the weak one (60% OOD vs 95-100% structured). Abacus found digit-position marking was the real blocker. A non-looped 18M ViT with a canvas encoding (VARC, 54.5%) beats the looped models on ARC. So the lesion check needs an equal-size non-looped control.

5. Looping adds manipulation, not storage (Ouro: ~2 bits/parameter either way; Saunshi). The language-scale from-scratch successes (Huginn 3.5B, Ouro, Parcae) used 0.1-7.7T tokens, so a small-data, nothing-pretrained design is only supported on closed, synthetic domains.

No paper found shows natural-language question answering at tens of millions of parameters from scratch beating larger models, apart from bAbI-era Universal Transformers.

## Synthesis: custom-readers

1. The closest published match to our sandwich is Latent Recurrent Thoughts (2609.01117): a 4.2M from-scratch reader plus a 7M TRM-style loop feeding a frozen 8B LM. It reports what Ben saw. The decoder is load-bearing, the answer is not in the latent (probe 57.5% vs 52.5% baseline), and from-scratch full-stack solvers beat it on symbolic tasks. Suggested: the talker must be custom, not only the reader.

2. What works with nothing pretrained is tiny I/O around a looped core that is re-fed the raw input every loop. TRM (embedding in, linear head out, 5-7M), Huginn (prelude/core/coda with input injection), Abacus (input injection + tied loops was best), recall (0% vs 97% at 512 bits), MAC (control re-attends to the words each step), MemN2N hops and Perceiver (latents re-read the input) all do this. Our current design reads the question once through the LM. That is the structural difference.

3. Readers need not be transformers or pretrained. Examples: position-encoded bag of words (MemN2N, EntNet), embedding + biLSTM (MAC, GloVe did not help), GRU (FiLM), char-CNN (best numeracy encoder, even untrained beats BERT), and byte/char hourglass encoders (CANINE, Perceiver IO, BLT). Compressing recurrent readers lose exact content (Repeat After Me). LSTM/sLSTM readers track parity and modular arithmetic themselves (xLSTM table), so they could steal the reasoning and need the lesion check.

4. Numbers: put structure in the encoding. Abacus and position coupling align digits; FoNE gives exact two-dimension-per-digit codes (38M scratch model beats fine-tuned Llama-1B at 6 digits); a raw scalar is weak (1909.07940) and xVal reports R-squared, not exact match. Output LSB-first (2307.03381).

5. Entities: key-value slot memories (EntNet, all 20 bAbI at 10k), indirection (ESBN: controller never sees content, >=95% with 95 of 100 entities held out), Slot Attention (vision only), symbol equivariance (SE-RRM). Systematic generalization needs correct hard-wired structure, and learned layouts often fail (1811.12889).

6. Talker (suggested, untested): TRM-style answer state with per-position heads, or Perceiver IO output queries. A linear head cannot reason.

7. Gap: I found no paper that runs a from-scratch non-transformer reader plus looped reasoner end to end on short natural-language word/number problems. Evidence is composed from CLEVR, bAbI, puzzles and arithmetic. DNC has no arXiv id (Nature 2016) and was not cited.

## Synthesis: custom-talkers

1. The collapse analogy is exact, and the literature says what the latent carries. Chen et al. (1611.02731) state it: the latent holds only what the decoder cannot model locally. Evidence (shown, in VAEs): Bowman's plain text VAE has KL near 0 and just matches the language model; Yang et al. get KL 13.3 down to 0.7 as decoder context grows; He et al. find even a repaired text VAE carries about 3 nats. Our frozen 1.2B LM, which also sees question words and pointers, is the powerful decoder, so a core that carries only a per-family mode is the expected outcome, not a bug.

2. Every fix works on the decoder's access, not the encoder. Word dropout (KL 0.01 to 20.9 nats), shrinking the receptive field (Yang, Chen), gist-style attention masks (2304.08467), a 4-token fusion channel that costs nothing (2107.00135), and a target-rate constraint (Alemi). Suggested first experiment: a talker-starvation ladder (drop or mask question words and pointers) with core-swap sensitivity plotted against it, as Yang et al. did against decoder context. Expect a cost in raw likelihood, and untested whether a frozen LM can still write answers.

3. Frozen LM plus trained prefix behaves the same way. Frozen (2106.13884) was within a few points of its blind control and its from-scratch version transferred 0.0. The nearest published system (LRT, 2609.01117) says "the answer is not sitting in the latent" and has not measured the split. Nobody in this family has reported a swap-the-latent lesion, so ours would be a real contribution.

4. Make ignoring impossible by construction. Perceiver IO output queries, SONAR/LCM decoders and pointer heads see only the latent or the input items. They read out; they do not reason. Fragility to off-manifold vectors (LCM) and exact-copy failures (gist) are the costs.

5. Move computation out of any LM: executed outputs. Neural Programmer, NS-VQA (270 programs, 99.8%), NeSS (100% SCAN length vs 13.8%) and PAL (GSM-hard 61.2 vs 23.1) show executors give scale-invariance and clean attribution. NeSS warns that an op able to write the whole answer recreates the talker. Toolformer shows tool use is learned only above about 775M parameters, so a tiny model needs supervised or hard-wired calls. Numbers spoken digit by digit work only in range (2307.03381).

6. Only TRM (2510.04871) shows a from-scratch tiny reasoner beating LLMs (Sudoku 87.4%, direct prediction 0.0%), but with grid input/output and per-task training. Free-text multi-kind reading and answering is untested.

## Synthesis: fair-eval

1. Measured levers for small from-scratch models (shown): (a) position codes that match the task (relative positions: Ontanon average 0.137 to 0.346 over 12 tasks; Devil SCAN length 0.30 to 1.00; Abacus digit-index 20-digit training to 120-digit addition); (b) sharing weights or looping with input injection (Ontanon +0.065; Abacus 99.1% with about 10x fewer parameters; Loop-Think: plain R=1 gets zero systematic generalisation while R=2 gets some, and iso-FLOP deeper plain stacks still fail); (c) copy/pointer output heads and structured output formats (copy +0.057 average; COGS tagging 0.28 to 0.64-0.78); (d) data format and diversity (reversed output, scratchpads, length-diverse training, task count). Gains are task-dependent and sharing is not uniformly good. An OOD validation set is essential, because in-distribution accuracy was about 100% for every variant.

2. Published tiny reasoners get their power from the loop plus deep supervision, not from hierarchy (TRM ablation; ARC Prize analysis of HRM, a blog not arXiv). They also read and write the grid themselves. Nothing shown yet uses a language-shaped reader and talker around a small core. Closest templates: prelude/core/coda (2502.05171, one jointly trained model) and the latent-program network (2411.08706: the decoder sees only input plus a 256-d latent, so the latent must carry the task; OOD 0% in-context versus 88% with latent search).

3. New task kinds from in-prompt examples need many task kinds and episodic training. Below a diversity threshold the model only identifies the task (matches the project's per-family mode signal): Kirsch, Raventos (threshold 2^14-2^15 tasks at 8 layers, 128-d), Lake (0.03% to 99.95% with episodic meta-training). Untested at 9M with text.

4. Lesion design: run both noising (necessity) and denoising (sufficiency); resample real other-question activations, not zero or Gaussian noise; report logit difference; include a perturb-everything positive control (COCONUT: 100%) and a same-compute placebo (filler tokens); expect self-repair through other talker inputs (Hydra) and leakage through soft prefix vectors (69% vs 50% chance). Interchange-intervention accuracy against a known algorithm (IIT) is the cleanest test and can also be a training loss.

5. Fair baselines: match parameters, FLOPs and wall-clock separately and report all three; test at two sizes (Tay); give baselines the same OOD-validation tuning (Devil; NDR tuned baselines differently). Non-arXiv notes: Lake and Baroni, Nature 2023 (MLC) and the ARC Prize HRM analysis were not verifiable on arXiv. Some WebFetch PDF summaries gave wrong numbers; the figures above were taken from full-text extraction.
