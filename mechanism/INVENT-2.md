## Ranking
- **74** Doubt Forks: force the runner-up at the model's confident decisions, and learn which confidences to doubt: HEADLINE. It is the only candidate aimed straight at the one bottleneck we have measured: on the puzzle model, search finds answers that greedy first-try never reproduces. Forked one-token-diff DPO pairs put the training signal on the exact token where greedy went wrong. It also does not depend on how many checks a task has. That matters because the only Reach-style logs I could read (/mnt/project-files/rlaif/kaggle-run1/runs/eval_base/eval_base.jsonl, 100 tasks, model not confirmed as K2) have a median of 2 checks per task, 27% of tasks with a single check, and many failures that are early 'done' claims or quitting after an error after only 2 turns. Those are exactly the confident decision points a turn-start fork can flip. The test is cheap: a CPU/Mac gate needing no GPU, then roughly 3.5 GPU-hours on the 5070 Ti for 2 seeds. Novelty is only partial (divergence-point DPO exists in 2606.23112), so the claim rests on forcing the logprob runner-up at confident tokens. The DF-ent and DF-loc controls test that claim directly.
- **66** Impasse Forge: break the route that worked, on purpose, so the creative mode practises getting unstuck where a way out is certified: BACKUP. Most ambitious answer to the zero-signal problem: it manufactures solvable stuck states from our own wins every night, with ground-truth checks and no narration loophole. It fits real use better than most (error_recovery-type quitting shows up in the logs). It depends on the HARD failure mix being 'route-blocked', which a $2 triage measures before any GPU time. It costs more than Doubt Forks and is close to Fission-GRPO, so novelty rests on P3 (beating random-block training).
- **58** Check-Lexicase Stepping-Stone Archive (Go-Explore over partial wins): The most open-ended idea, and on paper the purest 'new kind of search'. But the readable logs suggest Reach tasks carry 1-2 checks, rarely 3. With 2 checks there are at most 3 non-zero check vectors, and with 1 check there is just 1. Lexicase then reduces to best-first, CROSS has almost no complementary pairs, and the advisor's own Stage-0 gate (>=3 distinct non-zero vectors on >=50% of HARD tasks) will probably fail. The useful part, GREEDY-RETURN (continue from the replayed state of the best partial try), should be kept as a cheap add-on arm later. On this test bed it should not be the headline.
- **56** Verified Ladder: build checkable easier siblings of the stuck job in its own world, climb them, then sleep on them: Sound curriculum logic, and SHRINK is a genuinely new operator. Its code operators also need several droppable checks, though, and RELAX is impossible on single-check tasks. The advisor expects the gain over R+check to be 0-2. Its weight stage is the same composition bet that failed on the puzzle model. Novelty is lowest among the strong ideas (LADDER, ARQ).
- **54** Set-Credit Reframer: a learned creative LoRA that writes the reframe that adds a solve the rest of the set would miss: High ambition (a trained exploration policy) and good Mac fit. But the per-task leave-one-out credit zeroes correct, different ideas (shown by arithmetic), and on 0/32 tasks training pairs will be scarce. It must first pass a Stage 0 test showing that untrained reframes crack at least 10% of tasks. PKPO narrows the novelty.
- **51** Prepared Mind: an open-questions board where each new win goes looking for old failures: Cheap, and it fits the Mac well. But the first test as designed only measures forward retrieval (ExpeL/ReasoningBank with Jaccard), not the new reverse direction. The likely gain comes from good generic rules (the KP arm), not from matching. Worth keeping as a card-pipeline improvement, not as the creative-mode headline.
- **48** Night Lab: overnight read-only experiments that turn failures into verified facts about the tools: Best real-use story for Ben's persistent Mac environment, and very cheap. But the failures in the readable logs are about policy and diligence (claiming done, quitting, ask/confirm), not about misunderstanding tools. Each mock world is task-local, so the cross-task library is probably empty on Reach-1, and the R+P scouting arm will likely absorb any gain. Run LAB-0 (the free audit) as a side check, and keep the real-Mac pilot.
- **40** Latent Escape: learned fail-to-win activation directions as branches, then an overnight checker-guided search in their span: Ambitious, but two of its three failure modes are close to certain. On the same task, wins and fails share an identical prompt, so the direction encodes text the model already wrote (BORROW done in activation space). Greedy fitness is flat, so ES is about the same as random search. It is mined from mixed tasks, not from 0/64 ones. Lowest expected gain.

## Plain summary for Ben

Ben, here's the short version. We had eight ideas for the 'creative mode', the part of the assistant that switches on when it's stuck. I ranked them, and the winner is called Doubt Forks.

The idea: when the assistant fails, look back through its failed attempt for the spots where it was 'pretty sure, but not totally sure' about a choice. Examples are deciding the job was done, giving up after an error, or picking which tool to use. At each of those spots we make it take its second choice and see if that rescues the task. Then, overnight, we train on the exact spot where the second choice won, so next time it gets it right on the first try. Getting it right first time is the thing our earlier experiments kept failing at. Search would find an answer once, and the normal first try would never repeat it.

Why this one: it goes after that known weak point directly. It's cheap, and it runs on your Mac. It also fits what the logs show: many of the failures are the model saying 'done!' too early, or quitting after one error.

How we keep it honest:
- First, a free check with no GPU: are there enough of these 'pretty sure' spots in its failures, and does the sandbox replay exactly?
- Then a fair race, run twice. It goes against ordinary retries that see the failures, and against three look-alike versions: forcing the second choice where the model was unsure, restarting from the same spot without forcing anything, and forcing at random decision points. Every version gets the same number of tries and tokens.
- If Doubt Forks doesn't beat all of them by the margins we wrote down in advance, the idea is wrong, and we say so.
- Only if it wins do we try the overnight training. We test that on tasks it never trained on, so it can't just memorise answers.

The whole first test fits in about 6-7 hours on your PC plus the second Kaggle account. No paid compute.

The backup is Impasse Forge. It deliberately breaks things that already worked so the assistant can practise finding a way around, and it only runs if a cheap $2 check shows your hard tasks really fail that way.

I dropped the 'archive of partial wins' idea from the top spot for one reason: each task only has about 2 checks, so there isn't enough partial-credit information for it to work with.

## Pick 1: Doubt Forks (DF-1'): force the runner-up at confident decision tokens, then turn the fork wins into first-try habits with fork-DPO

### Mechanism
WHAT IT DOES. When the assistant is stuck, it reads its own failed attempts token by token and finds the decision points where it was nearly sure (p1 >= 0.9) but a second option still had real weight (p2 >= 0.005). At each such point it takes the second option instead and lets the attempt continue from there. Overnight, every fork that rescued a task becomes a preference pair that shares the prefix up to the fork: chosen = the winning continuation from the fork, rejected = the original failed continuation from the same point. These pairs train the nightly LoRA, so the next greedy first try makes the right call at that point.
STEP BY STEP (stuck mode, K2-3.7B only):
(1) Input: the 2 failed transcripts R+ already sees.
(2) One teacher-forced prefill per transcript with top-20 logprobs (vLLM prompt_logprobs=20; on the Mac, mlx_lm logits from a single forward pass). Record p1, p2 and the entropy at every assistant token.
(3) Decision-token typing from the chat-template spans: TURN-START (the first token of an assistant turn: tool call vs final answer vs clarifying question; this covers the 'claims done after 2 turns' and 'gives up after error' failures seen in the logs), TOOL-NAME (first token of the tool name), ARG-VALUE (first token of each argument value).
(4) Candidate = decision token with p1 >= 0.9 and p2 >= 0.005.
(5) ALIAS FILTER (advisor fix): greedy-complete the span after the forced token and keep the fork only if the decoded tool name, argument value or turn type differs semantically from the original. A leading space, quote, casing or same-tool prefix counts as an alias and is dropped.
(6) Rank by log p2 plus a per-type weight. The weights are fixed in advance: TURN-START 1.0, TOOL-NAME 0.7, ARG-VALUE 0.5.
(7) Pick the top 16 forks, at most 3 per 50-token window. If fewer than 16 qualify, do not fill the shortfall with lower-quality forks: the unused budget returns to the token pool and is reported as supply shortfall.
(8) Rebuild the world by replaying the prefix's tool calls into a fresh sandbox, with a hash check of every tool output against the original. Build the prompt as transcript prefix + forced token, then sample at temp 0.7 to the 6-turn cap. vLLM prefix caching makes this cheap, and no KV truncation code is needed.
(9) Score with the task's own checks. All checks must pass to count as a success. Replay each win 3 times from the same fork.
NIGHT: fork-DPO, LoRA rank 16, beta 0.1, with the existing KL anchor and normal replay mix. It trains only on pairs from HARD-train and LOW (1-4/64) tasks. A win also becomes a playbook-card candidate ('in jobs like X, don't assume Y, do Z'), shown to Ben for confirmation. The learned probe (v1) is deferred until there are at least 30 positive fork outcomes, and it must then beat the heuristic by >=0.05 held-out AUC before it is used. One model throughout, with no second model.

### Why fantastic
Ben asked for truly new ways of thinking. A model cannot name an assumption it is blind to, which is why FLIP's verbal 'opposites' collapse onto ask-vs-act. Doubt Forks reads those assumptions from the model's own probabilities instead: a near-certain choice with a live alternative is mechanically a premise it never examined. That makes 'think differently' a search over its own commitments, which re-shares probability mass the model already has but judged not good enough, close to what Ben described with FlowRL/GFlowNet. More importantly, it is the only candidate that also tackles the measured bottleneck. On the puzzle model, search finds answers that greedy first-try never reproduces. A fork pair differs at exactly the token where greedy went wrong, so the DPO gradient lands on that decision and nowhere else. That is the most direct route we have from 'search found it once' to 'it gets it right first time'. It also matches the failures in the readable Reach-style logs, many of which are confident turn-start mistakes: claiming the job is done after 2 turns, or saying 'the file does not exist, let me know' and stopping. It needs no extra checks, so it works on 1- and 2-check tasks where archive and ladder ideas lack enough bits. And it compounds: every night adds fork outcomes, and the probe eventually learns which confidences are worth doubting.

### Why new
Not FLIP: there is no listing prompt, and the alternatives come from logprobs. Not R+ or a temperature change: specific runner-up tokens are forced at chosen points. Not the high-entropy 'forking token' work (2506.01939, Qwen 80/20): that targets uncertain tokens, while this targets confident ones, and DF-ent tests the difference head-to-head. Not PivoARL (2607.03702): there the pivot turn is chosen by self-reflection. Narrowed honestly: divergence-point DPO for tool agents already exists (2606.23112), but its pairs come from naturally diverging rollouts. Here the alternative is forced by logprob at a confident decision token and certified by the checks. 2604.22271 argues confidence alone may not localise errors, and this is the hypothesis DF-ent and DF-loc test. The citation 2412.07961 is dropped as unverified. Known-ids: 2506.01939, 2607.03702 and 2606.23112 are not in known-ids.txt and should be added. No combination of confident-token forcing + exact sandbox replay + fork-DPO on a 3-4B tool agent against matched controls was found (7 searches; suggested, not proven).

## Pick 2: Impasse Forge (backup): manufacture certified stuck states from our own wins and train the creative mode to detour

### Mechanism
Each night, take sandbox tasks the plain model solves (>=1/8). Find the load-bearing step by ablation: the first tool call whose removal makes a replay fail. Block it with one of 3 typed blocks: (a) the tool returns an error, (b) the target item is moved or renamed, (c) the argument form used is rejected and another form is needed. The 'ask' block is dropped because the sandbox has no simulated user. Error wording varies, and 20% of blocks are decoys: temporary errors where retrying the same call is correct, so 'never repeat' cannot become a reflex. Certify each impasse: a detour passes the same end-state checks, found either by the base model within 16 R+ tries (early stop) or by one Claude Batches trajectory. Claude detours only certify and are never trained on. Keep the impasse only if the plain worker fails it >=7/8. The creative adapter then writes 8 approach lines per impasse, with 1 worker try each. DPO pairs: chosen = an approach whose episode passed; rejected = an approach in the same set that re-hit the wall, defined as the same failing tool and argument signature called 2 or more times after an error. LoRA rank 16, beta 0.1, KL anchor. At serve time it is an ordinary creative call.

### Why fantastic
It turns the scarcest thing, examples of getting unstuck, into something the system makes every night from its own wins, with ground truth. A 0/64 task gives zero signal, and this sidesteps that without hindsight relabelling (the goal stays fixed and only the route is broken). If it transfers, the assistant gets better at recovering from new failures without Ben having to fail first. The readable logs include exactly this kind of failure, for example 'the file does not exist, let me know' after 2 turns on error_recovery tasks.

### Why new
Fission-GRPO (2601.15625) trains recovery from errors made by an error simulator. Forge instead blocks the step found by ablation, certifies that a detour exists, retires and stacks blocks as a curriculum, and trains a separate exploration mode with 're-hit the wall' negatives. ToolMaze (2606.05806) only benchmarks injected failures. AgentHER, ECHO and HSL change the goal, not the route. Novelty rests on beating Fission-style random-block training (P3).

### Check-Lexicase Stepping-Stone Archive (Go-Explore over partial wins)
Keep every failed try that passed a different subset of checks as an elite in a persistent per-task archive. Pick parents by lexicase selection over checks, then either return to a parent's exact sandbox state and continue, or cross two parents whose partial wins complement each other. A 0/64 task gets cracked by recombining specialists, not by resampling from scratch.

- Mechanism: STORED: one JSON archive per task (about 2-20 KB). Cell key = (bitvector of end-state checks passed, sorted set of tool names used, asked-vs-acted flag). Each cell holds its best elite, ranked by checks passed and then by fewer turns: the transcript, the tool-call list, and a 40-token 'what this try was going for' note written by the model. A failed try that passes 3 of 5 checks gets its own cell. R+ throws exactly those tries away.
GENERATIONS (nights; also usable live): gen 0 = 8 plain tries seed the archive. Gens 1-3 each make 8 children. (1) Lexicase parent selection: shuffle the task's checks, keep the elites that pass check 1, then of those the ones that pass check 2, and so on, and pick at random among the survivors. A specialist that alone passes a rare check is picked often; an all-round mediocre try is not. (2) Two operators, half the children each. RETURN: deterministically replay the parent's tool-call prefix in the sandbox up to the step where its last newly-passing check became true, then let the model continue from that real world state, with the still-failing checks named (check name or docstring). CROSS: take two parents whose check vectors are complementary (their union passes more checks than either one), show both transcripts and their pass/fail lists, and ask for one attempt that keeps A's achievements and adds B's. (3) Score each child with the same checks. It is inserted if it opens a new cell or beats a cell's elite. The task is cracked when any child passes all checks. The success gate stays all-checks, so partial credit only shapes which tries are kept, never what counts as a success. That avoids the reward-shaping that caused say-instead-of-do.
ACROSS NIGHTS: the archive persists, so a task Ben hit on Monday starts Tuesday from its stepping stones. A crack becomes a playbook-card candidate: its note plus the parent lineage. MODEL PARTS: no weight change. K2 plays proposer, continuer and crosser through prompts. The existing card/LoRA morning step is the only training.
REAL-USE ANALOG of the per-check bits: tool-error types, Ben's 'that part is right but X is wrong', and the nightly stuck-low task checks.
- Why fantastic: Every creative arm so far (R+, FLIP, BORROW, dream school) restarts from scratch and keeps only full successes. In that regime a 0/64 task stays at 0. Open-ended evolution works because it keeps stepping stones that are different but not yet good. Lexicase selection is known in genetic programming to protect specialists better than average-fitness selection. Go-Explore's return-then-explore is what cracked hard-exploration games, and a deterministic sandbox makes the return exact and free. Partial check credit turns a flat 0/1 into a map of niches. The creative mode becomes a memory of how close it got and in which directions, and that memory compounds night over night. This is a different kind of search, not a better resample.

### Verified Ladder: build checkable easier siblings of the stuck job in its own world, climb them, then sleep on them
When a task is stuck, make a small tree of nearby easier jobs that still have real checks. Some are made by code (drop a check, shrink the world, keep only the first sub-goal), others by the model (same goal on another item, undo it, a read-only probe, each with a harness-run self-check). Keep only siblings the model solves sometimes but not always, show the solved ones to the real job as same-world stepping stones, learn which kinds of change pay off, and at night train on the verified sibling wins so the ladder moves back toward the original and greedy first-try improves.

- Mechanism: STORED: a ladder tree per stuck task. Each node holds the variant task, its check set or edited world, the operator used, its distance from the original in steps, its solve rate at 8 tries, and its best solving transcript. Plus a Beta-Bernoulli credit table over (operator × failure signature), where the failure signature is one of 5 rule-based classes read from the failed transcripts: repeated tool error / asked when it should act / acted when it should ask / wrong entity / stopped early.
OPERATORS. Code-only, so the checker is always real: RELAX (drop one end-state check from the gate), SHRINK (remove distractor files, records or tools from the world; the same checks apply), SPLIT (keep only the checks for the first named sub-goal). Model-written, each with a self-check made of read-only tool calls with expected values that the harness runs; the model never grades itself in words: SWAP (same goal on another entity in this world), INVERT (undo or reach the opposite state), PROBE (answer a read-only question the job depends on), DISAMBIGUATE (one clarifying sentence chosen from what the failed tries got wrong).
STAGE A, DAY OR NIGHT SEARCH: (1) Operators are chosen by Thompson sampling from the credit table, and K=4-6 children are made. (2) MINIMAL CRITERION: run 8 tries per child and keep it only if 1-6 of 8 pass (learnable, not trivial). Prune duplicates by solving tool-sequence. (3) CLIMB: T gets 8 tries with the R+ block plus up to 2 solved sibling transcripts, labelled 'A smaller version of this job, solved in this same world'. The tool results come from T's own world, which fixes BORROW's cross-world copying. (4) DOWNSTREAM CREDIT: an operator earns 1 only if T is cracked in a try that saw its stone. Solving the sibling itself earns nothing.
STAGE B, NIGHT WEIGHTS: a rank-16 LoRA with the existing KL anchor, trained on solved-sibling trajectories (each on its own variant request, never relabelled) plus climb wins plus a replay mix of normal-task wins. The next night mutates from the new frontier, one step closer to the original. Optional: DPO for the morph-writer, with chosen = a morph whose stone led to a crack and rejected = a morph that was solved but did not help (useful vs merely easy reframing).
REAL USE: for a stuck real task, the assistant builds a shrunken sandbox copy of the folder overnight, one sub-goal at a time. The verified path ('first do A, then the rest') becomes a playbook card. One model throughout; the mutators are code.
- Why fantastic: On a 0/64 task every training signal is zero by definition, which is why single-try RL, hindsight and dream school had nothing to grab. This makes real positive examples one checked step away from the exact failure, keeps them only where they carry signal (minimal criterion), and lets the frontier move each night, so the task set and the model evolve together (POET / minimal-criterion coevolution). It is Polya's 'solve a related easier problem' as a checkable, learned loop, with a credit table that learns which changes of the problem pay off for which kind of stuckness. 'More sleep nights' is the only thing that reliably helped on the puzzle model, and this aims those nights at the frontier instead of at random dreams. It also attacks the greedy bottleneck: wins found by search go into the weights, and the test measures greedy first-try on the original.

### Doubt Forks: force the runner-up at the model's confident decisions, and learn which confidences to doubt
Find the decision tokens in a failed attempt where the model was nearly certain (above 0.9) but a second option still had real weight. Force that second option, continue in the replayed sandbox, learn with a small probe which kinds of confident point are worth doubting, and train on one-token-diff preference pairs so the find becomes the greedy first try.

- Mechanism: DAY (stuck mode): start from the 2 failed transcripts R+ sees. (1) One teacher-forced prefill pass per transcript records the top-2 probabilities and the layer-L hidden state (L about 2/3 depth) at each assistant token. (2) Candidate forks are decision tokens only: the first token of each assistant turn (act or ask), the first token of a tool name, and the first token of each argument value. A candidate is kept if p1 ≥ 0.9 and p2 ≥ 0.005. The hypothesis (untested) is that confident but not certain choices are where unspoken assumptions sit, the reverse of the usual high-entropy forking-token view. (3) Ranking. v0 is a heuristic: log p2 plus a per-type weight. v1 is a learned logistic probe on the hidden state (about 3k parameters) that predicts P(the forked continuation succeeds). (4) Choose the top 16 forks, at most 3 per 50-token window. Rebuild the world by replaying the prefix's tool calls (the sandbox is deterministic), truncate the KV cache at the fork, force the runner-up token or tool, then sample at temp 0.7 under the 6-turn cap. (5) Wins are replayed 3× from the same fork and logged with fork type, position and the alternative token.
NIGHT: (a) Refit the probe on all fork outcomes so far, so the system learns which doubts pay. (b) Fork-DPO: chosen = the winning continuation from the fork, rejected = the original failed continuation from the same point. Because they share a prefix, the preference signal sits exactly on the forked decision. LoRA, beta 0.1, with the nightly KL anchor. (c) Each win becomes a card ('in jobs like X, don't assume Y, do Z') built from the token diff plus a one-line model summary, used only after Ben confirms it. PARTS: frozen backbone, a linear probe, the nightly LoRA. No second model.
- Why fantastic: A model mostly cannot name the assumption it is blind to, which is why FLIP's 'opposites' collapse onto ask-vs-act. Doubt Forks reads those assumptions from the model's own probabilities. A near-certain choice with a live alternative is, mechanically, a premise it never examined. That turns 'think differently' into a search the model can carry out over its own commitments. It is the candidate most directly aimed at the measured greedy bottleneck (on the puzzle model, search finds fits for about 36 of 205 pool questions by night 4 while greedy first-try stays near 0). The training pairs differ at the exact token where greedy went wrong, so a search find can become a first-try habit. The probe gives the creative mode a skill that grows each night instead of a fixed prompt.
- Prior art: partly-new. Prior-art check for Doubt Forks. Verdict: partly-new. ### Set-Credit Reframer: a learned creative LoRA that writes the reframe that adds a solve the rest of the set would miss
Train a small 'reframer' LoRA on the same 3.7B to write 8 short problem reframes for a stuck task. Each reframe is credited by how many solves it adds that the other 7 do not cover (leave-one-out marginal pass@set), with the worker frozen. Reframes from Claude and the 35B enter as off-policy candidates scored by the worker's real checks, never copied. Exploration is trained to complement, not to be likely or merely rare.

- Mechanism: WHAT IS TRAINED: only a creative LoRA (rank 16, attention+MLP) on K2-3.7B, switched in on the same weights. Input: task request + 2 failed transcripts (the R+ block). It runs the episode with one reframe added through the existing extra-block path (to confirm in the Reach-1 code, which is not in this checkout).
SCORING (night, sandbox): for a stuck training task, set S = 8 reframes, with 2 worker runs each (16 episodes, the R+×16 budget). p_i = reframe i's success fraction. V(S) = 1 − Π(1 − p_i). This is the step past FlowRL, which matches each sample to its reward independently and so keeps many copies of one good mode.

### Latent Escape: learned fail-to-win activation directions as branches, then an overnight checker-guided search in their span
Each night, mine the hidden-state directions that separated the model's own winning tries from its failed tries on the same tasks, and orthogonalise them into a small escape basis. When stuck, run each branch near-greedy under a different direction, so branches differ in where the model thinks from rather than in sampling noise. For tasks still stuck overnight, run an evolution-strategies search over combinations of those directions, scored by partial checks and decoded greedily so any find replays exactly.

- Mechanism: STORED: a basis E of 16 unit vectors at one middle layer L of K2-3.7B (L chosen once on train tasks from about 40/50/60% depth), each with a strength alpha_k, a win count, and an optional strategy label. About 200 KB. Per-task finds: a 16-number coefficient vector c, about 100 bytes.
NIGHT BUILD (forward passes only): (1) Take mixed tasks, some tries winning and some failing (screen 1-7/8, and in deployment day logs). (2) Re-run their transcripts through the current weights (after that night's LoRA) and record h_L averaged over the turn-1 assistant tokens (plan plus first tool call). (3) delta_t = mean(h|win) − mean(h|fail), minus the across-task mean, so that 'success in general' is not one shared direction. (4) k-means (k=16) on the normalised deltas, then Gram-Schmidt. (5) alpha = the largest scale at which ≥90% of steered turns still emit valid tool-call JSON on 20 easy tasks. Optional, report-only: Claude Batches labels each cluster's winning transcripts with a strategy name (under $2), so a direction becomes a readable card ('inspect state before acting'). Rebuild after every nightly LoRA.
DAY (stuck mode, MENU): with the same R+ block and no extra prompt tokens, branch i adds alpha·||h_L||·e_i at layer L on every generated token of turn 1 (all turns if train tasks favour it), decoding at temp 0.3. Use the top 8 directions by win count, 2 tries each.
NIGHT SEARCH (for tasks still stuck, from Latent Incubation): antithetic ES over c ∈ R^16 with the steering vector Σ c_k e_k: population 8, 4 generations, sigma tuned on train tasks, every candidate decoded GREEDILY. ### Prepared Mind: an open-questions board where each new win goes looking for old failures

### Night Lab: overnight read-only experiments that turn failures into verified facts about the tools
At night the creative mode does not retry a stuck task. It runs a scientist's loop: list what about the tools or world might differ from what the attempts assumed, design one tiny discriminating probe per guess in a throwaway sandbox copy, and keep the raw observed outputs as a growing, cross-task library of verified tool facts. Later tries get facts, not advice, and recurring facts go into the weights as a world model of its own tools.

- Mechanism: STORED: a fact library, one row per fact: (tool, normalised argument shape, situation) → observed output quoted verbatim from a real sandbox call, plus a one-line model gloss marked UNVERIFIED and a count of tasks it was relevant to. Examples: 'search_orders(name="Smith") returns [] but name="smith" returns 2 rows'; 'edit_file on a missing path silently creates a file and reports ok'.

(2) PROBES: each runs in a fresh copy of the task's world, 1-3 turns, and never runs the task's checks (so it cannot game them). The world is thrown away afterwards.
