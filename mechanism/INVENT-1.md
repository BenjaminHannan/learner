## Ranking
- **7** Ask once, then remember (stage 1 only: ask/act preference sleep; twin removed): It goes after the weak spot Ben would notice most (guessing wrong, or asking twice) and has the best real-use score of the set (7). It is fairly new: nightly ask-vs-act training from one user's own corrections, tied into the fact sleep. It costs nothing at serve time on either Mac and fits under 6 T4-h if the world is kept small. The advisor's fixes turn it into a clean single-change test: an A+ arm with slot memory and a prompt rule, B against B-sft, a realistic ask mix, and grading by slot ID. Stage 2 (the Ben-twin) is dropped.
- **6.2** Recall-Then-Read, stage 1 only (slept LoRA as diary search-key writer): Cheapest real test of the two-hop weak spot. It is inference only and reuses the ab_ob_b4_nb LoRAs, at about 2-3 T4-h. The serve side is close to GenRead/HyDE/Query2doc, so only the 'weights hold hop 1' case is new. The fixed test isolates that case with condition W and fair two-round retrieval and alias-table arms. Stage 2 (cue-sleep) waits until stage 1 passes, and should cite Ma et al. 2305.14283 and DeepRetrieval 2503.00223.
- **6** Error-and-provenance gate: High real-use value: it stops sends to the wrong Sam and deletes of the wrong file. Step 1 (provenance plus a self-consistency gate) is cheap and needs no training. Novelty is low, though: argument provenance is in 2607.01236, PACT and PACE, and tool-error probes are in 2608.27750. The only new part, the nightly per-user refit, likely gains about 0 at real label volumes. It also overlaps pick 1 on ask-vs-act. Worth building as plain engineering, but it is not a research pick.
- **5.4** Tripwire lines: The novelty is real (the model fires its own trap cue, trained on fire-on-success and no-fire examples). But the advisor's fair arms (C0+, R, P, T-gen) and clustered statistics push the cost to about 20 T4-h and $25-35. The expected gain over C0+ or the prompt list is 0-4 pp, which 40 tasks cannot detect. It should wait until the skill-sleep thread reports.
- **5.2** Tomorrow-first sleep: Good Mac fit, and Stage 0 is a free go/no-go on real logs. But a recency-plus-weakness ranking probably explains most of the gain. Ben's traffic is mostly coding and school work with little calendar trace. A today-card in the prompt probably captures what is left. The full Stage 1 is about 45 T4-h. Run Stage 0 only, whenever 10+ days of real logs exist.
- **4.9** Pointer-ledger folding: ARC (2607.25066) already covers the append-only ledger and expand(id), and the zero-training arms (masking, a regex ledger) likely capture most of the gain. The 3.7B full-context teacher may rarely be right, and the 8 GB M2 night cap of 8k context breaks the 'full-context' teacher. A better first move is to adopt the zero-training ledger as engineering.
- **4.6** Alias-to-Entity State Tying (ASTie): The mechanism is interesting, but real use is weak (3). The serve-time alias line and deterministic alias substitution are near-free rivals that also handle changed links. The pre-gate as written cannot pass because it runs on the base model. With all fixes it costs about 15 T4-h. It only wins in a cross-day, text-deleted case that may not exist in Ben's setup.
- **4.4** Sleep-grown macro tokens: The decode-step saving is mostly already available from grammar jump-forward or harness templating, with no vocabulary risk. The end-to-end gain at 4 agents is about 0-8%. It also risks wrong-argument tool calls. Its CPU-only Stage 0 can be run for free as a sanity number, but it is not a pick.

## Plain summary for Ben

Ben, here is the short version. Eight ideas were checked, and two are worth testing first.

Pick 1, "Ask once, then remember": each night the assistant looks at today's mistakes. One kind is when it guessed and you had to correct it. The other is when it asked you something it already knew. It then practises asking in the first kind of situation and acting in the second. The fair rival is a version that just keeps a small note of your past answers and is told "ask if something is missing". That rival gets exactly the same amount of night training, just on other examples. The test asks one question: does the night practice make it better at spotting brand-new ambiguities, like a "Sam" it has never met, without pestering you on clear requests? If the note-only version does just as well, we keep the note and skip the training. It costs about 5 hours of free Kaggle GPU and about $5 of Claude, and it is only run in a fake test world for now. If you have a week of real assistant logs, we first check that you actually make enough corrections for this to learn from.

Pick 2, "Recall-then-read": for questions like "what's my sister's dentist called?", the trained memory writes a few search words ("sister = Maya") and the untrained model reads what the diary search finds. It must beat two simpler tricks: searching twice, and a ready-made "who is who" list. It matters most when the original diary entry is gone and only the trained memory still knows the link. It needs no new training, about 2-4 GPU-hours.

The other six were set aside, either because a much cheaper trick probably does the same job or because they cost too much to test fairly right now. Every pass mark is fixed in advance, and each plan says which result would show the idea is wrong.

## Pick 1: Ask once, then remember: ask/act preference sleep (stage 1)

### Mechanism
NIGHT, inside the existing KL-anchored sleep LoRA (ab_ob_b4_nb, r16, attention+MLP, same anchor on 100 general prompts). (1) TAG the day's diary with a rule pass plus one Claude Batches pass. A REGRET turn: the assistant committed a slot value (recipient, date, file, amount, which meeting) without asking, and within 3 turns Ben corrected it or the action was undone or re-sent. An ANNOYANCE turn: the assistant asked about a slot whose answer was already in context or in the diary (BM25 hit on the answer string), or Ben said 'obviously' or 'just do it'. (2) BUILD PAIRS. Regret gives a DPO pair: chosen = a one-line question naming the slot with the 2 most likely values (written by Claude from the correction), rejected = the guessed action. Annoyance gives a DPO pair: chosen = the direct correct action, rejected = the question. (3) Ben's answer or correction becomes a fact sentence ('Ben's "Mom" = ann.h@...') for the names re-read and fact-dream pipeline. This step is shared by all arms and is not the change under test. (4) These pairs take a fixed 15% slice of the night's compute. In the fair baseline, the same slice goes to extra fact and corrected-action SFT. SERVE, identical in every trained arm: a BM25 slot memory over past question/answer and correction diary entries (top 3, about 150 tokens) plus a one-line rule: 'if a required detail is missing and not in memory, ask one short question naming it'. No serve-time cost is added on top of the baseline. Irreversible actions are not treated specially here; that belongs to the separate provenance-gate work. The Ben-twin (stage 2) is removed.

### Why new
Prior ask/clarify training uses simulated or verifiable rewards online, at a fixed time: Abstain-R1 2604.17073, CIGAsk 2609.24290, the future-turn DPO of 2410.13788 (already in known-ids), and ClarifyBench 2511.08798 (which is uncertainty-weighted GRPO; the candidate wrongly called it SAGE-Agent/EVPI). PAHF 2602.16173 asks before acting and updates memory from corrections, but in-context, not nightly in weights. AdaMem 2606.21144 learns memory from corrections, with no ask-vs-act policy. Ask-or-Assume 2603.26233 is a scaffold. New here: slot-level ask/act preference labels mined from one real user's corrections and redundant questions, trained nightly inside the KL-anchored sleep LoRA, and tested against a strong memory-plus-prompt baseline. The question is whether learning WHEN to ask about unseen ambiguities adds anything beyond 'remember the answer'. The project's tried and queued lists contain no ask-training item (prior-art check, notes only). This is a lead from 8 searches, not proof. Fetch the full text of PAHF and AdaMem before claiming novelty in any write-up.

STEP 0 (free, no GPU). If 7 or more days of Ben's real gateway diary exist, run the tagger on them and have Ben hand-check 100 tagged turns (about 20 min with a yes/no sheet). Gate: at least 5 regret+annoyance events per night on average, and tagger precision of at least 0.8. If real logs exist and either gate fails, stop: nothing is trained and the candidate is dropped for real use. If fewer than 7 days of real logs exist, the simulated test below may run, but any adoption decision waits for this gate. That is stated in the result.

TASK MATCHING REAL USE. A mock email/calendar/files assistant with deterministic Python tools (send_email, draft_email, create_event, move_event, open_file, share_file). The Claude-built persona 'sim-Ben' has 60 slot facts: ambiguous nicknames with several contacts, which 'meeting', default meeting length, which file is 'the essay', send-vs-draft rules. It also has 40 neutral background facts. The world runs 5 training days x 40 requests, about 50% underspecified given what the model knows at that point. The 8 changing preferences are dropped (they belong to a later multi-week run).

DATA SOURCE (Claude Batches). Sonnet writes the persona and request stream. Haiku 5.5 is the user sim, run in 2 Batches rounds per day: round 1 answers the agent's questions; round 2 writes corrections. A correction is triggered DETERMINISTICALLY when the committed slot value differs from the persona's value, and Claude only phrases it. Day logs are made once by base K2 (fp16, served with the prompt rule, T=0.7, fixed seed) and are SHARED by all arms. This offline design means each arm learns from identical days. It is a stated limitation, since on-policy days come later. Training labels come from the REAL tagger run on these logs, not from oracle labels. Tagger precision and recall against the deterministic oracle are reported.

EVAL (days 6-7, all slots and phrasings unseen). Split H, 200 items: 80 knowable (slot answered or corrected on days 1-5, so the right move is to act with the correct value), 80 new ambiguity (a slot never seen in training, so the right move is to ask about it), 40 fully specified (act). Split R, 200 items at a realistic mix: 15% new ambiguity, 35% knowable, 50% fully specified. Silent-wrong, 20 items: the model guessed earlier and sim-Ben never corrected it. Also 100 general non-assistant prompts to measure the general ask rate. GRADING: an ASK is right only if it names the correct persona slot ID, which a Haiku grader maps to one of the 60+N slot IDs (exact-match check; grader agreement measured on 60 items against a Sonnet regrade, kappa reported). An ACT is right only on an exact tool-argument match (JSON-normalised, NFKC). The Claude grader settles free-text ties only.

ARMS. All trained arms use the same base, LoRA config, KL anchor, 5 nights, optimizer steps, and compute measured in token-passes: a trained token counts 3 units (forward+backward), a reference-only forward token counts 1 unit. They also use the same number of Claude Batches calls per night (B's calls write the chosen questions; A+'s calls write the same number of fact and SFT paraphrases), and the same serve stack. All night compute is logged and asserted equal within 5%.
- A+ (fair equal-compute baseline): ab_ob_b4_nb + answers-as-facts + corrected-action SFT. The 15% slice is extra fact/SFT top-up from the same corrections. Serve: slot memory + prompt rule.
- B (the one change): A+ with the 15% slice replaced by regret/annoyance DPO (beta 0.1, reference = that night's starting adapter).
- B-sft: the same 15% slice as SFT on the chosen sides only.
- P (reference, untrained): base K2 + slot memory + prompt rule.
SEEDS: 2 (seeds 1 and 2 change LoRA init, data order and dream sampling; the day logs stay fixed).

METRICS. Right-move rate per subset and split; ask rate on fully specified, knowable and general prompts; silent-wrong accuracy; one-hop fact recall on the 100 slot+background facts; ARC-Easy (fixed 500-item subset); the trajectory of DPO reward margin and KL per night (to watch for the collapse described in 2608.00301).

PASS MARKS (fixed in advance; paired McNemar on the 160 pooled paired items of 2 seeds x 80, exact two-sided):
1. Split H new ambiguity: B >= A+ + 8 points, p < 0.05 pooled, and the sign is positive in BOTH seeds.
2. Split R overall: B >= A+ + 3 points, and B's ask rate on fully specified items is at most A+'s + 3 points.
3. Knowable: B >= A+ - 2 points.
4. ARC within 1 point and fact recall within 2 points of A+; general-prompt ask rate at most A+'s + 3 points.
5. B >= B-sft + 3 on new ambiguity. If B-sft also meets marks 1-4, adopt B-sft instead (cheaper and more stable).
All five must hold for B to be adopted. P is reported as a reference row only.

RESULT THAT PROVES IT WRONG (any one): B <= A+ + 3 on new ambiguity; B's Split R change below 0 (over-asking eats the gain); B more than 5 below A+ on knowable items. Diagnostic, not a pass mark: if A+ matches B everywhere, the useful part is the slot memory, so ship the memory and the rule without ask-training. If tagger precision on the sim is below 0.8, the result is flagged as label-noise-limited, and a follow-up on oracle labels is allowed only as a diagnosis.

COMPUTE PLAN (Kaggle T4x2, fp16; one arm-seed job per GPU so the two run in parallel). Day-log collection with base K2 on vLLM: about 0.4 GPU-h. Timing pilot: night 1 of A+ seed 1. If one night takes more than 9 min, cut dream and re-read counts EQUALLY in every arm until it fits. Training: 3 trained arms x 2 seeds x 5 nights x about 8 min = about 4.0 GPU-h. Eval: 4 arms x 2 seeds x (420 decisions + 100 general + recall + ARC) with short decodes = about 0.6 GPU-h. TOTAL about 5.0 GPU-h, hard cap 6.0. The PC 5070 Ti is an optional backup for one seed. API via Batches: persona and requests about $1.5; user sim 10 rounds about $1.5; tagging, pair writing and paraphrases about $1; grading about $1. TOTAL about $5.

## Pick 2: Recall-Then-Read, stage 1 (slept LoRA writes diary search keys; base reads)

### Mechanism
SERVE only, no new training. The trigger is a memory question whose plain FTS5 hit on the raw question is weak; in the test, every two-hop item. (1) KEY PASS, LoRA ON: a fixed 1-shot prompt asks the slept K2 for up to 4 bridge names and short facts in at most 40 tokens, greedy ('my sister -> Maya; Maya -> dentist Dr. Lee'). (2) RETRIEVE: BM25/FTS5 over the day-by-day diary, with queries {question} plus each key, merged by reciprocal-rank fusion and trimmed to the same 1.5k k_tokens budget as the plain-diary arm. (3) READ PASS, LoRA OFF (base reader): answer from the retrieved turns. The main arm uses all keys (tau = -inf); a logprob gate is only an ablation. The idea under test: the weights act as a memory of WHICH names to search for (hop 1), while values are read from the diary. That matters most when hop 1 is no longer findable by search (condition W). Stage 2 (nightly cue-sleep on a hit@3 reward) is out of scope until stage 1 passes. If it is ever run, it must cite query-rewriter training on retrieval reward (Ma et al. 2305.14283, DeepRetrieval 2503.00223).

### Why new
Producing retrieval text from parametric knowledge is prior work: GenRead 2209.10063, Recitation 2210.01296, HyDE 2212.10496, Query2doc 2303.07678, Generate-then-Ground 2406.14891, and IRCoT 2212.10509 and Self-Ask 2210.03350 for iterative retrieval. These must be named as the main baselines, and the description of 2604.04651 must be corrected. 'Adapter on for keys, off for reading' is a deployment choice, not a new mechanism. What is untested and project-specific is whether a NIGHTLY PERSONAL sleep LoRA, which already recalls 91% of names, gives better diary keys than a second retrieval round or a cheap alias table. That matters most in the case where hop 1 survives only in weights. The project's router (memory if p>=0.5 else diary, a bad result at 57-60) picked one source instead of chaining them. This test chains them, against fair rivals.

## All candidates (with attacks)

### Alias-to-Entity State Tying (ASTie)
At night, train the LoRA so the mid-layer hidden state at the end of a description ('my sister', 'the guy who fixes the boiler') matches the hidden state at the entity's own name ('Maya'). Facts stored under the name then fire from the description, so two-hop joins should work for pairs that were never dreamed together.

### Error-and-provenance gate (nightly per-user error probe plus argument-source check before risky calls)
Before an irreversible tool call, the gateway checks where each key argument came from (request, context, deterministic resolver, diary, or nowhere), and a tiny linear probe on K2's hidden state, retrained each night on Ben's real corrections, scores how likely the call is one of the model's usual mistakes. If the gate fires, the agent redrafts the call or asks a one-tap question built from its own runner-up value.

- Mechanism: SERVE (gateway plus a custom mlx-lm step; no policy change): tools are tagged reversible or irreversible (send_email, delete_file, create/accept event, payments, git push). (1) PROVENANCE: for each argument of a call (recipients, ids, dates, paths; bodies and subjects are exempt), label it grounded (exact or NFKC-fuzzy match in context), resolved (deterministic resolver: relative date -> absolute, nickname -> address via contacts), diary (BM25 hit), or orphan. (2) PROBE: at the call's closing token, read the residual vector from 2-3 middle layers (about 12 KB per call; already computed during decoding) and compute p = probe(h), about 3k multiply-adds per layer. (3) GATE: an orphan argument on an irreversible call, or p above a cost-scaled threshold, fires the gate. Then fork from the cached KV prefix (plain-attention K2 is trimmable), sample 2 more candidate calls at T=0.7, and score each by the probe at its own closing token. If the best candidate is below tau and fully grounded, run it. Otherwise (or if the call is irreversible and any argument is orphan), ask: 'Send to A (x@..) or B (y@..)?', where A is the emitted value, B is the runner-up from re-scoring the argument span over top-k first-token alternatives, plus 'something else'. Reversible calls with no orphan are never gated. ### Sleep-grown macro tokens (per-user vocabulary that grows each night)
Each night, the most repeated multi-token spans in Ben's agent turns (tool-call JSON skeletons, file paths, his sign-off, calendar field names) become new single tokens trained inside the sleep LoRA, so one decode step can emit 5-20 real tokens, and unlike speculative decoding the gain does not shrink when 4-8 agents share a batch.

- Mechanism: NIGHT (after the normal ab_ob_b4_nb data is built): (1) Mine the day's diary JSONL, assistant turns only, for spans of 4-40 base tokens, ranked by count x (len-1) = decode steps saved. Drop spans containing a digit run, ID or email address seen fewer than 3 times. Append-only macro table capped at 1024 entries with fixed IDs, at most 128 new per night; a macro unused for 14 days is retired (row frozen and masked). (2) Add each macro as a row in K2's untied embedding and lm_head: embedding = mean of its parts; lm_head = the first part's row minus log(len), so the macro starts unlikely. (3) Re-tokenise all of tonight's training text (dreams, re-reads, skill-sleep data, KL-anchor prompts) with greedy longest-match on input and target. New rows train at 10x the LoRA LR alongside the usual LoRA. The KL anchor is computed over the base vocabulary by summing each macro's probability into its first base token. DAY: mlx_lm.server loads base q4 + LoRA + about 1024 extra fp16 embedding/head rows; the detokeniser expands macros. Prompts are macro-tokenised too, so tool blocks and repeated tool results get shorter (less prefill and KV per agent). The prompt cache is invalidated after each night.

### Tomorrow-first sleep (calendar-forecast dream selection)
Each night, rank which facts and two-hop pairs to dream by how likely Ben is to need them in the next 48 hours, from his own calendar, unanswered email and open tasks, so the fixed night budget goes to what tomorrow will ask instead of a random or coverage-ordered sample.

- Mechanism: STORED: nothing new at serve time; same diary and LoRA. NIGHT, before the ab_ob_b4_nb dream stage: (1) Build a 'tomorrow context' T from local data only: calendar events in the next 48 h (title, attendees, location, notes), email threads whose last message is to Ben with no reply, open todos and unfinished agent tasks from today's log, and a decayed copy of the last 3 days' recurring topics. (2) Take the candidate pool the recipe already builds (tagged one-hop facts and two-hop bridge pairs). (3) Score each candidate by BM25 against T plus a bonus when an entity is an attendee or sender in T; a two-hop pair is scored by its weaker hop. (4) Reallocate the fixed budget (4x work at s=4): 60% of dream and re-read steps go to top candidates ranked by score x (1 - current closed-book recall probability from a cheap probe), 40% to the normal replay mix so old facts are not starved. The names re-read pass is limited to names in T or the top candidates. Same LoRA targets and KL anchor; only item selection changes. Optional later extra, not tested here: a 300-token 'today card' of top forecast facts in the cached system prefix.
