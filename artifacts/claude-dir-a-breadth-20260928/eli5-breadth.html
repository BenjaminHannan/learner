One test explained · practice breadth

Practise on ten things, not two

Today the reasoner practises only two kinds of puzzle before it meets a new one. Does practising many kinds help it learn a new kind from very few examples?

The idea

A driver who has only used two roads

A person learns to drive in about 40 hours because they bring skills from bikes and games. A net that has only seen two kinds of puzzle may have little to bring.

What we know today

Two kinds of practice already help on mazes

Score is the average right out of 100 across eight example budgets, on mazes it never practised (seed 0, shown).

But that is one new kind, after only two practice kinds.

The one change

Same practice time, spread over about ten kinds

Nothing else moves: same net, same total steps. The kinds are made by code and built as general puzzles, not maze-shaped ones.

The exam

Three kinds it never practised

Mazes, graph-hop (how many links away is each thing) and digit-rank (how many digits are smaller). It sees 1 to 16,384 examples, then is graded on fresh puzzles.

mazesgraph-hopdigit-rank
Who it races

Against the old practice and a plain net

The ten-kind loop is compared with today's two-kind loop, and with a same-size plain net given the same ten kinds. A fresh net is the floor.

How we stay honest

Marks are written before anything runs

Pass marks, and the result that would prove the idea wrong, are committed first. Two seeds, and a blind recount. nothing has run yet

What happens next

I write the test; the Director queues it

I build the kinds and marks now, then hand the GPU job to the Director. I do not rent anything.
