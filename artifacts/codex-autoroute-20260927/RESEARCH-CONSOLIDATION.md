# Consolidation and replay feedback for a later experiment

Research note, 2026-09-27. This proposes **no change to AR1**, its sealed marks, software, or runs. It concerns the next *single* intervention, if AR1 shows enough old-skill forgetting to justify one. The intended system remains one label-free dense reasoner at inference, with the same 1,646,750 model parameters, 6,500 optimizer steps, and 400 replay batches unless a later preregistration explicitly says otherwise. A learned training-time controller is extra learning machinery even if it adds zero inference parameters; its state, compute, and memory must be counted.

## What the primary work supports

| Work | Relevant finding | Limit here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Complementary learning systems, McClelland et al. (1995)](https://web.stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/McCMcNaughtonOReilly95.pdf) | Fast storage and slower interleaved reinstatement offer a biological *analogy* for rehearsal during new learning. | It does not prescribe an artificial controller, a replay quota, or which weights to update in these puzzles. |
| [Maximally Interfered Retrieval, Aljundi et al. (2019)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/15825aee15eb335cc13f9b559f166ee8-Paper.pdf) | Scores old candidates by loss increase under a virtual incoming update and replays those most exposed to interference. | A virtual update plus before/after candidate evaluation adds substantial compute; its short-horizon loss proxy may miss later exact-puzzle damage. |
| [Adaptive Memory Replay, Smith et al. (2024)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/ELVM/papers/Smith_Adaptive_Memory_Replay_for_Continual_Learning_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf) | Uses forgetting feedback and a nonstationary bandit to choose past-data groups when past data are abundant but training compute is limited; it replaces some new examples with replay to hold processed examples constant. | Group-level selection is closer to our code-generated practice supply than a fixed memory buffer, but its vision/language pretraining results do not establish a benefit on exact grid/sum/maze solving. Selection evaluations still need an explicit budget. |
| [La-MAML, Gupta et al. (2020)](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/85b9a5ac91cd629bd3afe396ec07270a-Paper.pdf) | A replay-informed meta-objective modulates per-parameter learning rates: aligned old/new gradients can increase plasticity; interfering gradients can reduce it. This is a direct precedent for learning *where to update*. | Its inner/outer updates, hypergradients, and learning-rate state are extra optimization machinery; copying its algorithm would change more than AR1's replay schedule and likely break an undisclosed compute cap. The other protection agent covers controller architecture. |
| [Does Continual Learning Equally Forget All Parameters?, Zhao et al. (2023)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zhao23n.html) | Reports that forgetting is uneven across modules and studies periodic replay fine-tuning of sensitive modules. | Module vulnerability is empirical and architecture-dependent. A hard-coded list of protected blocks from that study would be unjustified here. |
| [Replay can provably increase forgetting, Mahdaviyeh et al. (2026)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v330/mahdaviyeh26a.html) | Gives settings where replay quantity and selected examples make forgetting non-monotonic, including harmful replay. | The main analysis is linear regression; it is a reason to measure harm, not a prediction of harm in this model. |

## A usable feedback signal for a future controller

The user's desired reward is **old skill retained *after* a stretch of new-skill learning**, with a constraint against stopping new learning. A controller that merely chooses high-loss old examples optimizes immediate fit, not that delayed objective. A future preregistration could define decision windows of `K` ordinary optimizer steps. At window start and end, score *fresh code-generated practice-probe* streams for each already mastered kind and the current kind. Record exact whole-puzzle accuracy at the model's own stop as the interpretable outcome; a bounded answer-plus-stop loss could be a smoother *training* signal. The sealed 600-item final panels and diagnostic panels must never provide actions, rewards, stopping decisions, thresholds, or hyperparameter choices.

One explicit window reward is

`R_t = mean_old[A_old(theta_(t+K)) - A_old(theta_t)] - lambda * max(0, delta_new - (A_new(theta_(t+K)) - A_new(theta_t)))`.

Here `A` is accuracy on independent generated practice probes, `delta_new` is a preregistered minimum new-skill gain, and `lambda` is a preregistered penalty. For a phase-end objective, also compare each old skill with its *pre-new-skill mastered* practice-probe score so the controller cannot call a flat but already-forgotten skill “protected.” This is a proposal, not a reported result. It explicitly rewards delayed retention, penalizes freezing, and needs no inference task ID: kind is known only to the training generator and offline probe checker. To prevent probe memorization, rotate disjoint generated probes, hash visible inputs, reject any overlap with training and sealed panels, and report a separate untouched final grade. A probe repeatedly consulted by the controller is training feedback, not independent validation.

For a controller that acts on update gates rather than replay content, the **same reward definition** can evaluate its chosen training action; this note does not choose the gate architecture. Log the action before the `K` updates, then assign its reward only after those updates and post-window probe scores. The old-skill term should be compared both locally (`t` to `t+K`, useful for feedback) and with the mastered phase-entry anchor (needed to detect accumulated forgetting). If exact accuracy is too coarse over a small probe, use masked answer/stop loss as a prespecified *shaping* signal, while keeping exact own-stop puzzle accuracy as the primary observed reward and the sealed-panel outcome. Avoid giving the controller a reward from its own training batch: it can lower that loss by practicing a narrow set without retaining the skill distribution.

One observed action/reward pair cannot prove that the action caused the score change: model state, current-kind difficulty, and the step schedule change at the same time. A simple training-time bandit can learn from noisy delayed feedback with prespecified exploration, but a causal verdict still needs paired independent trajectories against a fixed-policy baseline. Save action probabilities, chosen strata, probe fingerprints, pre/post scores, and the eventual phase-end score so credit assignment can be audited. Do not perform unbudgeted fork-and-restore counterfactual updates merely to estimate an action advantage; those would consume additional model updates or full-model evaluations and must be counted as such. This is the key distinction between a learned retention objective and an after-the-fact heuristic that reads the final test.

The clean single-change candidate is **a training-time learned choice of replay examples or groups within the already fixed replay slots**, with feedback from these old/new reward windows. A small bandit over prespecified generator strata (for example kind and size) needs only value/count state, not a second inference model; its action picks practice content inside a scheduled slot. It can use a few inexpensive old/new probe summaries to learn which selections protect later performance. If the next test instead makes the controller gate parameter groups or learning rates, that is a *different* single intervention; hold replay selection fixed and use the same reward. Combining learned replay selection, learned update masks, changed replay timing, and extra losses in one comparison would prevent attribution. The bandit example is motivated by [Smith et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/ELVM/papers/Smith_Adaptive_Memory_Replay_for_Continual_Learning_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf); delayed old/new protection reward and puzzle strata are our proposed adaptation, not a claim from that paper.

Conflict-aware alternatives have different costs. [MIR](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/15825aee15eb335cc13f9b559f166ee8-Paper.pdf) would select old examples whose loss rises after a hypothetical new update, but the virtual update, candidate scoring, and optimizer-state handling are expensive for this recurrent model. Choosing by *current* old loss needs only candidate forward passes but risks oversampling chronically hard or unrepresentative puzzles rather than preventable forgetting. A controller should log selection frequencies, unique puzzle/size coverage, and post-window old/new gains; it should keep a preregistered exploration floor so one easy or difficult stratum cannot monopolize 400 slots. Gradient alignment is a useful diagnostic from [La-MAML](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/85b9a5ac91cd629bd3afe396ec07270a-Paper.pdf), but computing separate old/new gradients across 1.65M parameters is not free and immediate alignment is not the requested delayed outcome.

## Cost accounting and falsifiers

AR1's 6,500 steps at batch 64 process **416,000 training examples**, of which 400 replay batches are **25,600 examples**. Keeping those counts does **not** by itself keep training compute constant. For any controller, record `N_probe` separate forward requests, recurrent rounds per request, extra backward/virtual updates, controller state bytes, wall minutes, and peak memory alongside the 6,500/400 counts. If probes are taken both before and after each decision window, with `P` total puzzles per window and `D` decisions, the added evaluation work is `2PD` whole-request inference calls. For illustration only, `P=64`, `D=13` already means **1,664** extra requests, potentially up to 48 recurrent rounds each; this cannot be called zero-cost. It must be measured against the baseline's actual forward/backward work and any hard 10% overhead limit before an experiment is registered. A full MIR virtual update at every replay slot adds up to 400 additional candidate-update events *plus* scoring; it is unlikely to fit a tight overhead limit without a cheaper approximation. Do not claim that counting only optimizer steps proves equal compute.

A future preregistration should state a hard action/reward frequency and overhead cap, then compare on paired seeds and independent sealed panels. It should be considered wrong if old final accuracy fails to improve despite higher practice-probe reward, if new sums or mazes miss their learning floor, if a win disappears at matched *wall/compute* cost, or if controller actions collapse to a small stratum. More subtle failure: loss on probed old items improves while own-stop exact correctness deteriorates, or grid retention rises only because new maze exposure was displaced. Report these separately instead of relabeling them as consolidation. None of the cited work demonstrates “brain-like” durable procedural skill in this exact label-free model; the biological connection is an analogy and the next causal claim must come from a registered local comparison.
