## Research question and comparator

Can a **learned sleep-time plasticity controller** help one shared-weight, label-free reasoner protect grids after learning sums and mazes, while still learning the latter, compared with an ordinary **same-size local dense model**? The brain connection is a design analogy about selective plasticity, not a neuroscience claim. [ANML](https://doi.org/10.3233/FAIA200193) demonstrated learned selective activation/plasticity in a different continual-learning system; [differentiable plasticity](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/miconi18a.html) and [Meta-Experience Replay](https://research.ibm.com/publications/learning-to-learn-without-forgetting-by-maximizing-transfer-and-minimizing-interference) support learning plasticity or interference behavior. None establishes that this small controller, sparse rewards or puzzle sequence will work. [GEM](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2017/file/f87522788a2be2d171666752f97ddebb-Paper.pdf), [A-GEM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.00420), [EWC](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611835114) and [SI](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/zenke17a.html) motivate computed alternatives; they are **not** evidence for a learned controller. Published facts stop there; everything below is an implementable hypothesis.

The **paired baseline** is the AR1 label-free `AutoNet` design with four visible-context vectors, ordinary AdamW, the original e4 replay schedule, 1,646,750 trainable parameters and fresh local seeds/panels. The **candidate** is one coherent intervention: a learned controller replaces one of those context vectors' parameter budget and scales the *actual proposed updates* in the same dense reasoner during B/C new-kind practice. This is a fair total-size comparison, but **not** a pure optimizer ablation: four versus three context vectors can itself affect capacity or context inference. Report that trade explicitly. Both arms get the same 2,500 A + 2,500 B + 1,500 C actual optimizer steps, batch 64, code-generated source distributions, and **400 earlier-kind replay batches**: B 250 grids; C 75 grids + 75 sums. No teacher, expert copy, per-skill parameter snapshot, inference router, extra old batch, or external data. Use fresh paired seeds and mixed panels declared in new pushed marks *only after AR1 is complete and reviewed*; historical e4's oracle-`env` 470.17 and AR1's ongoing scores cannot serve as the paired label-free control.

## Policy, delayed reward, and exact budget

For each region `i`, form a 16-number **training-only** feature vector at the beginning of a new-kind block. A concrete indexing is: `x[0]=1`; `x[1]=log1p(RMS(g_new,i))`; `x[2]=log1p(RMS(d_AdamW,i))`; `x[3]=log1p(RMS(g_grid,i))`; `x[4]=cos(g_new,i,g_grid,i)`; `x[5]=log1p(RMS(g_sum,i))`; `x[6]=cos(g_new,i,g_sum,i)`; `x[7:9]=min(reference_age/100,1)` for grid and sum; `x[9]=previous_gate_i`; `x[10:13]=tanh(EMA_loss)` for grid, sum and current new kind; `x[13:16]=tanh(latest_EMA_loss_change)` in that order. RMS is root mean square over the region's elements, and cosine uses a fixed numerical epsilon. Zero reference vector and age one represent an unseen old kind; its cosine is defined as zero. The exact epsilon and loss-EMA decay must be fixed in pushed marks before tests; no learned preprocessing or kind-to-region assignment is added. Training generator provenance distinguishes old reference gradients but never supplies an inference label. At B start, the already-computed final A gradient seeds the grid reference; at C start, the last B sums gradient seeds the sum reference. Old references are at most two float32 parameter-sized temporary optimizer buffers, ~12.56 MiB combined, not model copies.

Let `mu_i = sigmoid(W_i · x_i)`. Initialize `W[i,0]` (feature #1, the constant) to `logit(0.95)≈2.9444`, all other entries to zero, so training starts near ordinary dense updates. Sample a continuous `a_i∈(0,1)` from a Beta distribution with mean `mu_i` and fixed concentration 20; hold all 16 gates over the nine new-kind steps between replay slots. This is one policy action per block, so a reward after several updates assigns credit to that block's gates. At each new-kind step, compute the ordinary clipped-loss AdamW moment transition and full proposed parameter displacement `d` **including momentum and decoupled weight decay**, then apply `a_i*d` to each tensor in region `i`. Replay steps remain ordinary full AdamW updates. Scaling the raw gradient instead would let Adam normalization, old momentum or decoupled decay defeat the intended gate. Keep the moment-state behavior fixed and report it. Forced **all-zero gates must leave every reasoner weight bitwise unchanged, including decay**; forced **all-one gates must reproduce the ordinary AdamW weight trajectory on the same three-context candidate architecture** with identical batches/seed. These are mandatory future software tests before capability scoring, along with region coverage and `W` gradient tests.

At the scheduled replay step after a gated block, use that ordinary old batch's **pre-update** answer CE + 0.5 stop BCE as `L_old_after`; it was held out from the intervening new-kind block. Use the next ordinary new batch's pre-update loss as `L_new_after`. Compare them with fixed, non-trainable rolling means from previous practice batches of the same source, captured before the block. A possible fixed reward is `R = (EMA_new_before − L_new_after) − max(0, L_old_after − EMA_old_before)`, coefficient one. A running scalar reward baseline reduces variance; update `W` by REINFORCE using `R × sum(log density of the block's 16 Beta gates)` on the next already-scheduled optimizer step. No extra optimizer step or second-order unroll is assumed. The new term and exact S_B/maze/T gates penalize freezing; the old term rewards protection **after** new learning. This is a stochastic *loss proxy*, not exact old-skill accuracy or a same-puzzle loss delta: differing generated puzzles make it noisy. At C, the 75 grid and 75 sum replay slots provide sparse, alternating feedback. There are fewer than 400 usable delayed rewards, so a 256-parameter policy may fail to learn at all.

**Counting rule:** each arm still uses 6,500 real optimizer steps and exactly 400 old replay-batch forwards/backwards on 400 scheduled batches. Reward reuses the pre-update losses from those same scheduled batches and the next ordinary new batch: **zero extra old-example forward passes** and zero extra old samples. Count controller sampling, feature reductions and policy backward separately in runtime/peak memory; the candidate is not equal-compute merely because update counts match. A more reliable before/after probe on the *same* old puzzle would require at least one additional old forward per probed block and would exceed 400±10% if used at all 400 slots. This draft does **not** silently assume that larger budget. If exact own-stop reward or multi-step meta-unrolling proves necessary, that is a different, unapproved experiment with explicit extra passes/steps and new marks.

## Review addendum: Bernoulli gates and boundary timing

**Conclusion:** Bernoulli region gates are a **more credible bounded *software prototype*** than Beta gates: `a_i∈{0,1}` makes `a_i*d_i` an exact full-update-or-no-weight-update action, has a simpler log-probability, and directly exercises the required all-zero/all-one AdamW semantics. This is **not** stronger evidence that the policy will learn or beat plain dense. With initial `p_i=0.95` and independent gates, each region is off on only about 5% of windows: roughly **12 B and 7 C off-actions per region** across 249+149 credited windows at the initial policy. Sixteen simultaneous decisions, noisy old/new loss baselines, and just **398** delayed rewards make attribution weak; the controller could stay near always-on, overprotect old skills, or learn only a global effective learning-rate reduction. A Beta gate gives denser variation but makes every update fractional. Neither has a measured advantage here. If a Bernoulli version is ever chosen, it **replaces** Beta in a separately pushed protocol; it is not an AR1 change or an in-run option.

The proposed one-optimizer-step boundary sequence is coherent if written precisely. At phase B/C start sample the first action for new steps 1–9. On each scheduled replay step, record the old batch's **pre-update** loss, perform its ordinary full core update, and leave `W.grad=None`. At the *next* new step, compute that batch's pre-update core loss/gradient and detach the current feature vector and both reward losses. Those observations finish the **previous** action's reward. Accumulate the ordinary new-batch core-loss gradient and `-advantage × sum(log Bernoulli probability of previous actions)` on `W` only, then call **one** AdamW step with the controller's separate, preregistered learning rate. Form/save the core's full proposed displacement including momentum and decay; after the step sample a **fresh** 16-gate action from the *updated* `W` using the detached current features, and apply that fresh action to the current step's proposed core displacement. Hold it for subsequent new steps until the next replay slot. Using a provisional full AdamW core step followed by restoration to `old_weight + gate × (provisional_weight−old_weight)` is one possible implementation, but it needs a temporary weight buffer and exact forced-zero/forced-one tests. On nonreward new steps and replay steps `W.grad` stays **None** (`zero_grad(set_to_none=True)`), so standard AdamW should neither move `W` by decay nor advance its moments then. Predeclare whether W's phase optimizer moments reset or carry over; its **weights** carry over.

The last sampled B action reaches replay step 2,500 but has no in-phase following new-batch loss; discard it rather than use the first maze batch as a cross-phase reward. Likewise discard the final C action. This yields **249 B + 149 C = 398** rewarded blocks, assuming a first action at each phase's step 1 and replay at steps 10,20,… . There are still exactly **6,500 joint optimizer calls** and **400 ordinary old replay batches**, with no extra old or new model passes for reward. Do not mistake 398 controller-gradient events for 398 additional optimizer steps. An important credit caveat remains: the new loss read at the first new step *after* replay includes the intervening **full replay update**, so it is not solely caused by the preceding nine gated new steps. The old pre-update loss is cleaner for that block, but compares different generated old puzzles through an EMA. That noise and credit bleed must be logged as limitations, not described as a precise before/after causal reward. A same-puzzle pre/post probe, or a second new evaluation before replay, would add passes and needs a separately counted budget.

## Final reward-objective caveat: rolling baseline versus mastery anchor

The illustrative reward above uses a **rolling `EMA_old_before`** inside `max(0, L_old_after − EMA_old_before)`. If grid loss gradually rises during B/C, that EMA rises too. The penalty can then shrink toward zero **while grids remain forgotten**: it measures a local deviation from recent performance, not retention of phase-A mastery. A consolidation-style alternative is a **fixed scalar training-loss anchor** for each old kind, captured when that kind first becomes old (grid at the end of A, sum at the end of B) from an EMA of ordinary code-generated practice batches. Grid's anchor would remain its A-exit value through both B and C; resetting it to the B-exit value would erase B's forgetting from the target. A reward term such as `max(0, L_old_after − anchor_old)` would retain a penalty for persistent degradation without storing model weights, teacher predictions, skill copies, or drawing extra examples. This is a *possible objective*, not a selected replacement or a measured fix. One batch is a noisy anchor, and even an EMA of prior ordinary batches may differ from the later replay distribution. A fixed anchor can also keep penalizing an already damaged skill when the present gate cannot repair it, encouraging broad freezing.

The **policy-gradient variance baseline** is a different quantity: it may be a running scalar from *previous* rewards, detached and independent of the current sampled action conditional on the current state. It should not be mistaken for the old-skill mastery target. The new-skill term and old penalty also have no guaranteed balance: early new-loss gain might be around 2 while old penalty is around 0.1, for example, so the policy could ignore early forgetting; later both gains may plateau, giving little signal even if some regions are frozen. Those are scale illustrations, **not observations from this model**. Exact own-stop skill retention is still decided only by fresh sealed scoring, never by the training-loss reward.

Any future pushed PASSMARKS must choose **one** old-loss reference (rolling local EMA or fixed phase-entry mastery anchor), how it is computed from existing practice batches, whether an anchor ever resets, the old/new reward coefficient and normalization, the separate REINFORCE variance baseline, and the phase-boundary handling **before** controller software tests or training. It must log the reference, current old/new losses and both reward components so a zero penalty cannot be misreported as old-skill preservation. This draft makes no such choice and authorizes no new run. AR1 and HEAD remain untouched.
