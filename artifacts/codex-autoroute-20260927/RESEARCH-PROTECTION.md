# Shared-weight interference protection: bounded research recommendation

**Status:** literature and repository-results review only, 2026-09-27. No model, software test, training, download, or panel scoring was run for this note. This is a possible *next* experiment, not an amendment to the pushed [AR1 marks](PASSMARKS.md) or a claim that AR1 succeeded. Keep AR1's replay-timing hypothesis and execution intact. The user's “brain” comparison is a design analogy about retaining an old capability while learning a new one, **not** a neuroscience result. Ben's later idea—learn *which regions to update during sleep*, rewarded for old-skill protection **after** new learning—sets the priority here.

## Recommendation and evidence rank

**Prioritize one learned plasticity-controller experiment**, after AR1 is reported and separate marks are pushed. The controller produces one scalar in [0,1] per declared region of the same dense reasoner, scaling that region's *actual proposed AdamW weight displacement* during sleep/new-skill training. It learns from delayed old-plus-new practice outcomes. This is qualitatively different from calculating a Fisher importance or projecting a gradient with a fixed formula. [ANML](https://doi.org/10.3233/FAIA200193) is primary evidence that meta-learned selective activation/plasticity can help continual learning in its setting; [differentiable plasticity](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/miconi18a.html) and [Meta-Experience Replay](https://research.ibm.com/publications/learning-to-learn-without-forgetting-by-maximizing-transfer-and-minimizing-interference) support learning an update policy or representations that reduce interference. They do **not** validate this controller, this task, or this short budget. ANML used a separate modulatory network and much more meta-training than proposed here.

The ranking under Ben's stated objective is **(1) learned controller, most faithful but highest variance/compute risk; (2) cached replay-gradient projection, efficient computed fallback; (3) online synaptic importance; (4) output/function regularization or a larger orthogonal subspace.** The fixed targets—grids5 after C >=180 mean and >=160 each, sums4 after B >=195, maze7 within 10 of paired baseline, and T >=paired baseline+40—remain severe. No cited paper establishes all four on this architecture. The [e4 dense results](../claude-rsn358e4-20260927/RESULTS.md) show grids falling from 196.5 after A to 128 after C, but that arm had oracle `env` and read dev panels; it is **not** the label-free control. A controller may protect grids simply by suppressing maze learning; the new-skill gates must rule that out.

## Preferred next experiment: learned regional update gates

**One candidate change and equal size.** In a *new* paired study, both arms use the same controller-bearing architecture and the same code-generated practice stream. The baseline controller learns from new-skill improvement alone; the candidate controller learns from **new-skill improvement minus old-skill degradation**. That is the one tested change: the delayed reward objective. Both arms have 1,646,750 trainable parameters at all phases, 6,500 real optimizer steps, batch 64, exactly 400 scheduled old-kind replay batches, and the same A/B/C schedule (2,500 grids; then 250 grids/2,250 sums; then 75 grids/75 sums/1,350 mazes). The common controller architecture costs parameters: replace both arms' four 256-wide visible-context vectors with three (freeing 256 scalars), and use precisely those 256 for `W[16 regions,16 training features]`, with no learned bias. Count those 256 even though the controller is training-only; verify the exact count mechanically. This common capacity trade is disclosed and may lower both arms. The paired baseline, not historical e4 or AR1 scores, defines the maze and T comparisons. Use fresh sealed mixed panels and seeds; do not select them from AR1 results.

**What it controls.** Before registration, publish a complete mapping of the reasoner's parameter tensors into exactly 16 regions (embeddings/context, each block's attention/MLP/norm pieces, answer/stop heads; every trainable tensor exactly once). The controller takes a fixed 16-number *training-only* feature vector for each region: recent new and replay gradient RMS/cosine, old/new practice-loss trends, proposed update norm, previous gate, and fixed missing-value flags for unavailable history. Publish all 16 formulas/scales in the new marks. No `env`, inferred kind, target, checker result, panel identity or phase is passed to the inference model. The training loop may use known generator provenance to maintain old-loss trends; there is no per-skill inference router. Sample each gate from a fixed-concentration Beta policy with mean `sigmoid(W_i·x_i)` and hold the 16 sampled gates for the new-kind steps until the next replay slot. The actions are continuous in (0,1); forced endpoints 0 and 1 are reserved for software tests. The controller's parameters learn via policy-gradient reward, not a hand-set gradient threshold.

For each reasoner tensor, form the normal AdamW proposed displacement `d` **after** its moments, bias corrections and decoupled weight decay. Apply `gate*d` to the live weight. Keep the AdamW moment evolution predeclared and identical in both arms. Scaling raw gradients alone is wrong here: adaptive normalization may cancel continuous scaling, while momentum or decoupled decay can still move a weight whose raw gradient was zeroed. The essential software tests force all gates to **0 and verify every reasoner weight bitwise unchanged, including decay**, and force all gates to **1 and verify the full weight trajectory matches ordinary AdamW** on identical batches/seed. Tests must also check that each named region receives the intended gate and that controller gradients/REINFORCE log-probabilities reach all 256 controller parameters. The 0-gate test is about *weights*; optimizer moments can still evolve if that is the registered rule.

**Delayed reward without hidden extra replay batches.** Use each scheduled old replay batch as a probe **immediately before** its normal update. It has not been trained during the preceding gated block, so its pre-update loss measures old-skill harm after several new-kind steps. Use the next scheduled new-kind batch's pre-update loss as a similarly unseen new-skill probe. Keep non-trainable running baselines of these training losses from prior ordinary batches. For the block's delayed reward use, for example, `R = (EMA_new_before - L_new_after_block) - max(0, L_old_after_block - EMA_old_before)`; the baseline arm omits the old term. Fix the coefficient at 1 and the EMA rate in pushed marks. Both losses use the same answer CE + 0.5 stopping BCE units. The new term makes full freezing unattractive; the exact S_B/maze/T marks remain the decisive new-learning checks. The old term is **noisy** because successive generated batches differ; the EMAs are training-only baselines, not sealed-panel scores. This is a delayed *proxy* for harm, not a direct reward from final exact accuracy. Update `W` with a REINFORCE estimator `(R - running_reward_baseline)*sum(log_prob(gates))` at the next ordinary optimizer step; count that as one of 6,500, not a hidden meta-step. No differentiable K-step unroll or second-order model backward is assumed. Fewer than 400 reward blocks are available, which may simply be too little signal.

**Budget semantics.** This version reuses the ordinary pre-update forward/loss for the 400 old replay batches, so it adds **zero old-example forwards and zero old optimizer batches** beyond the schedule. It uses the next ordinary new batch's forward/loss in the same way. The controller does add Beta sampling/log-probability work, a small policy backward, gradient-feature reductions, and up to two retained parameter-sized replay-gradient vectors (2×1,646,750×4 bytes = 13,174,000 bytes ≈12.56 MiB) plus controller optimizer moments. The 256 controller weights are included in the fixed model count. If a proposed implementation instead re-evaluates old probes before and after every block or performs virtual lookahead, count **every** added forward/backward and old-sample exposure; it would breach a 400±10% cap interpreted as *all old-example passes* and is not this experiment. Publish optimizer-step, unique-batch, sample-exposure, forward/backward, wall-time and peak-memory counts for both arms. Do not claim equal compute merely from equal optimizer steps.

**Outcome and integrity.** Freeze the requested gates in new pushed marks: G_C mean >=180 and each seed >=160; S_B mean >=195; maze after C >=paired baseline−10; T after C >=paired baseline+40, with the paired-win condition if retained from AR1. A gate policy that protects grids by preventing sums or mazes from learning fails. Evaluate one shuffled mixed stream per phase, one request at a time, with the model's own stopping decision. Poison hidden `env`, `size`, target, metadata and panel names, and permute request order/batch neighbors: all predicted tokens and stop probabilities for the visible request must be unchanged. The controller, its training provenance and reward baselines are absent from the public inference interface; the checker sees hidden metadata only after outputs are fixed. Recount raw predictions independently. A single final reasoner checkpoint answers all three kinds.

**What is and is not learned.** The 16 feature formulas and region boundaries are designer choices; the mapping from those features to gate distributions is learned from delayed outcomes. Success would support a learned update policy **on these three distinct native puzzle formats**, not general semantic routing or a guarantee that old skills never fade. Compared with a static importance score or deterministic gradient projection, the key evidence would be region-gate changes that predict lower later old loss *without* slowing new exact accuracy. Report region-gate distributions, replay/new losses and their changes, policy entropy, controller gradient norms, seeds, training minutes and peak memory. Pre-register ablations only as report-only software diagnostics, not as candidate selection. A gate saturated near zero, chance-level policy, or new-skill failure is a negative result, not grounds to add meta-training after reading scores.

## Computed fallback: cached replay-gradient projection

This is **not** combined with the learned-controller candidate. It is a separate, lower-priority hypothesis if the user later wants a simpler computed rule. [GEM](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2017/file/f87522788a2be2d171666752f97ddebb-Paper.pdf) constrains old memory losses; [A-GEM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.00420) projects against an average old reference gradient. The budget-fitting version below is a stale-gradient adaptation, not a result from either paper. It would need its own pushed marks and paired control. Its lower training compute is the advantage; its reference may be too stale or weak to meet the strict grid target.

### Separate alternative specification, not part of the controller experiment

Pair six *fresh, preregistered* seeds and sealed mixed panels. Give both arms AR1's common label-free 1,646,750-parameter dense model, native one-request inference, code-generated distributions, AdamW settings, batch 64, 6,500 optimizer steps and **identical e4 schedule** in both arms: A 2,500 grids; B 250 grids + 2,250 sums; C 75 grids + 75 sums + 1,350 mazes. Thus each receives exactly **400 old-kind replay batches** (325 grid, 75 sum), well within 400±10%; new-kind exposure is also identical. The baseline has ordinary AdamW updates. The candidate changes **only the update rule on B/C new-kind steps**. Keep the same model weights trainable throughout. Do not recycle AR1's evaluated panels to select a variant, and do not compare a new label-free candidate to e4's historical labeled-input 470.17 as if paired.

For the candidate, maintain at most one non-trainable float32 gradient vector per previously seen kind, outside the model and absent at inference. The final already-computed A batch gradient initializes `r_grid` at B entry; the final B sums-batch gradient initializes `r_sum` at C entry. Every scheduled replay batch is the normal optimizer step; immediately before applying that step, replace that kind's reference vector with its current loss gradient. Do **not** draw an extra old batch or take an extra optimizer step. On a new-kind step, compute the ordinary clipped-loss-gradient AdamW state transition and its *proposed full parameter displacement* `d`, including weight decay. Apply the closest displacement `d*` in Euclidean norm satisfying `r_grid·d* <= 0` in B, and both `r_grid·d* <= 0` and `r_sum·d* <= 0` in C. A one- or two-constraint nonnegative quadratic projection has an analytic active-set solution; detect zero-norm/near-collinear references and use a declared tolerance rather than an unstable division. Update AdamW moments exactly as in the paired baseline, then replace only the candidate's proposed displacement by `d*`. Replay steps themselves use ordinary AdamW updates. Fixed rule, no validation-score feedback, no chosen-best checkpoint, no gradient mask keyed by an inferred or oracle kind at inference. The training loop already knows which generator produced a training batch; that provenance is only used to refresh the two reference vectors. The public request still contains only visible tokens and slots.

Projecting the **actual AdamW displacement** matters: projecting a raw gradient and then allowing Adam momentum and decoupled weight decay to move the weights can violate the intended first-order constraint. For a fresh old loss gradient `r`, `r·d* <= 0` means that loss is not predicted to rise to *first order at that point*. It says nothing about a finite nonlinear step, a different old example, exact puzzle accuracy, or future steps. Here the reference can be up to roughly ten B steps old and much older for an individual kind in C; this is a material departure from fresh-reference GEM/A-GEM. The scalar product on the cached batch is a software invariant to log, **not** an old-skill success metric.

This rule adds **zero model parameters, zero extra backward passes and zero extra training examples**. It stores two vectors of 1,646,750 float32 values: 13,174,000 bytes, about **12.56 MiB**, plus one candidate displacement and projection workspace of roughly one or two more such vectors. It adds vector dot products and scale/add operations on each B/C new-kind step and a tiny two-constraint solve, so GPU/MPS transfer or flattening can still make wall time nontrivial; benchmark time is an outcome, not assumed free. AdamW already maintains its usual moment tensors. The reference vectors are temporary optimizer state, not teacher outputs, parameter anchors, expert weights, persistent inference state, or per-skill model snapshots. A checkpoint for exact interruption recovery may hold current optimizer/reference state, but only the final **single model** is served.

Pre-register the **same numerical targets and paired-seed decision logic** as the user's requested next gate (G_C mean >=180 and each >=160; S_B mean >=195; maze mean >=paired baseline minus 10; T mean >=paired baseline+40, with paired wins if retained from AR1). Also declare the exact tie/tolerance rule, logging, seeds, panel separation, and falsifier in new pushed marks *before* any implementation or throughput probe. There is no authority in this note to change AR1's pushed marks or to run this candidate now.

## Why computed alternatives rank lower for Ben's learned-controller goal

- **Gradient constraints** use the same old supervised loss and replay batches already in the budget. GEM and A-GEM provide primary evidence for projection on their benchmarks ([GEM](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2017/file/f87522788a2be2d171666752f97ddebb-Paper.pdf); [A-GEM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.00420)). The proposed cached version trades away fresh reference gradients to respect the 400-exposure cap. **Inference:** it is the lowest-compute fallback, but it does not learn which regions to train from the delayed retention outcome.
- **EWC or synaptic intelligence** slow movement of important weights through a quadratic penalty ([EWC, PNAS 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611835114); [SI, ICML 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/zenke17a.html)). A diagonal importance vector and parameter anchor cost roughly another two full model-sized arrays (at least ~12.56 MiB float32), plus importance estimation/bookkeeping; keeping separate anchors/importance per old kind scales with kinds and resembles the disallowed per-skill snapshot scheme. A single online consolidated anchor avoids that scaling but still needs a saved parameter anchor and a fixed penalty coefficient, and strong penalties can restrict maze learning. The synapse analogy in those papers is inspiration, not evidence about this project's brain claim.
- **Functional regularization** compares current outputs with stored old outputs or a frozen teacher on representative inputs. [Learning without Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282) uses old-task knowledge to constrain new training; [functional GP regularization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11356) retains task function information through inducing points/posteriors. Here an old model/teacher or per-kind output cache violates the stated no-teacher/no-snapshot intent or adds target storage and repeated forward work. It could also preserve the old model's *mistakes* rather than exact checker performance. Since code-generated replay with correct targets is available, this is a poor first budget use.
- **Orthogonal Gradient Descent** preserves old output-gradient directions rather than only old loss gradients ([OGD, AISTATS 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/farajtabar20a/farajtabar20a.pdf)). That addresses the important weakness that near-solved old examples can have nearly zero loss gradients. But even 16 full parameter-space float32 basis vectors would require ~100.5 MiB, plus orthogonalization, Jacobian computation and projection each step; a small basis may miss relevant old behavior. OGD's own paper notes sensitivity to large learning rates and difficulty when tasks are dissimilar. It is a useful fallback research direction, not the first constrained run.

## Relation to the expert failures

The three old expert results do **not** already test the learned-controller proposal. [e4](../claude-rsn358e4-20260927/RESULTS.md) froze old expert paths and had only 352,944 trainable weights in later phases; new-kind sums and mazes were poor, with some routing failures. [e5](../claude-rsn358e5-20260927/RESULTS.md) warmed the router, but maze learning remained 5.67/200 mean. [e6](../claude-rsn358e6-20260927/RESULTS.md) let shared attention/norms train, raising sums after B to 199.17 while old grids after C fell to 42.33. These results support an interference/plasticity tension in *that labeled-input expert architecture*; they cannot show that learned regional updates of a **single dense label-free model** will or will not work. Equally, they warn against saying that any protection automatically keeps old grids *and* learns mazes. The strongest tested total remains e4 dense at 470.17/600 on old dev panels, with oracle env and substantial forgetting.

## Failure modes and integrity tests for a separate projection seal

1. **Stale or weak references:** old loss gradients may be nearly zero after A/B or may no longer represent the old distribution after many new steps. Log reference norms, age, fraction of new steps projected, pre/post `r·d`, grid/sum replay loss and exact mixed-panel accuracy. A zero-norm reference is skipped under the registered tolerance; repeated skips weaken the hypothesis rather than justify a post hoc fallback. No diagnostic may alter the update rule.
2. **Two old objectives can conflict:** C's grid and sum halfspaces may leave a very small useful maze direction; T, S_B, maze and every-seed grid floor can fail despite reduced old-batch loss. Log displacement norm ratio and two-constraint activity. If the cone solver is ill conditioned, treat it as integrity failure under the declared tolerance, not as permission to tune a softer penalty after scores.
3. **Local constraint is not retention:** even exact `r·d*<=0` can coexist with nonlinear old-loss increases, different old examples worsening, or a wrong stopping round. Report the model's own-stop exact checker scores on one shuffled mixed stream; fixed16/any48 are diagnostics only. Do not claim a guarantee that old skills “never fade.”
4. **No label or target leak:** repeat [INPUT-AUDIT.md](INPUT-AUDIT.md)'s hidden-field poisoning and request-order independence. The forward/inference signature takes only visible token/slot arrays; no `env`, phase, size field, target, checker result, batch neighbor, file path or hand-written shape→kind switch may reach the model. Make training data provenance accessible only to the training-side reference-vector update. Verify answer and stop gradients reach the shared context and both dense blocks; count 1,646,750 parameters in both arms and one final checkpoint. Check mixed request outputs/stops before any offline checker consults metadata.
5. **Budget and reproducibility:** count exactly 2,500/2,500/1,500 steps, 400 replay batches and no extra old-reference forward/backward. Log wall time, peak temporary memory, source/marks hashes, request fingerprints and raw predictions. Pair arms on seeds and device/software; independently recount the frozen outputs. Any change to the candidate, tolerance, schedule, or gate after seeing results requires a *new* pushed registration.

**Evidence boundary:** the published methods establish mechanisms and results on their own benchmarks. The local e4/e5/e6 files establish only the measured historical outcomes stated above. Both the learned-controller design and the cached-gradient fallback are engineering inferences; neither has been run here. Passing fresh mixed panels would show retention and learning on these three visibly distinct native formats; it would not establish general semantic skill inference on ambiguous requests, a neuroscience explanation, or a 1B assistant result.

## Bounded conclusion while AR1 trains

AR1 is already running its twelve sequential MPS trajectories with frozen software; this research note creates **no new experiment, software revision, mark change, or run**. The relevant conclusion is that a learned controller is the most direct research answer to Ben's “which areas to train during sleep?” question. The primary literature establishes that selective plasticity can be *learned* in other systems ([ANML](https://doi.org/10.3233/FAIA200193), [differentiable plasticity](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/miconi18a.html)); it does not establish that 256 controller parameters and fewer than 400 delayed rewards can meet this project's strict retention and new-learning marks. The proposed reward uses ordinary code-generated practice losses after new updates, never sealed panel scores. Its old-loss EMA compares different puzzles, so the reward can be noisy even when the policy has a real effect. A more precise same-puzzle before/after reward would add old-example forward exposures and must be accounted against the still-binding 400±10% replay limit; do not hide them as “evaluation only.”

If this line is later registered, the clean *single-change* comparison is retention-aware reward versus new-only reward in the **same counted controller architecture**. That tests whether the old-retention signal improves a learned update policy; by itself it does **not** prove that having a controller is better than a plain dense model. A plain same-size comparator would require a separately declared arm or a later registered test and more training cost. No result should be claimed from this note. The minimum correctness bar before any capability score is the optimizer semantics: a forced zero gate leaves every reasoner weight bitwise unchanged, including decoupled decay, and a forced one gate reproduces ordinary AdamW; scaling gradients before AdamW is insufficient. After that, only fresh paired end-to-end mixed-request scores can decide whether old skills remain while new ones are learned. AR1's final result may inform a later hypothesis, but its pushed marks and running processes remain untouched.
