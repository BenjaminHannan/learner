One design explained · the reasoner

One fact, not four

Today the reasoner learns "the cell above matters" and "the cell to the left matters" as separate facts. This design lets them share one.

Why it might help

A turned maze is the same maze

Each fact is learned from examples. Four facts cost about four times as many examples as one. Our best guess, not measured.

Not built for mazes

It only uses "how far apart"

The rule reads distance along each axis, never the puzzle. It works the same on a line of words, a grid or a 3-D block.

Not too strict

A small slow note keeps direction

Sums carry to the left, so direction sometimes matters. A small direction note stays, and it learns slowly.

Cost

80 new numbers out of 1.6 million

The reasoner has 1,645,726 weights. This adds 80, about 5 in 100,000. It also needs no extra thinking time.

The race

It must beat the loop by 10 points, twice

Same sealed test as the other designs. It fails if it misses +10 in both seeds, breaks an old skill, or the swap check shows no symmetry gain. Our guess: under one in three to pass.

What happens next

Code is built. The runs wait for the Director.

The checks on the code run on a CPU. The real training is queued and held until the Director releases it.
