# Re-running layers with no training: what is known (2026-10-27)

This note supports PREREG-think2 Addendum H. Three Haiku researchers each covered one angle, and a skeptic checked each angle's key numbers against the sources. Each claim below is labelled **shown** (a measured result), **suggested** (argued or indirect) or **untested**. The raw output is in the thread's workflow log.

## Repeating layers in a pretrained model, no training

- **shown (author's own probes, small n, no intervals).** RYS-II ran on Qwen3.5-27B, which has 64 layers and the same 3 DeltaNet + 1 attention pattern as our model.
  - Repeating one middle block raised a 120-item math probe and an EQ-Bench probe.
  - Best single block: layers 24-34 (+11 layers): math +0.120, EQ +0.090.
  - Smallest Pareto point: layer 33 once (+1 layer): math +0.018, EQ +0.095. The math gain is about two items.
  - The first ~5 and last ~10 layers could not be repeated profitably.
  - Source: dnhkng.github.io/posts/rys-ii/
- **shown.** RYS-XLarge repeated layers 45-51 of Qwen2-72B, picked by a sweep over 3,240 blocks on 16 math questions plus EQ-Bench. Model-card changes: MuSR +17.7% and MATH-L5 +8.2% (it does not say whether these are relative), IFEval -2.1%.
- **shown.** Lys et al. 2026 (arXiv 2602.14759) looped a middle block of frozen Gemma 2 2B/9B and Llama 3 8B.
  - Gains are about one point, mostly inside the standard errors. Gemma 2B GSM8K: 25.02 to 26.00 (±1.2).
  - Looping with no regularizer was worse.
- **shown.** CircuitProbe 2026 (arXiv 2604.00716) duplicated one block, measured on 250 items.
  - Gains below 3B: Qwen2.5-3B 49.2 to 54.0.
  - Losses at 7B and above: Qwen2.5-7B 50.4 to 48.8, Mistral-7B 52.8 to 47.2.
  - 20-32B models were not tested.
- **shown.** Transformer Layers as Painters (arXiv 2407.09298), on frozen Llama2-7B:
  - Repeated middle layers stayed below the full model.
  - Copying a single centre layer was the worst variant.
- **shown (ours, Oct 27).** On the K2 pre-screen, repeating any middle or late block hurt or tied on village depth 4-6.

## Loops that were trained

- **shown.** Models trained from scratch as loops follow pointer chains far deeper than unlooped models of equal parameters.
  - Saunshi et al., ICLR 2025, p-hop induction: a 1-layer model looped 6 times scores 99.5-99.9, against 49 for the plain 1-layer model.
  - Sesodia et al., NeurIPS 2026: the loop decodes hop k at iteration k.
- **shown.** Recurrence retrofitted by continued training (McLeish et al. 2025, TinyLlama) raised GSM8K from 46.2 (static) to 52.0 at 32 recurrences.
- **shown.** Huginn (Geiping et al. 2025) is trained for recurrence. It stops per token when the KL between successive states falls below 5e-4, and easier tasks stop earlier. The rule watches the hidden state, not the answer.

## Gaps (untested anywhere we found)

- Repeated layers in a pretrained model, measured on chain-following depth.
- A stop rule on a model that was never trained to loop.
- Repeating blocks that contain Gated DeltaNet layers in a 35B MoE, or at 3 bits.

## Design advice taken into Addendum H

- Repeat whole 3+1 periods, never single layers.
- Choose the block on dev rows, then test once on independent rows.
- Fix the marks and the "proved wrong" line in advance.
- Use at least 100 rows per depth and paired intervals.
- Report the answer-stable stop rule against depth.
- Add a control block.
- Expect a null.

## Advice not taken, and why

- **Hidden-state KL stop rule.** It is untested on untrained models and needs a threshold we would have to tune. We keep the answer rule that Addendum G uses.
- **Expert-routing logging per lap.** It costs memory on the M3 and makes no decision.
- **An 8-bit comparison first.** It is a follow-up if the test passes.
