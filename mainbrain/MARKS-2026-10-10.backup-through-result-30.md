## What the free checks found (no training; scripts mainbrain/gp0.py, mainbrain/kf0.py on branch claude/project-thread-trabt2)
- **Shown (GP-0):** at every layer of the reader and thinker, the spreadsheet questions use the same input directions the
  old questions use. With the directions that hold 97% of the old questions' input energy protected, only 3-9% of the
  spreadsheet input energy is left outside them, and 19-23% of the spreadsheet gradient (by norm) survives the projection.
  At 99%: 1-3% and 10-13%. So "learn only in directions old questions never use" leaves little room at this size.
- **Shown (KF-0):** protecting only the directions the old answers depend on (input covariance x output-gradient
  covariance, K-FAC style) keeps about twice as much of the spreadsheet gradient at the same share: 33% vs 19% at 0.97,
  21% vs 10.5% at 0.99. The K-FAC estimate of old-answer harm per small step falls to about 3e-4 of the unprotected
  step's at 0.97 for both. This estimate is local (one small step) and measured on the diary, not on unseen old questions.
- **Shown (pipeline check):** the screen runner reproduces v4's night 1 exactly (first-10 loss 29.8894, quiz 6.25 before).
  Ten plain updates already fire fewshot_number_rule.
- **Shown (papers, Haiku):** Retaining by Doing (2025) found SFT on the model's own correct samples ("Self-SFT") forgot as
  much as plain SFT in 1-1.5B models (MMLU drop 34.6 vs 28.9, 14.0 vs 11.9; numbers as read by the helper). With our
  unique worked steps, an own checked try is the same target text as the tool's row (A6), so own tries change only which
  problems train. **Suggested:** own checked tries alone will not stop this forgetting; the report's OT path is
  deprioritised here (not run).

## The change under test: protect what old answers use, during the night
Before night 1 the model runs its own diary (4,096 old questions with its pre-mode answers) and measures, per Linear and
Conv1d layer, which weight directions those answers depend on. Every night step is taken as v4 takes it (AdamW lr 1e-4,
weight decay, clip 1.0, half diary replay), then the change of each layer's weights and bias is projected so it does not
move the protected directions. Embeddings, LayerNorms and the talker's output bias are frozen (they act on every question
and cannot be projected). One constant, the protected share `energy`, the same for every subject.
- **Arm KF-97:** K-FAC protection (pairs of input direction x output direction holding 97% of the old-answer importance).
- **Arm GP-97:** input-only protection (GPM's recipe: input directions holding 97% of old input energy).
0.97 is GPM's published setting for Split CIFAR-100 (0.95-0.99 across its benchmarks). Fixed now, not tuned.
Everything else is v4's night 1, byte for byte: the same parent, diary, 721 night-1 rows, 361 updates, seed, and the same
512-prompt forgetting check. Reference v4 night 1 (no protection): near 2.14 -> 26.90 (+24.76), in_dist drop 1.47,
fired rule_apply and seq_next, check agreement 97.27.

Autonomy: the model measures its own old questions and its own answers, and the projection is part of how it trains, a
fixed rule with one constant like AdamW and the undo gate (A11's Level 1). No panel, DEV row or per-subject setting.

### A better guard than the add-on: arms NK and K (KL to the parent on replay), and the join-transfer test, marks written 7:38 PM ET 10-11 (before any run)
Ben (7:26 PM ET 10-11): "see if there's a better anti-frgetting guard" than the add-on. Why it matters: Far transfer found the add-on
blocks transfer to a never-practised join layout (L5 held-out layout 5.2% with the add-on vs 36.7% with every weight learning). Paper
check (three Haiku readers): projection papers (OSFT 2504.07097, SGP 2302.01386, AlphaEdit 2410.02355) show nothing that forgets less
than a hard mask; their one transferable idea, keeping the protected subspace across nights, is arm U (running). Weight blending
(WiSE-FT, LiNeS) is already ruled out here: the forgetting thread's parent-to-v2 blend lost old skills before it gained the new one.
Sparse memory finetuning needs memory layers; SDFT costs about 2.5x. The strongest new lead is **shown** in arXiv 2605.26097 (Marek et al.,
May 2026): a forward KL to the frozen original model on replay rows prevents forgetting better than next-token cross-entropy on the same
rows, and works with real or self-made replay (2M-205M models, Llama-3.2-1B). Our replay is cross-entropy on the model's own greedy
answers, which (forgetting thread, shown) has almost no pull: loss 0.045, and its gradient pushes toward more confidence even at the
parent, which is why freeing it (arms R, R2) did damage. A KL to the parent is exactly zero at the parent and grows only as the model
drifts, over the whole output distribution, not just the top answer.
