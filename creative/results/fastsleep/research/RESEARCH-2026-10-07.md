# Fast sleep: more research (10-07)

Ben asked at 9:34 AM ET on 10-07: "Do some more research into fast sleep". The coordinator's default for this work:
- look for what would make memory sleep learn more per night at the same low compute, especially on the multi-step rules (sq_plus, affine);
- check whether the answer note can come back without the harm.

Everything here is the small card experiment (B2 model, C2 task) on the DEV split, which is the tuning split. Nothing here is about the village model. Every test had its marks written and committed before it ran. The labels mean:
- **shown:** measured here;
- **suggested:** a paper or an indirect result points to it;
- **untested:** an idea only.

## Summary for Ben

- **The cheap sleep is a notebook.** It writes down the programs the model got right and copies them when a similar question comes up. Copying is why it is cheap: about 1 TFLOP, against 20-40 for the fine-tune.
- **Copying is also its limit.** For "a·x + b" every question has a different a and b. I gave the notebook about 200 correct affine examples per model and it still scored 0% on new affine questions. A notebook can't turn "3x + 7" into "5x + 2" (shown).
- **Answer note: safe now, but barely worth it.** With a new rule (it may only speak when a step note also spoke), the answer note did no harm on any of 8 models. The gain was +0.98 points, and the pre-set bar was +1.0. It stays off (shown).
- **Night chaining: proved wrong.** I glued pairs of stored programs together at night to write notes for the questions the model couldn't do. It gained +0.7 points, against +5 needed. A blind search with no notebook did just as well (shown).
- **The sleep is cheap, but the night isn't yet.** The 32 tries per question the model makes before it sleeps cost about 25 TFLOP, about as much as the old expensive sleep. The next big saving is in the tries, not the sleep (shown, measured on one model).
- **The C2 test is easy for plain search.** A blind search with no learning at all gets 77% right using 6,000 guesses per question. C2b should report a blind-search score beside the model, so learning can be told apart from searching (shown).
- **Weights pilot:** when the search notes go into a normal fine-tune instead of the notebook, the model partly learns x²+k (from 0-2% to 20-27%) and gains +4 to +8 points overall at the same cost. It still doesn't learn a·x+b. That is 2 models, a pilot, so it is a lead, not a result (shown on 2 parents).

## 1. Why the multi-step rules miss

These are read-only probes on DEV of what the notebook could reach at best. The key was read only to score, and no model was run. Each parent's library is its W programs plus the 512 old add/mult notes, 66-82 distinct programs.

DEV right, % (ranges over the 8 parents; the last two rows are s205 only):

| What may be used | affine | square | sq_plus | last_digit | double_add |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| One stored program, copied as it is | 0-8 | 100 | 27-39 | 100 | 65-71 |
| Two stored programs chained, shortest one that fits the 3 examples | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| The same with old notes only (s205) | 100 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| The same with W notes only (s205) | 6 | 100 | 28 | 100 | 65 |

- **shown:** copying whole programs caps sq_plus near 30% and affine near 0%. Their constants change from question to question, and the night never stored most of them.
- **shown:** chaining a W shape with an old "+k" or "×k" note, checked against the examples, reaches 100% on every kind and every parent.
- **shown:** the fine-tune B has the same gap. On all six confirm parents it scores 0-2% on affine, 0-10% on sq_plus and 0-16% on double_add, because the night's W records contain almost none of them. So the gap comes from the records the night writes as much as from the notebook.

## 2. Is C2 a test of learning or of search?

A blind breadth-first search with no notes and no model. It builds values from x and the constants 1/2/10/100 and takes the first program that fits the 3 examples. DEV, all kinds:

| Guesses per question | DEV right |
|---|---|
| 1,000 | 64.5% |
| 6,000 | 77.3% |
| 50,000 | 94.9% |

- **shown:** C2's held-out kinds are easy for any method that can check guesses against the examples.
- **suggested:** C2b's "first try" score is still a fair test of the model, because it does not check. But any future arm that checks candidates at test time must be compared with a blind search at the same number of guesses. Otherwise search will look like learning.

## 3. Answer note behind an agreement gate (arm M2)

The answer note may fire only on a question where a step note also fired in the same pass. Nothing else changed. Full table: `ANSWER-NOTE-MARKS.md`.
- **shown:** harm is 0 on chain-5 for all 8 parents, including s205, where the old answer note cost 3.9 points. On the whole never-scored frame slice (6,800 rows, 34 families) harm is -0.13 to -0.41.
- **shown:** the gain is small. It is +0.98 pooled over the 6 confirm parents (+0.83 over all 8), and most of it is one parent (s202, +5.1). Four parents did not change.
- **Decision by the marks:** not adopted. If the roadmap ever wants the answer note, M2 is the safe form.

## 4. Night chaining (arms MC and MB)

At night, for each pool question the model's own tries missed:
- **MC** wrote the shortest chain of two library programs that fits the 3 examples;
- **MB**, the control, wrote the first fit from a blind search with 5,000 guesses.

Then each ran memory sleep exactly as arm M. Full tables: `NIGHT-CHAIN-MARKS.md`.

| Arm | Pooled DEV gain | affine | square | sq_plus | last_digit | double_add |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M | 31.1 | 0.0 | 83.6 | 0.0 | 73.0 | 4.9 |
| MC | 31.8 | 0.2 | 61.0 | 9.3 | 71.6 | 22.8 |
| MB | 31.8 | 0.2 | 73.8 | 3.9 | 73.5 | 13.5 |

The kind columns are DEV right in %, mean over 8 parents.

- **shown:** the chain search found a fitting program for every missed pool question, 701-843 per parent, in 13-22 seconds of CPU and no model FLOPs.
- **shown:** the notebook could not use them. Affine stays at 0% with about 200 correct affine notes per parent. sq_plus and double_add rise, but square falls from 84% to 61% as the new notes crowd its votes.
- **shown:** chaining is no better than blind search (MC minus MB = 0.0).
- **suggested:** a nearest-neighbour notebook can only replay, not adapt. This matches the papers: no paper shows a kNN notebook composing steps without training (WISE, arXiv 2405.14768, reports the same weakness).

## 5. Can weights learn the multi-step kinds from the search records? (pilot, 2 parents)

Same fine-tune as B (lr 1e-3, 16 visits, the same number of updates and the same cost), but trained on W plus the chain records instead of W alone. Full table: `NIGHT-CHAIN-FT-MARKS.md`.

| Parent | Arm | affine | sq_plus | double_add | DEV gain | Chain-5 harm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| s200 | B (W) | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 35.9 | 1.2 |
| s200 | BC (W + C) | 3.8 | 27.5 | 2.0 | 43.8 | 1.3 |
| s201 | B (W) | 1.9 | 2.0 | 7.8 | 39.1 | 0.8 |
| s201 | BC (W + C) | 1.9 | 19.6 | 7.8 | 43.4 | -0.1 |

The kind columns are DEV right in %.

- **shown (2 parents):** weights partly learn x²+k from the search records, rising from 0-2% to 20-27%. The notebook did not, staying at 9% even in MC.
- **shown (2 parents):** neither weights nor notebook learn a·x+b at this budget, 2-4% at best.
- **suggested:** a rule with one constant can be picked up from about 200 examples. A rule with two constants needs more than more examples. Maybe the model needs a way to work out a and b as separate sub-goals, the ExeDec idea. That is an architecture question, not a sleep question.
- The 4-8 point overall gain at equal cost is 2 parents only, so it needs a 6-parent check before anyone relies on it.

## 6. What a whole night costs

| Part | Cost | How measured |
|---|---|---|
| Memory sleep (arm M) | 0.8-1.2 TFLOP | counted, 8 parents |
| Fine-tune sleep (B) | 19-39 TFLOP | counted, 8 parents |
| The night's tries: 32 per question on 1,024 pool questions | about 25.5 TFLOP | counted on s205 for 64 questions (1.59 TFLOP), then scaled |
| Chain or blind search over the missed questions | 13-22 s of CPU, no model FLOPs | timed, 8 parents |

- **shown:** with memory sleep, the night's tries are now about 95% of the night's compute.
- **suggested:** the next cheapness lever is the tries. One option: let the notebook answer first and sample only where its answer fails the examples. Another: stop sampling a question as soon as one try fits. Both are untested here.

## 7. What the papers say

The full list is in `/mnt/project-files/papers/fast-sleep-papers.md`. It has about 70 papers with labels, and the citations were checked against arXiv. Points that bear on these results:
- **Gate maths (shown, maths).** A "0.99 quantile" set from only 24 rows is about the maximum of those rows, and a fresh unrelated row passes it about 4-8% of the time. That fits the s205 answer-note harm. The agreement gate fixed it here. Recalibrating on 1,000 or more executor-made unrelated rows is the other fix.
- **Check before trusting (suggested).** In AlphaCode, CodeT and LEVER, candidates are run on the prompt's own examples and kept only if they fit. For our notebook, that would mean taking the top few programs it votes for and using the first that fits. That should stop the crowding seen in MC (square 84% to 61%). It cannot help affine, because none of the stored affine programs has the right constants.
- **Parameterised rules need training or a different note (suggested).** Composition in program synthesis comes from:
  - a learned search (CrossBeam, ExeDec);
  - "dreaming" new tasks and training on them (DreamCoder);
  - repeated rounds of sample, check and retrain (STaR, CodeIt).
  None of them uses a pure lookup.

## 8. Next steps, ranked

Each step is one change, with its marks fixed before it runs. Ranked by value for cheap learning:

1. **Cut the night's tries (biggest FLOP saving, untested).**
   - Change: the notebook answers each pool question first; the model samples its 32 tries only where the notebook's answer fails the examples.
   - Measure the TFLOP saved and the W records lost.
   - Pass: at least half the sampling FLOPs saved, with W records at least 90% of today's.
   - Wrong if: fewer than a quarter of the questions are settled by the notebook.
2. **Check before trusting the notebook (suggested by AlphaCode, CodeT and LEVER).**
   - Change: take the top 4 distinct programs the notebook votes for and use the first that fits the prompt's examples.
   - Compare with a blind search at the same 4 guesses, so search can't pass for learning.
   - It should stop the crowding seen in MC (square 84% to 61%).
   - On skills rows without examples it can never fire, so harm there is 0 by construction. On rows with examples it fires only when the program fits them.
   - It cannot help affine.
3. **Rare consolidation with search records (shown on 2 parents, needs 6).**
   - Change: every so many nights, fine-tune on W plus the night-search records instead of W alone.
   - The marked check: 6 parents; pass if pooled DEV gain is at least +3 over B at equal cost and harm is at most 2 on every parent.
   - This is the only route here that moved a multi-step kind (sq_plus).
4. **Report a blind-search score in C2b (shown that C2 is search-easy).** C2b's sealed score would sit beside a blind search at 1,000 and 6,000 guesses. It costs seconds of CPU and makes "learning or searching?" answerable.
5. **a·x+b needs a new idea (untested).** Neither notebook nor weights learn it. The candidates are working out each constant as a sub-goal (ExeDec) or a learned search over sub-results (CrossBeam). An outside-opinion prompt for this is in `reviews/gpt-fast-sleep-two-constant-rules-2026-10-07.md`.

Not recommended:
- night chaining into the notebook (proved wrong);
- the answer note (safe now, but not worth it by the marks);
- more notebook records of the same kind, which crowd out the kinds already learned.
