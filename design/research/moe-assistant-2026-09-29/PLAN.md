# How to build and train the best MoE reasoner for Premonition's goal

Written 2026-09-29 17:31 UTC (date -u) by Ben's MoE session, from four Sonnet research reports (web: training/RL, new skills, architecture; repo: goals and existing pieces). Labels: **shown** (primary source or a result file), **suggested** (inference), **untested**. The research agents re-opened only some papers; items marked "recalled" in their reports need a check before we lean on them. Nothing here is a result; every step below is a test with marks sealed before it runs.

## The goal (Ben, 09-29)
A general assistant that talks English and performs tasks well; the reasoner does everything, a thin talker translates words to state and state to words; the reasoner can learn new skills later. Data is not scarce: we can generate tasks and use heavy RL. Ben wants a very sparse MoE (GLM-5.1 / DeepSeek-V3 style: 256 experts, 8 routed + 1 shared).

## What the evidence says, in one screen
- **Sparse MoE pays off with lots of data, and can hurt with little** (shown: ST-MoE, dense wins at 250 examples, sparse at 138k; TRM: MoE hurt tiny Sudoku/maze sets). Ben's plan (plenty of generated data) is the setting where it should help.
- **More, smaller experts beat fewer, bigger ones** (shown: Krajewski et al. 2402.07871; DeepSeekMoE; OLMoE), with shrinking gains (OLMoE: 8->32 experts about +10%, 32->64 +1-2%). **No published result goes as small as our width 256 / expert hidden 64** (untested), and very sparse models need more tokens per stored weight (Joint MoE scaling laws, recalled).
- **With many experts and small batches, balance the load with the DeepSeek-V3 bias trick and global-batch statistics**, not only a per-batch loss (shown: 2408.15664, 2501.11873); per-batch balance with 256 experts is noisy and fights specialisation (suggested).
- **Looped + MoE is a real line of work** (shown: MoEUT 2405.16039, distinct layers grouped inside each repeated step, as our design does). Nobody has trained a 48-round MoE loop (untested). Cheap insurance: feed the round number to the router (suggested).
- **Training recipe for a small assistant** (suggested from shown sources): stages. Pretrain on lots of code-made tasks plus simple English. Then practise in the exact task format, easy to hard, all kinds mixed. Then supervised examples from GLM / GPT-6 Luna. Then RL with code-checker rewards. Reasoning Gym (2505.24760) shows procedural tasks with checkers transfer within related families.
- **RL cautions** (shown): RL mostly sharpens what a model can already sometimes do (2504.13837). Start RL only when the model passes 20-60% of the tasks, and use GSPO (2507.18071) or Dr.GRPO/DAPO with no KL. **MoE-specific:** the router picks different experts in rollout vs training; fix with rollout routing replay (R3, 2510.11370) or GSPO, and freeze the router during RL. **Looped models:** plain RL gave little in Ouro (2510.25741); spreading reward over all rounds (RLTT, 2602.10520) is proposed (search snippets only). **In this repo:** GRPO-style nights on MiniCPM5-1B failed (dl-1: R 63/148 vs S 160/169), and an RL call-count controller collapsed in 2 of 3 seeds (rsn294). So RL has to be tested small first, not assumed.
- **Learning new skills without forgetting** (shown in this repo): plain maze learning erased sums and grids to 0 of 200; sleep with replay brought them back to about 150 and 90 of 200; slp-358n3 PASSED (sleep 381 vs no-sleep 257 of 400 on sums, 0 of 300 lost). The 358e "grow frozen new experts" family FAILED because the router, trained on the new kind only, sent old kinds to the new experts. Research (Lifelong-MoE 2305.12281, BTX 2403.07816) says new experts work when they start as copies of old ones, the old router columns stay fixed while new columns are added, and the router trains on old and new data together, with distillation from the old model. For facts, a memory layer (Memory Layers at Scale 2412.09764) beat MoE at matched compute (shown, at >=134M scale).
- **Talker link:** the likely failure is a talker that ignores the reasoner state and just sounds fluent (suggested). The translator check already requires a talker-alone control that must fail. A 16-32-vector soft prefix trained jointly is the closest known mechanism (suggested).

## The plan: one change at a time
0. **Now:** finish the running test. The sealed 64-expert deep MoE on the fair ruler; holdout verdict after the dev records are committed.
1. **Speed first (engineering, no marks):** the MoE is launch-bound on the GPU (about 0.6 s per practice step on an RTX 5080). Grouped expert matmuls and fewer host syncs should cut cost 2-4x before any big run (untested).
2. **Big-data race (the main test):** the super-sparse MoE (8 layers per round, 256 experts, 8 + 1 shared) vs the loop vs a 64-expert MoE, all trained long on a large mixed stream of every code-made kind in the repo. That is sums, Latin grids, mazes, numbers, the 10 breadth kinds, H1 graph and rank, and HB dates, easy to hard, same compute. **Some kinds are held out entirely.** Scored on held-out kinds after a few examples (the new-skill measure on Ben's goals page) and on the practised kinds. Marks: MoE-256 beats the loop by >= 8 points (twice the noise) on held-out kinds in both seeds; 256 vs 64 reported as its own row.
3. **Balancing for 256 experts:** DeepSeek-V3 bias balancing plus global-batch statistics vs the current per-batch loss, on the winner of step 2 (one change).
4. **RL with checker rewards:** on the step-2 winner, only once it passes 20-60% of RL tasks. GSPO, frozen router, same forward code for rollout and training, log rollout-vs-train routing agreement. Pass: held-out kinds improve by >= 8 points in both seeds with no loss on practised kinds; proved wrong if best-of-8 vs single-sample gap is under 10 points (nothing for RL to sharpen).
5. **New skills by day, consolidated at night:** router-preserving expert growth (new experts copied from old ones, old router columns frozen, trained on new + replayed old, distillation from the pre-day model) vs today's replay sleep. Pass: new kind learned (>= 100 of 200) with old kinds within 10 of before, and under 10% of old-kind cells routed to new experts.
6. **Talker link:** the sealed translator check, then a jointly trained soft prefix, always with the talker-alone and shuffled-state controls.
7. **Facts:** one memory layer as its own test, later.

## Costs and risks (suggested)
- Step 2 is the expensive one: several models trained much longer than today's 12,000 steps. Before the speed work, a 50,000-step practice run is about 8 GPU-hours per model on an RTX 5080 (about $2.60 each at $0.33/h), so 3 arms x 2 seeds is about $15-20 plus ladders; after a 2-4x speedup, $5-10. Price confirmed with Ben before launch.
- Risks: (1) too small for 256 experts to find enough data per expert; (2) routing noise compounding over many rounds, especially in RL; (3) puzzle skill not transferring to English assistant behaviour. Step 2's held-out kinds and step 6's controls are there to catch these.
