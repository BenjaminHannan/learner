## Q1. Pseudo-rehearsal (fresh random or generated inputs, labelled by the pre-update net, no stored examples)
- **Reported (S):** Robins 1995 (Connection Science 7(2), 123-146, https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Catastrophic-Forgetting,-Rehearsal-and-Robins/5ac423a83b4321b43249224fcc528bb70e086826) built pseudo-items by feeding random input vectors through the old net and recording its outputs, then mixing them into new training. Robins and McCallum extended it to Hopfield-type nets, where it helped "particularly when the generated items are representative of the actual task items" (search summary, not opened). Small nets: yes, that is where it was shown.
- **Shown (A), large nets:** Atkinson et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03875 say pseudo-rehearsal is "very effective for simple tasks" but had not worked on complex ones "because it is difficult to generate representative items"; they use a GAN to generate items and lose 1.67% absolute on CIFAR-10 after three tasks.
- **Shown (A), large nets:** data-free distillation finds noise itself is not enough for deep image nets: DeepInversion (https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08795) optimises inputs from random noise against the fixed teacher's batch-norm statistics; Zero-Shot KD (https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08114) synthesises "Data Impressions" from the teacher. Brain-flavoured version: FearNet, https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10563 (generative recall, no stored examples; abstract read).
- **Shown (A), large nets:** unlabeled data from a related distribution, labelled by the old net and chosen by confidence, cut forgetting by up to 46.5% (https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12648). So old-net labels work when the inputs are drawn near the real distribution.
- **What breaks it (suggested, from the above plus our setting; untested):** (1) inputs off the old task's distribution get confident but meaningless labels, so the update pins the net to noise behaviour, not to the skill; (2) our solver takes token grids with a fill-slot mask, so uniformly random tokens are almost never valid puzzles, unlike random pixels; (3) which round's answer is the label (the loop's answers change by round); (4) a pre-update net that is only partly right passes on its own errors (our 128-store distillation added just +4.5 and +9.75 of 200 over true-answer replay: `artifacts/claude-distill-20260928/RESULTS.md`, shown there, on real stored inputs, so pseudo inputs should do no better).
- **Cheapest untested check (suggested):** three input sources, same net, same 512-update sleep: random tokens, random valid-format puzzles from the format alone, and stored real items; labels always from the pre-update net's final round. If random-valid beats random-token by more than the sleep-draw margin, format-validity is the missing ingredient. Note: for sums and grids the repo already has code generators, so "fresh valid puzzles with true labels" costs nothing there; pseudo-rehearsal only matters for kinds with no generator.

## Q2. Model-chosen replay at very small buffers, and weakly-encoded memories
- **Shown (A), large nets:** tiny memories work: "repetitive training on even tiny memories of past tasks does not harm generalization... gains between 7% and 17% when the memory is populated with a single example per class" (https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486), with plain joint training on memory beating designed CL methods. Their baseline sampling is simple; the abstract says nothing about selection.
- **Selection methods (A):** GSS picks a buffer that maximises diversity in gradient space (https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08671); MIR replays the stored items whose predictions the coming update would hurt most, and reports consistent gains and less forgetting over random replay (https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742); Rainbow Memory picks by per-sample uncertainty for diversity (https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.17230); forgetting-event counts show some examples are forgotten often and some never (https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05159, standard training, not CL). RL analogues: Prioritized Experience Replay (https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952, T), Selective Experience Replay (https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10269, T).
- **Hardest-only risk (A, large nets):** repeatedly optimising a small buffer overfits it: "models... are at risk of overfitting on its sample memory" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446) and gives "tight and unstable decision boundaries" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06443). Suggested: a hardest-only tiny buffer amplifies this (noisy or unlearnable items dominate). I found no source that compares a failed-plus-succeeded mix against random or hardest-only at buffers of a few items. **Untested.**
- **Retrieval vs storage:** MIR picks what to replay from a buffer, not what to store. The question of the learner choosing what to store (before it knows the next task) is not answered by anything I read. Untested.
- **Neuroscience.** Shown (S, summary of Denis et al. 2021, J Neurosci 41(18):4088, https://www.jneurosci.org/content/41/18/4088): 54 people learned word pairs at three encoding strengths; awake, weak items were forgotten fastest; a nap made forgetting equal across strengths; fast spindles coupled to slow oscillations predicted consolidation of weak items only. **Contested (S/T):** a 2025 bioRxiv preprint asks whether the sleep benefit depends on memory strength at all and reports the question "remains heavily debated" (title "All Memories Are Equal at Night"; https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.03.686279v1; I read only the opening of the abstract, so its result is not quoted). Drosopoulos et al. 2007 and Stickgold and Walker's "sleep-dependent triage" are from memory (M). Reading: weakest-first replay has some human support and some doubt. Not a design proof.

## Q3. Mixture of experts and forgetting; does more sleep help monotonically?
- **MoE and forgetting.** Shown (A, theory, overparameterised linear regression): MoE beats a single expert at continual learning because a router sends tasks to specialised experts; the gate must stop updating after enough rounds; more experts need more rounds to converge (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16437). Shown (A, large LMs): Lifelong-MoE credits "additional model capacity and proper regularization", i.e. added experts, not sparsity alone (https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12281). Shown (A): active dendrites plus local inhibition route by context and reduce interference (https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00042; details past the abstract not read). Expert Gate (https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06194) and PathNet (https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08734), T only.
- **Not found:** any study isolating "deep experts vs a dense net of the same active size" on forgetting; none of the above matches active parameters. A search summary (S) notes that expert-routing changes are an added forgetting mechanism not present in dense nets (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18699v1, not opened). **Untested.**
- **Our own data (shown, small recurrent net):** the sparse loop (an MoE in each loop block) was not shown to forget less: seed 1 sums were 146 vs the loop's 155 of 200 after the k=64 sleep, 9 below the gate; seed 0 broke no old-kind gate; F_eq was +3.88 and -6.25 (`artifacts/claude-sparse-20260928/RESULTS.md`, verdict NOT PROMOTED). That run was not built to compare active size or to test forgetting, so it is weak evidence either way.
- **Does sleep length help monotonically? Not in our data (shown):** in `artifacts/claude-dir-h6-sleeplen-20260928/DESIGN.md` section 1 (recounted from `slp358n3` raw seeds, 2 seeds for L), a 20x longer night (6,000 vs 300 steps, same lr) tied the short night on sums (L-S +7, +3) and lost the short night's grid gain (day_grids L-S -76 and -94 after night 3; L about equal to no night). **Literature (A, large nets):** repeated optimisation on a small replay pool overfits it (Rehearsal Revealed, LiDER above), which fits diminishing or negative returns with a small store. Synaptic downscaling as a reason sleep helps is from memory (Tononi and Cirelli 2014, M); I read no source giving diminishing returns for sleep length in artificial nets. Suggested: returns fall once the store is fitted, and a lr x steps budget (H6's arm B) is the cleaner control. **Untested** beyond H6's own runs.
