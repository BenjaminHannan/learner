## 1. What the code says (shown, read on 09-29)
- `claude_fewex_net.py:78-80,106`: state h starts at zeros; each round is `z = h + e`, two blocks, then a LayerNorm (`ln_state`). So the state is renormalised every round and the puzzle is re-injected every round. Read head and halt head both read `ln_out(h)` (halt = Linear on the mean over cells).
- `claude_fewex_net.py:161-172`: stop target = "every fill cell exactly right this round", trained only at rounds up to 16 (`TRAIN_ROUNDS`), on sums and grids that the practised net gets 400 of 400 right at depths 16, 32 and 48 (SL DESIGN section 6, shown). So on practised kinds the target is "1" at every late round: the head can score well by learning "the state looks settled / round is large" rather than "this answer is right" (suggested). It is never shown a late round with a wrong answer except early in practice.
- `claude_fewex_bench.py:76-78`: stop needs BOTH q > 0.5 AND three identical whole-grid answers, at some round 3..47, else 48. Two conditions, and the repo has never said which one blocks (suggested gap; SL's read records q at the chosen round and at round 48 only, plus flips in 39-48).
- Maze adaptation has no stop loss (bench.py:205), and each update sees 5 rounds (3 free, 2 with gradient), state carried between updates (H12 section 3).
- Baseline: right 263 of 300 with 300 of 300 cap hits (seed 0, k=256), and fixed-16 gets 254 (SL section 6, shown). So on 250+ mazes the answer is right and stable at round 16 yet the stop still does not fire. That is not "the net cannot solve it"; it is "the stop head or the 3-agree test is wrong there" (shown numbers; the split between the two is untested).

## 2. Does the state converge past round 16? What is known, what is not
- Not measured in the repo (suggested, from reading SL's read job: it records only |h48 - h47| / |h48| once, plus flips in rounds 39-48). No curve of movement over rounds 1..48, no comparison with rounds <= 16, no test that the endpoint does not depend on the start.
- Literature (shown by authors, A): the loop-extrapolation papers say thinking longer than trained works when the iteration is well-behaved:
  - Anil et al., Path independence, NeurIPS 2022 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09961, A): equilibrium models that reach the same end state from different starts do better on harder instances and out-of-distribution samples, and "interventions made to promote path independence" improved that, measures penalising it hurt. Author-only, abstract read. This is the closest match to Ben's question. Note it is correlational plus interventions, on their own tasks (prefix sums, mazes, ...), not on halting.
  - Bansal et al., End-to-end algorithm synthesis with recurrent networks (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826, A): plain recurrent nets "overthink": they get worse when iterated past training depth. Fixes: keep the problem in view every round (Premonition already does: e re-injected) and a progressive-training loss that stops the net learning behaviour specific to an iteration number. Author-only.
  - Geiping et al., Scaling up test-time compute with latent reasoning (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171, A): 3.5B recurrent-depth model trained with a random number of loop iterations (random unroll), backprop through only the last few, and a KL-divergence-between-successive-steps exit rule; they report latent states stabilise across iterations. Author-only, abstract read; I did not check how the KL exit was calibrated or how often it fired. The training recipe (random depth, truncated backprop) is nearly the same as Premonition's, except their depth mean is far higher relative to test depth (suggested; details not read).
  - Bai, Koltun, Kolter, Stabilizing equilibrium models by Jacobian regularization (https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14342, A): penalising the Jacobian of the update makes the fixed-point iteration stable and convergent at scale (WikiText-103, ImageNet). Author claim. It targets weight-tied nets that are supposed to reach a fixed point, which is our loop's regime.
- Overall: (shown by authors) convergence is what lets a weight-tied loop keep helping past training depth; (untested here) whether Premonition's 2-block loop, trained to 16 rounds, converges at 17-48. Its per-round LayerNorm and re-injected e make a fixed point plausible, but a limit cycle or slow drift is equally plausible (suggested).

## 3. Halting methods, and what each says about transfer
- ACT (Graves 2016, https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08983, not opened this pass, M): learned halting probability plus a ponder cost. Known in the later literature to be sensitive to the cost weight and to give unstable step counts (M; Banino cites this).
- PonderNet (https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407, A): halting as a per-step probability with a proper distribution, trained on expected loss plus a KL to a geometric prior. Authors report it extrapolates on the parity task where ACT fails. Abstract read; I could not confirm the parity details or how the prior sets the mean step count. Transfer to a new input family is not what they tested (suggested). Pond in the repo already implements the windowed variant; my findings only bear on it as in section 5.
- HRM (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734, A, abstract says nothing on halting) and TRM (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871, A): from memory (M, unverified here) HRM halts with a Q-learning head (halt vs continue) and TRM simplified this to a single "correct now" head, which is the same target as Premonition's exact-now BCE (`ex`). If that memory is right, then TRM is not evidence that this target transfers; it is the same design evaluated in-distribution (suggested; check body text before relying).
- CALM (Schuster 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07061, A): early exit with confidence measures and a calibration step that gives a stated performance guarantee (authors) by choosing the exit threshold on held-out data; speedup up to 3x. Relevant idea: set the threshold from a small set of new-kind examples instead of trusting 0.5. Abstract read only; the guarantee assumes calibration data drawn like test data (suggested), which is what a few-example setting can supply.
- PABEE (patience, https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04152) and the SL/Pond/H12 tests already cover: answer-stability target, hazard plus price, label stop on mazes. I do not repeat them.

## 4. Why a stop trained on "exactly right now" on sums and grids never fires on mazes (suggested, ranked; none tested)
1. **Feature shift into a fixed linear head.** The halt is one Linear on mean-pooled `ln_out(h)`. Maze states differ from sum/grid states, so the logit shifts by a near-constant offset and stays below 0. A constant bias problem would show as q flat and low on right mazes at every round (T1 measures this). CALM-style recalibration or H12 would fix it; SL's target may not, since it shares the same head.
2. **Rounds 17-48 are never trained.** The head is evaluated at 32 rounds it never got a target for, on states that may have drifted. This is about the mechanism fact Ben flagged. Pond and H12 keep the window at <= 20 rounds; SL trains 1..16 too (SL section 7). Only T2 below changes coverage.
3. **The head may have learned a shortcut.** With 400 of 400 right at every depth on practised kinds, "exact now" is nearly the constant 1 after the first few rounds; the head can learn "round is large" or "state norm/pattern of a finished puzzle". That transfers to nothing (suggested; matches the old "stop learned on exactly right" complaint in note 02).
4. **The 3-agree test blocks.** If maze answers flicker by one cell each round in rounds 3..48 even when 250 of 300 are right at round 16, the stop cannot fire whatever q is. Flicker would also mean the state is not at a fixed point. T1 separates 1-2 from 4.
5. Not a cause (shown): accuracy. The stop costs time, not accuracy (fixed-16 within 0-14 of learned, H12 section 2).

## 5. What would make halting transfer, from the sources (suggested mapping)
- Make the loop converge first (path independence, Jacobian penalty, Bansal's progressive loss, random-length unroll). Then "stop when the state stops moving" needs no per-kind learning, because the signal is a property of the dynamics, not of a kind (Geiping's KL exit, DEQ solvers' residual test; authors' claims). This is the only route in the list that works with no new-kind labels and scales with size (a bigger looped model converges just as well or better per Geiping; author-only).
- Calibrate the threshold on a few examples of the new kind (CALM). Cheap; fits "improve with use" because the threshold sharpens as the net practises the kind.
- Train the head on the full range of rounds that will be tested (long unroll). Suggested; supported indirectly by Bansal/Geiping (they train with wide depth ranges), not by a direct halting ablation that I read.
- How this bears on the queued tests: SL's stability target only helps if the round-48 answer is itself stable, so convergence is a precondition for SL, not an alternative (suggested). Pond's price only bites if q can rise at all on mazes; if T1 shows q flat at the floor on right mazes, Pond's head-only training (257 numbers) will have less to work with. H12 tolerates both because it trains the body with the stop loss.

### T3. Contraction nudge in practice (one change: add a convergence term to the source training loss; conditional on T1 = DRIFTS/CYCLES)
Untested. On each gradient round t of `loop_train` beyond the first, add `0.05 x mean over items of ( |h_t - h_{t-1}|^2 / |h_t|^2 )` to the loss, only on the k gradient rounds and only when `total >= 8`. Risk (suggested): the trivial solution is a net that stops moving after one round; the answer loss on the same rounds stops it (accuracy is measured at every round). The weight 0.05 is a guess: fix it now, no second value; if it fails, that is evidence for a different penalty, not for a sweep.

## 8. Short version for Ben
The reasoner only ever practised stopping between rounds 1 and 16 on puzzles it always solves, and then it is asked to decide about rounds up to 48 on mazes. Papers on looped nets say thinking longer only works if the state settles down (converges); nobody has measured that for our net past round 16. First look (free, hours): draw how much the state moves each round, how often the stop signal rises above 0.5, and whether the answer flickers. That tells us whether the stop is broken because the state never settles, because the stop signal is off for mazes, or because of the extra "three answers the same" rule. Then try one of two training changes: practise with much longer thinking (T2), or add a small penalty for a state that keeps moving (T3).
