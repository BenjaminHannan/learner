**Literature breadth and six deeper comparisons.** Searches covered positional memories, Funnel/Hourglass hierarchies, BLT/H-Net, compressed recurrent memory, autoencoders, selective depth, and compressed RAG. Recent extensions include [ATDC, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30080), which schedules compression ratios, and [multi-byte prediction, August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15454), which addresses byte-generation throughput. Neither establishes a small v2-tokenized village reasoner benefit; the latter was screened at abstract level only.

Also screened: [Inference Time Context Sparsity, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24168). Its non-injectivity theorem concerns attention distributions mapped through a fixed value matrix; it does not prove dense attention cannot answer a particular question or sparse selection is universally superior. Its own evaluation shows a substantial extreme-sparsity penalty for a smaller Qwen2.5 checkpoint. That is contrary evidence against transferring large-model sparsity headlines uncritically.

1. **TST: cheaper exposure, followed by recovery.** Raw embeddings are averaged and the next bag predicted, then ordinary token training resumes. Output-only bags preserve ordered inputs and change supervision without shortening the input computation. The paper’s smallest configuration is 270M with untied embeddings; Core and D tie their heads. Resetting both embeddings and output head at the phase boundary removes the gain, but does not isolate either component. Recovery, added data and longer effective context must count; there are no repeated identical runs establishing seed robustness. This is already in the project plan, not a new proposal. [TST v2, §§3–5,7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06546).

2. **H-Net: contextualize before selecting fewer positions.** A cheap encoder produces contextual states; a cosine-similarity router selects boundaries; a larger inner network handles retained states. Dechunking uses smoothing and fine-resolution residuals, with a ratio loss controlling compression. Thus order can survive upstream encoding, unlike raw averaging. Boundary-state selection performs comparably to more elaborate pooling in Appendix E.1. However, full-resolution encoder/decoder work remains; the inspected version reports up to approximately 2× slower training than an isotropic model and variable-memory hazards. Learned boundaries are not guaranteed semantic events. A fixed line-boundary hierarchy is the cheaper comparator; changing the tokenizer is outside the current v2 contract. [H-Net, §§2,4,E.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07955v1).

3. **Compressive Transformer: recent detail plus older summaries.** Old activations leave a detailed FIFO memory and enter a compressed bank, which attention can still read. Pooling/convolution compressors can learn through reconstruction objectives; attention-output reconstruction worked best. Compression gradients are stopped at the main network. That objective deliberately permits information unused by its training queries to disappear. My implication: a later question about a previously irrelevant detail may fail despite good reconstruction loss. Count both banks, per-layer states and auxiliary reconstruction work. This is useful prior art for event/history tiers, not evidence that a tiny episode store needs another hierarchy. [Compressive Transformer, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.05507).

4. **Mixture-of-Depths: skip work while retaining residual paths.** A router admits a fixed top-k subset to attention and MLP computation. Static capacity improves batching, but sequence-wide top-k requires a causal auxiliary predictor for autoregressive inference. Skipped tokens are also unavailable to that block’s attention, so compute sparsity can remove an information path. Random routing underperforms; intelligent selection is not free. [MoD, §§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02258v1). Related [CoLT5](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09752) keeps a light path for every token and separately selects heavy queries and keys/values. Its base model uses more parameters than LongT5-base and has a slightly lower average task score. D’s planned register-only reader is a simpler first comparison; neither attention-matrix shrinkage nor fewer active rows is a whole-model speedup.

5. **REFRAG: compressed overview with exact expansion.** Precomputed contextual chunk vectors replace most passage tokens; selected chunks return to ordinary tokens. Mixed-input training and an RL policy using continuation perplexity teach selective expansion. Published comparisons report similar downstream quality when retrieved contexts are equal; prominent speed gains concern long-context decoding. Precomputation and policy training must be charged to a fresh village. If fraction p of chunks expands and each chunk has s tokens, effective compression is `s/(1-p+p*s)`, not s. Query-dependent expansion is legitimate only after the query arrives. The architecture is close prior art for proposal 2, not a reason to add RL here. [REFRAG, §§2–5,A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01092v1).

6. **Memorization–utilization gap, ACL 2026.** Contextual mean-pooling plus projection feeds memory embeddings to a decoder; reconstruction/continuation training, instruction tuning and outcome RL develop selective reconstruction during answers. Preserving text alone does not guarantee using it. Important qualifications: 16× vector compression becomes approximately 5.3× decoder-position reduction because of delimiters; latency assumes offline encoding; the reported training totals are 428 and 1,208 H100 GPU-hours for 7B and 32B. This is evidence to measure usable information, not a suitable training recipe or budget forecast for Premonition. [Ting et al., §§3, limitations, appendices J–K](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.682.pdf).

**Small-information checks before training.** For any raw bag, `mean(e(A),e(B))` equals its reversal. “A gives B X” and “B gives A X” collide if all differing positions share a bag. Adding positions before averaging cannot help: `mean(e_i+p_i)=mean(e_i)+mean(p_i)`. A balanced isolated two-answer collision bounds accuracy at 50%; it says nothing about performance after ordinary-token recovery.

D is different: [CardWriter](/Users/ben-hannan/Desktop/projects/beautiful-model/premonition/model.py:171) pools contextual reader outputs. No permutation theorem forces those outputs to collide. Proposed diagnostics should compare opposite agent/recipient roles, negation, swapped update order, random entity–value bindings, and unchanged-answer distractors. Hold preceding context fixed or regenerate it consistently, and inspect every surviving path: contextual carryover, chronological indices, raw diary, and name table. Probe failure alone does not prove information-theoretic erasure. Contrary to effortless compression assumptions, [ICAE Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.06945v4) reconstructs random text far worse than ordinary text at the same compression ratio; arbitrary village bindings deserve this stress test.

A minimal proposed information audit is 16 balanced pairs for each of those five perturbation types, inspected at the compressor interface before any fitting. This detects concrete collisions, not general sufficiency. For online storage, freeze one episode encoding before revealing any subsequent question; reuse it across different questions. Re-encoding after a question is an explicit paid reread, never free question-aware compression.

**Proposal 1 — Fixed position-sensitive pooling; conditional first choice.**

For a student: mark words differently according to where they occur, so exchanging the giver and receiver can change the note. Replace learned softmax pooling with `p=(1/J)Σ_j ℓ_j⊙h_j`, where `h_j` are existing contextual line states and `ℓ_ij=(1-j/J)-(i/d)(1-2j/J)` is fixed. Feed p into the existing key/value maps. Keep line age and source fields. No semantic-role parser or future question is supplied.

This directly borrows [MemN2N positional encoding, §4.1](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/5846-end-to-end-memory-networks.pdf). The particular contextual-writer transfer is untested here; worldwide novelty is not claimed. It removes learned pool concentration with no added trainable weights or stored dimensions, adding O(Jd) elementwise work. The reader still costs full price. Positional weights may preserve role distinctions, but are not an injective code and may fail when paraphrases relocate arguments.

The simplest competitor is the already-prepared uniform contextual mean; also retain current single-pool and Opus’s two-pool variant, which adds d+1 parameters, not d+1 stored numbers per card. A small future screen would use the frozen read/choose ladder plus held-out role reversal, event order and paraphrase, with identical answer supervision and no new oracle roles. Supplied-card arms are diagnostic only. Require ≥95% single-fact reading and better paired choosing/role accuracy than the mean at matched total time. Reject the added positional structure if only probes improve, paraphrase performance regresses, or mean/two-pool is equally effective and cheaper. This is a contingency after Opus’s current comparison, not a competing implementation assignment.

**Proposal 2 — Reread exact text behind selected cards; conditional second choice.**

For a student: use the index card to find the page, then reread the exact sentence instead of trusting an abbreviated note. Keep the existing query-independent writer and ASK. For each selected line, obtain its visible token span through `line_start/line_len`; encode it in original order with the existing reader and expose token states to the answer reader. Keep those evidence states immutable. Initially expand every selected line under one declared total token cap; report overflow instead of silently truncating. Do not add a learned expansion gate.

REFRAG is close external prior art; C* already recalls raw lines, and read-only memory readers are already proposed locally. The narrower untested question is whether D’s existing selected lines become usable when their exact tokens bypass the pooled-value bottleneck. This is an interface/control experiment, not a new memory principle. It does not repair selecting the wrong second-hop relation. Independently encoding a line also loses earlier context needed for pronouns; such failures must remain visible.

Use the same selected indices for diagnostic pooled-versus-expanded reads, then test ordinary end-to-end retrieval. Compare with mean/two-pool D, token-budget-matched C*, and E-long. Add distractors and two-hop compositions while keeping input access and training labels matched. Expansion earns consideration only if it improves actual answers and relevant/irrelevant interventions, then reaches a common target sooner or matches quality with lower measured deployment cost. Reject if C* dominates, most lines require expansion, or Q=1 costs erase the benefit. Persistent tokens/offsets, reread passes, extra answer attention, cached states, and training adaptation all count; no additional teacher labels are necessary.

**Whole-system limits and decision discipline.** Under an illustrative 140-line, 20-token/line episode, uint16 source tokens occupy 5,600 bytes before metadata, versus 53,760 bytes for 140 `(64+128)` bf16 card vectors. Actual [store code](/Users/ben-hannan/Desktop/projects/beautiful-model/premonition/store.py) normalizes keys in fp32, so that vector figure is not a measured implementation footprint. Caching 2,800×128 bf16 token states instead costs 716,800 bytes before other caches. Exact text can be cheap to retain and expensive to reread.

Report `T_episode + Q*(T_search+T_reread+T_reason+T_decode)` for Q=1/10/100, plus memory and amortized training costs. With compressible time fraction f and speed factor s, ideal speedup is at most `1/((1-f)+f/s)` before overhead. Include padding, data generation/tokenization, projections, scans, sorting, gather/scatter, optimizer state, recovery and unsuccessful development. The aligned pilot logs 331,874,304 processed positions; its report estimates only about 200M real tokens. Removing padded work while preserving visit boundaries is a plausible lower-risk efficiency control, not a demonstrated speed gain.

Runtime remains unknown until measured. Preserve tied embeddings across any TST/recovery comparison; untie only as an explicitly parameter-matched study. Freeze label-free preprocessing and exact tokenizer identity; keep gold evidence, hidden roles and depth privileged diagnostics. Evaluation stays read-only. Finalists require three precommitted seeds, untouched test data and paired visit-clustered uncertainty (99% bounds; existing coverage floors); retain `full_verdict: false` until the complete contract is met. None of these designs opens an experiment budget.

**Lead-review addendum — limitations, source locators, and reconciled shortlist.**

This addendum supersedes the earlier first/second-choice ranking. After Opus's existing mean/two-pool comparison, retain **one bounded selected-line reread fallback, only if the model retrieves the required facts but pooled values remain unusable**. Current held-out retrieval (both facts fetched in 1/189 cases) does not establish that condition. Positional pooling remains a cheap possible control, not an additional priority. No experiment was performed or authorized.

**Positional pooling does not force preservation.** The same trainable [Reader and CardWriter](/Users/ben-hannan/Desktop/projects/beautiful-model/premonition/model.py:158) can still learn value-only states: removing learned pooling weights leaves the encoder and value projection free to discard identity. The fixed reducer also permits cancellations. Algebraically, its coefficient is `(1-i/d)+(2i/d-1)*(j/J)`, giving each feature one mixture of its mean and first positional moment, not a lossless sequence code. An informative key still cannot supply information excluded from the decoder's value interface.

Fixed position is not semantic role. “A gave X to B” and “B received X from A” move the same participants to different positions; insertions also change normalized positions. Contextual states could learn this invariance, but the reducer provides no guarantee and could strengthen template shortcuts.

The minimum useful behavioral condition is **joint success on complementary, previously unknown questions from one frozen card**, under fresh random bindings, crossed with meaning-preserving paraphrases and meaning-changing role swaps. Balance word inventories, positions, line order and lengths so incidental cues do not predict answers. Correct responses must change with the binding and survive paraphrase; compare at identical supervision, stored dimensions and available metadata, without raw-text or key bypasses. Both-twins-correct is more informative than average accuracy. This establishes accessible binding on those cases, not lossless preservation; a paired frozen-reader comparison would further isolate the reducer from encoder learning. Probe scores or one-template gains alone cannot make that attribution. Privileged role labels remain diagnostic only.

**Timing wording verified.** [H-Net v2, §4, “Efficiency”](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07955v2) says “our current implementation may be approximately up to 2× slower than an isotropic model during training.” This is a qualified implementation-specific observation, not a universal multiplier or a time-to-quality comparison. The authors explicitly discuss engineering limitations.

[ACL 2026, Appendix J, PDF p.16/printed p.14964](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.682.pdf#page=16) assumes offline encoder precomputation. Figure 10 and Table 13 (PDF p.17) report **TTFT**, reaching 13.3×/11.4× at 120K for 7B/32B, on H100s with FlashAttention-2. These exclude fresh-context encoding and do not measure total answer latency. [Limitations, PDF p.10/printed p.14958](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.682.pdf#page=10) specifies approximately 5.3× decoder-length reduction despite 16× memory compression and explicitly favors multi-query reuse to amortize encoding.

**Cross-critique of memory report.** I read [02-memory.md, including its revised recommendation](/Users/ben-hannan/Desktop/projects/beautiful-model/design/research/final-sweep-2026-09-19/02-memory.md) and agree with its final shortlist; there is no substantive ranking disagreement. [ColBERT §§3.1–3.5,4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.12832v2) supports a different intervention: token addresses could help when a correctly formed query misses a fact because pooled keys lost a distinction. Independent MaxSim matches do not guarantee consistent roles, and retaining the same values leaves payload binding unresolved. Neither token addresses nor rereading an already-selected line supplies an unrequested second-hop relation.

Rereading instead tests utilization after retrieval: hold the model's selected indices fixed and expose their ordered text, with matched interface adaptation. It may still fail because the reader ignores identity or a line needs earlier pronoun context. Charge any prefix expansion and give it to matched controls. A failure of an unadapted decoder on unfamiliar token states is inconclusive.

Neither alternative has demonstrated efficiency here. Token addressing stores vectors for all indexed tokens and increases matching/transfer work; memory's 86× is only its illustrative address-dot-product MAC ratio. Rereading can retain compact token IDs, but adds per-query encoding and answer attention; caching full token states trades those costs for substantial state. Bounded expansion may still win against reading full history, while losing to adequate pooled cards. Compare complete episode-plus-Q-query costs and useful answers for Q=1/10/100, including raw text, offsets, caches, training adaptation and overflow. I accept withdrawal of the arbitrary three-point margin: no small observed difference establishes equivalence. All existing seed, visit-cluster, read-only and label-free rules remain in force. These alternatives are not to be stacked.
