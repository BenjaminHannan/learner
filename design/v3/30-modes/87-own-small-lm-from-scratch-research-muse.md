## 1. How small can coherent simple English go (2024–2026 evidence)

**TinyStories** (Elden/Li; verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759). GPT-Neo-style decoders, context 512, vocab restricted to top 10k tokens, trained on ~2M synthetic stories (~465M tokens post-tokenization per https://huggingface.co/datasets/KabirBakhshaei/TinyStories-tokenized-10k). Released sizes 1M/3M/9M/28M/33M + 1–2-layer variants (https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07759v2). Measured quality is GPT-4-as-teacher grading on grammar/creativity/consistency: coherence emerges around hidden-size 128; the ~28M model writes fluent multi-paragraph stories; the ~80M model scores near-perfect on grammar and consistency but still trails on creativity (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07759v1). Community repro: a 3.6M GPT-style model reached loss 2.17 after ~3 h on one T4, a 19.3M model loss 1.40 after ~4.7 h on one A100 (community report, not peer-reviewed: https://github.com/SauravP97/tiny-stories-hf). Takeaway: **1–30M params + ~0.5B simple-English tokens = coherent simple English** — verified fact.

**BabyLM challenge** (verified: 2024 findings https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05149; 2023 findings https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08165v1). Budgets: Strict-Small 10M words, Strict 100M words. Winners both years both tracks were **encoders** — ELC-BERT (2023), GPT-BERT (2024) — judged on BLiMP/GLUE comprehension, *not* open-ended coherent generation, so they do not answer Q1 directly. The generative datapoint is **Baby Llama**: a 58M LLaMA-style decoder distilled from a 705M GPT-2 + 360M LLaMA teacher ensemble, all on the same 10M-word corpus (verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02019); it beat its teachers and 125M baselines (e.g. 93.1% on BLiMP irregular forms vs 67.5% OPT-125M). Follow-up BabyLlama-2 (345M, 10M words) confirmed distillation beats direct training at this scale (https://aclanthology.org/2024.conll-babylm.26). Related: 10M-word corpus comparisons show Gutenberg text beats TinyStories on BLiMP for ≥44M models — i.e. simple data helps fluency, harder data helps grammar judgments (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06672v1). Takeaway: **~60M decoder + 10M words (distilled) = strong grammar; generation fluency at this scale is carried by TinyStories-style results, not BabyLM.**

**SimpleStories** (Finke et al. 2025; verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09184; NeurIPS 2025 Datasets track https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/34696303f5996d5c54e699c0b462ae48-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html). 2M stories each in English/Japanese, parameterized prompts for diversity, fully open code+data (https://github.com/simple-stories/simple_stories_generate). Trained suite 1.25M/5M/11M/30M/35M with a 4k custom tokenizer; reports better sample efficiency than TinyStories and "moves the frontier for the fewest-parameter LM that outputs grammatical English" (per the NeurIPS abstract). Takeaway: **the current floor for grammatical English is ~1–10M params** — verified as a published claim; treat exact numbers as their measurement, not ours.

**SmolLM2-360M** (the placeholder; verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02737): 360M params trained on **4T tokens** (≈$250k compute for the 1.7B sibling). This is ~1,000× the tokens a simple-English talker needs — it is evidence *against* imitating that recipe, not for it.

**Consumer-GPU hours (partly estimated).** Verified datapoints: see T4/A100 repro times above. *Estimate (mine, not measured):* a 20–35M bf16 decoder on ~300–500M simple-English tokens on one 5070 Ti should take roughly **1–5 wall-clock hours** (small models are launch-bound, not FLOP-bound); the mandatory 200-update smoke test in our own recipe must replace this guess before any wave is planned.

## 3. Architectures that keep a small LM fact-controlled (evidence at ≤100M)

- **Retrieval-conditioned decoding (RAG / FiD).** RAG couples a seq2seq generator with a dense Wikipedia index; RAG models beat parametric-only baselines on open QA (verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401). FiD encodes each passage independently and fuses in the decoder; FiD-base (220M, T5-base) set SOTA on NQ/TriviaQA (verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01282v2). Honest scaling note: both demos are >100M — but the mechanism (concatenate retrieved text, attend, generate) is size-agnostic, and our notebook *is* the index. This is the closest verified ancestor of "reasoner hands the mouth a record."
- **Copy mechanisms.** Pointer-generator networks add a copy-vs-generate switch plus coverage, fixing exactly our failure mode (hallucinated details, repetition), with +5 ROUGE-1 on CNN/DM at small seq2seq scale (verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368). This is the direct evidence for doc 43's pointer/slot design: **a ≤100M decoder that can only copy content words from its input has small-scale measured precedent.**
- **Tool-token / reflection-token decoders.** Toolformer (6.7B GPT-J learns `[API(…)→result]` tokens kept only when they reduce future-token loss; verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761). Self-RAG (7B/13B learn `<retrieve>`/critique tokens for controllable factual generation; verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511). **Neither has any ≤100M-params evidence — stated plainly.** Adopt the *protocol* (mouth emits a lookup-shaped token sequence that software executes against the notebook) but do not claim a small model can *learn* the gating until rung data says so.
- Net position: at our scale, measured support exists for **retrieval-concatenation + copy-only outputs**; tool/reflection tokens are an unproven-at-small-scale second step. Doc 43's four locks (alphabet, slot-use, ears-loop, template fallback) are therefore the right shape: software guarantees first, learned fluency second.

## 4. Recommended first run (proposal, not a sealed plan)

- **Model:** 20–35M decoder-only transformer (SimpleStories-30M config: 10 layers, d=512, 8 heads, 4k BPE; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09184) **plus** a copy head over the input (pointer-generator switch, https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368). No retrieval weights in v1 — facts arrive as prompt text from the reasoner.
- **Data:** SimpleStories-EN (open) single pass, ~300–500M tokens; TinyStories-V2-GPT4 as the one-change alternative arm.
- **Cost:** *estimate* 1–5 h bf16 on one 5070 Ti; confirm with the 200-update smoke test per brief 48.
- **Pass marks to seal (draft):** (a) val perplexity ≤ smoke-test-derived threshold on a sealed held-out split; (b) 0 unfaithful content words (any word outside mouth-lexicon ∪ input record strings) on N=3,000 record→sentence probes; (c) 20/20 world-fact questions with an empty notebook answered don't-know; (d) every seed reported, never averaged.
- **Falsification signature:** a single probe reply that states a content fact absent from its input record = FAIL the "mouth can be trusted" claim; fall back to contract templates (doc 43 lock 4), no re-runs into a pass.

## What it means / What it does not mean

**What it means:** a 20–35M from-scratch decoder trained on open simple-English text can plausibly replace the borrowed 360M placeholder for the mouth job, with facts fenced off in the notebook and copy-only outputs.
**What it does not mean:** none of the surveyed work shows a ≤100M model learning *when* to consult facts by itself — controllability at our scale must come from software locks first, with the evidence for learned tool/reflection tokens still missing.
