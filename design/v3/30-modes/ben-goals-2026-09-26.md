## Improves over time and carries skills to new places (Ben, 15:41 UTC)
Ben's words (cmsg_01FuvegZXjMmeUzStiEFVnEW1MwBQ8K4pkaQkCHGK86t3s): "part of this model that I didn't mention is to take the brains ability to naturally improve over time and be generally intelligent. Apply skills learned to other plaves".
- A skill learned on one kind of problem should help on other kinds, not only on more of the same.
- What our tests measure today (checked 15:44 UTC):
  - 358i trains on small puzzles and tests bigger ones of the same kinds (sums, grids, numbers). That is the narrowest form of carry-over.
  - The sleep tests (dl-2, dl-5) measure next-day skill on fresh puzzles of the kind practised. They count only harm on other kinds (forgetting), never help.
  - No test yet checks whether a skill carries to a different kind of problem.
- From now on:
  - Reasoner and sleep tests add carry-over rows: skill on a kind never practised, reported next to the practised kind.
  - A dedicated carry-over test follows 358i: practise some kinds, then measure a held-out kind.
- Ben's example (15:42, cmsg_01FuvegZXjMmeUzStiEFVnEWSwBn3kvRrNHJRVH9GEcD9v): a self-driving system needs huge amounts of footage and practice, but a person learns to drive in about 40 hours, because they bring skills from bikes, games and so on. **So the main measure is how few examples a new kind takes to learn, given what it already knows**:
  - Compare against a fresh net, and against a plain net of the same size that had the same practice.
  - Solving the new kind with no examples at all is reported, not required.

When reasoning and overnight learning both need money beyond their $2, **reasoning gets it first.** Spending over $2 still needs Ben's yes.

## When something isn't working, ask how the brain does it (Ben, 16:05 UTC)
Ben (cmsg_01FuvegZXjMmeUzStiEFVnEWMWVJnvL8azz8YgCn21XSC8): "How does the human brain do this? A good thing you should keep in mind for when something isn't going well is to refer to the brain".
Every brainstorm and FAIL fallback starts by asking how the brain solves the same problem.
Ben, 16:08 (cmsg_01FuvegZXjMmeUzStiEFVnEWH4QwrNgME6fFLSTyR8FFXW): "Obviously where silicon can improve biology it should do so". The brain is the starting point, not a limit. Where silicon does better, we use it:
- exact recall from the notebook;
- word-for-word provenance for every fact;
- a calculator for arithmetic;
- keeping the raw experience in full;
- overnight changes that can be checked and undone. The answer then becomes one sealed single-change test, not a claim. Pointers given to Ben at 16:07, labelled simplified textbook science and not checked here:
- **Selective attention:** the brain attends to what matters for the goal, not to everything.
- **Situation model:** understanding keeps track of who is who across the whole conversation.
- **Hippocampus:** it stores experience by default, as pointers to the raw episode, and handles doubt at recall.
- **Sleep:** it replays old and new memories together (interleaved), and the cortex learns slowly.
- **Reused circuits:** thinking is repeated passes over the same circuits, which are reused across tasks, and this is where carry-over comes from.
