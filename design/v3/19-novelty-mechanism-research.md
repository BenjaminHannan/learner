## Short answer

Add **compositional replay**: between practice blocks, let the system combine familiar pieces into questions it was never given, check them against remembered stories, and practise them. Brain recordings support this kind of recombination; they do not give us a complete recipe for original thought. Recent work connects replay with constructing new plans and relationships [R4, R38, R39].

Start small: generate useful practice, then test question types excluded from both ordinary practice and replay. Give the transformer exactly the same practice too. Transformers can learn composition, so beating a weak baseline would prove little.

This could challenge the controller’s habit of stopping after three calls. It would still be a first step: creating new practice is weaker than inventing an operation or concept. My runner-up is learning a library of reusable routines.

### What the research establishes

**Replay is more than repeating the last event.** Gupta et al. recorded rodent sequences consistent with routes not traversed as complete paths [R1]. That is stronger than literal rehearsal, but weaker than proving the animal deliberately invented and used a shortcut. Liu et al. found human replay organized by learned abstract order rather than presentation order; separable sequence identity and position codes preceded item codes [R2]. The rule itself had been taught.

Kurth-Nelson et al. explicitly propose compositional computation as a hypothesis, including relational binding and assembling compound structures [R3]. Do not cite that perspective as a successful causal experiment. Schwartenbeck et al. combined human imaging and MEG in construction tasks: replay-related signals became more consistent with inferred building-block solutions during deliberation [R4]. This is evidence about constrained inference, not unrestricted concept invention. Its final published title differs from the earlier “compositional visual understanding” title.

Two newer findings make this a better candidate than the classic papers alone. **Bakermans et al. (2025)** combine an explicit computational model with rodent recordings: reusable spatial components enable transfer to changed environments, and replay predicts formation of compositional place responses [R38]. The useful primitives and representational assumptions are part of the model; this is not a clean transformer comparison. **He et al. (2026)** recorded hippocampus and cortex in 28 epilepsy patients performing two construction tasks. Ripple-associated replay tracked reorganization of candidate sequences and cortical representations during inference [R39]. Direct recordings improve temporal evidence; the findings do not establish that an offline sleep phase causes general creativity. Our offline phase is an engineering extrapolation.

**Preplay is a separate and contested claim.** Dragoi and Tonegawa reported sequences preceding later spatial experience; Silva et al. found trajectory events depended on experience under their recording/analysis conditions [R5–R6]. Distinguish recombination of familiar elements from a claim that the brain already represents arbitrary unknown future experiences. The recommendation needs the former, not the latter.

**Factorization helps explain transfer.** TEM connects reusable structural codes to sensory contents, including tests against hippocampal/entorhinal representations [R7]. In our terms, “the object currently filling the next role” should change without relearning what that role does. Qu et al. (2026), studying 203 participants aged 8–25, linked nonspatial grid-like codes to development and inference [R37]. This supports investigating structured representations; it does not prove that a grid module produces originality. Behrens et al.’s cognitive-map account also emphasizes relational organization beyond literal space [R35].

**Imagination and control have complementary roles, but not a settled wiring diagram.** Constructive episodic simulation proposes reusing details of remembered episodes [R9]; hippocampal lesion findings support a role in coherent scene construction [R10]. Beaty’s network coupling result is correlational [R11]. Bartoli et al. (2024) add a causal intervention: stimulation of sampled DMN sites reduced originality in an alternative-uses task without the same effect on fluency [R12]. It does not follow that DMN equals generator and executive cortex equals verifier in every creative task. Campbell’s blind variation and selective retention is a theoretical ancestor of generate-and-test, not a verified neural mechanism; “blind” need not mean uniform randomness [R13]. Spens and Burgess implement constructive memory and imagination using an autoassociative memory and generative networks [R8]; this is a computational account, not a model module already validated in our toy.

**Insight is a change in representation, not just an accuracy jump.** Kounios and Beeman organize evidence around reinterpretation and insight-related neural processes [R14]. Wagner’s sleep result concerns discovering a hidden regularity after practice [R15]. Lewis et al. propose complementary NREM abstraction and REM association [R16]. Löwe et al. (2025) found greater insight following N2 in a particular perceptual task [R17]; sleep-stage membership was observed, not a clean random assignment of a specific neural mechanism. None licenses coding two alternating optimizers and claiming to reproduce human insight.

**Exploration supplies alternatives; it does not establish their value.** Adaptive-gain theory connects LC–noradrenaline modes to engagement and exploration [R18]. Novelty bonuses and information seeking have behavioral and neural support [R19–R20], but novelty, reward prediction, uncertainty and arousal are distinguishable. RND operationalizes a novelty-like prediction-error bonus [R28]. For our model, rewarding unusual strings without verifying usefulness could create nonsense. This is why exploration bonuses are deferred to a separate intervention.

**Analogy and program learning reach further toward new routines.** Gentner’s structure mapping and Holyoak’s role-based account motivate transferring relations rather than surface features [R21–R22]. Rostral PFC imaging localizes parts of analogy processing without establishing a literal symbolic interpreter [R23]. DreamCoder learns reusable library functions alongside program search [R24]. Lake and Baroni train compositional meta-learning with standard transformer components [R25]. The Child as Hacker proposes learning by constructing and improving programs; it is a research framework, not a discovered brain algorithm [R26].

### Why transformers sometimes struggle, and what has measurably helped

There is no verified premise that transformers cannot originate useful combinations. Lake and Baroni provide a particularly relevant counterexample: a standard transformer trained across compositional tasks supports systematic reuse [R25]. Our corrected I1-H1 baseline fitting all four cells is another reason to reject the blanket claim.

They do not prove our failure has the same internal cause. |

Stale-association retrieval is not original thinking. This does not support adding the module here. |

The strongest small-scale **transformer-module** precedent here is relational abstraction. The closest **constructive-replay** precedent is Bakermans’s model, with Spens and Burgess providing a different generative-memory implementation. Neither establishes that our proposed replay sampler outperforms a transformer on matched practice. Generic deep generative replay also has a different purpose: Shin et al. mainly address forgetting by regenerating previous-task data [R36]. Generating new *combinations* must be measured separately from maintaining old performance.

For Track A, the evidence supplied by Ben is more specific than these general theories. The corrected baseline’s same-length relation-10 failure suggests a failure to reuse the terminal lookup under that distribution. Its longer-output failure is additionally confounded by untrained output positions. Reg+ctx’s exactly-three-call behavior suggests a stopping/program-tracking policy limited to practiced lengths. These are **suggested explanations**, not causal diagnoses. No longer-chain training or decoding was run for this note.

## Top recommendation and runner-up in detail

### First: a minimal phase of compositional replay

**Purpose.** Test whether internally generated, previously unasked questions create useful learning beyond spending the same number of updates rehearsing old questions. The first novelty target is **useful practice generation**, followed by transfer to an excluded question family. It is deliberately weaker than inventing a primitive operation.

**Inputs.** Remembered training stories and the raw questions asked during ordinary practice. Store visible facts and question tokens with provenance. The proposal component sees operation strings with entity names removed. It never sees validation worlds, their answers, a target test length or a list of desired successful examples.

**Smallest proposal model.** Fit empirical next-token counts over `{BOS, LINK, 8, 9, 10, EOS}` from ordinary questions. Sample walks from that learned transition table. A repeated `LINK → LINK` transition can yield a complete question longer than any observed question without inserting a hand-written four- or five-call template. Bind a sampled string to a remembered story and start person. This supplies a compositional bias: adjacent transitions can be reused in new sequences. It does not learn the principle of composition from nothing.

**Outputs and timing.** At the end of the 6,000-update ordinary-practice block, create a frozen buffer of sampled questions; then perform a 2,000-update offline practice block. “Sleep” means offline computation only. The sampler is independent of the dispatcher’s STOP policy, so a dispatcher that stops at three cannot prevent proposals longer than three.

**Fixed versus learned.** The transition probabilities are learned counts; the dispatcher learns through its existing RLOO objective. Story memory, the sampler, syntax constraints, a five-call proposal limit and training-world answer verification are fixed. The operator remains frozen. There are **zero added neural parameters**, at most 36 stored transition counts, and an episodic buffer. The proposed count model is intentionally simpler than a neural dream generator. Do not add novelty rewards, a critic, new memory slots or macro execution in this first experiment.

**Where answers come from.** A deterministic interpreter follows the proposed question in the remembered visible facts and returns a final answer to the reward service. It does not provide the controller with a path, next-operation pointer, remaining-call count or STOP target. This is explicit simulator supervision. It is neither label-free learning nor proof that the model invented a verifier. The learned operator still makes every native execution call and can be wrong.

**Connections.** During sleep the controller receives the same raw question and self-produced transcript as before. At deployment the proposal component is removed; the unchanged dispatcher/operator interface answers unseen questions. A paired plain transformer receives the same stories and questions, using its established step-target and attention-hint training recipe. Its stronger intermediate supervision is reported, not concealed.

**Novelty definition.** Repository `hops` counts all lookups, including the terminal attribute lookup. Write `c` for that count: `c=4` means three LINK calls and one attribute call. Ordinary practice has `c=1..3`. Replay may autonomously generate `c=4..5` with endings 8 or 9. Every composite ending in 10 stays excluded from all training, including replay. The main evaluation asks whether useful self-generated practice transfers to `c=4/5, ending=10` on new stories. Merely passing replayed 4/5-call types is synthetic practice success, not zero-shot inference on those types.

The companion draft fixes seeds, buffers, controls, cells and pass marks. Its core contrasts are recombination versus intact rehearsal at equal updates, and the same comparisons for the plain transformer. A simple random-grammar control asks whether learning proposal statistics buys anything beyond generic augmentation. If it does not, prefer the simpler sampler and say so.

### Runner-up: induce and verify a library of routines

After reliable execution, record successful traces as expressions over `lookup(person, relation)` with variables. A bounded search can identify repeated subexpressions, replace entity constants by arguments, and propose reusable functions. For example, a discovered `lookup(lookup(x, LINK), LINK)` can become a candidate two-link routine. Select a routine only when its description plus all rewritten programs is shorter than the previous library/program description, and when it agrees with its expansion on separate training-world audits. Keep both accepted and rejected candidates.

Start with a maximum of eight routines, depth three, and 128 candidate expressions per sleep block. These are proposed search limits, not verified timings. Search and a symbolic library add zero neural weights; eight width-32 learned routine embeddings would add 256 weights, while a learned selector would add more and require separate registration. Inputs are practice programs, example constraints and current library; outputs are parameterized routines and their provenance. A new interpreter adapter expands selected routines into calls to the existing frozen operator; log every expanded call and charge its actual cost.

This is closer to inventing a reusable operation, but our present question format already states a unary program. Compressing `LINK LINK` into a name may only save notation. A meaningful follow-up must test whether an induced routine solves new goals more accurately or with less *total* search/execution cost than: no library, a library with fixed human-written routines, and equal-budget extra search. Matching only macro-call count would hide work. Aliases need demonstrated or specified meanings; an unseen word cannot acquire a uniquely correct meaning from nothing.

I would choose this second because learned routine selection, variable binding, compression and execution are several interacting uncertainties. Adding it while the controller cannot reliably continue would make a negative result hard to interpret. DreamCoder motivates the direction; it does not make a miniature DreamCoder implementation automatic [R24].

### Recommended order

1. Finish interpreting the already-running v4 arms and preserve their results. Do not select a winning arm retrospectively for this comparison.
2. Freeze a common reg+ctx recipe and a corrected transformer recipe; establish ordinary-fit gates on fresh common training data. Preserve current checkpoints as historical evidence. Use an adequate common execution cap from the start; never force continuation.
3. Test replay as **one proposed remedy** for the three-call ceiling. It need not wait for an independent stopping fix: it generates longer practice without relying on the failing STOP head. If it only moves the ceiling from three to five, it has changed the curriculum, not learned unrestricted continuation.
4. Keep six-to-eight-call probes separate: they exceed both ordinary and replay practice. If those fail, investigate stopping/program tracking in a separately registered change. Do not add hand-supplied offsets or a recent-result marker to rescue this experiment.
5. Once execution is dependable, try library induction and a richer novelty task with alternative routines or goals. That is where claims about new routines can become substantive.

## What we must not claim

- Neuroscience has not identified a complete, necessary-and-sufficient algorithm for original thought. Replay, imagination, control, motivation, analogy and knowledge interact; their contributions vary by task.
- Observing an unusual neural sequence is not proof of insight, intention, usefulness or a new concept. Generative replay during awake inference is not automatically evidence for a sleep-specific mechanism.
- Correlation, direct recording, lesions, stimulation and computational simulation are different evidence types. Even a causal effect on one creativity test does not identify the full computational mechanism.
- Structural priors, a grammar, primitive labels, program interpreters and verifiers are supplied knowledge. Our sampler is engineered; only its transition statistics are learned. We cannot say the dispatcher spontaneously acquired a desire to practise.
- A replayed type is no longer held out. “Never externally practised,” “absent from all solver training,” and “new instance” must be reported separately.
- This first test can show useful autonomous question recombination and bounded transfer. It cannot show invention of a new primitive, an explanatory theory, a self-chosen goal or general intelligence. The unresolved gap to Ben’s broader goal remains explicit.
- Adding supervised long examples can repair trained output positions or a length prior. That is not proof of unlimited length extrapolation. Compare six-to-eight-call outcomes separately.
- A standard transformer can learn composition; current toy results and published counterexamples rule out a categorical impossibility claim. No result here shows humans have a capability unavailable to every transformer-based system.
- System S’s existing success depends on supplied bookkeeping features. Reg+ctx removes those two features, not all architectural assumptions. Grow-blind still uses gold intermediate people; never call it label-free. The current v4 seed-0 manifest points to a hinted canonical operator, not grow-blind: keep that operator provenance in the proposed comparison.
- Development panels are not untouched confirmation. Previously scored “confirmation” panels cannot confirm a newly selected design. No Track A finding establishes anything about the village model.

### Independent search and parallel report

The literature search and recommendation were developed independently before checking [Fable’s parallel report](/Users/ben-hannan/Desktop/projects/beautiful-model/.claude/worktrees/card-experiment-handoff-7c5b27/artifacts/fable-novelty-research-20260920/REPORT.md). It was absent at the first check and available at the final review. I agree with prioritizing constructive replay, keeping usefulness separate from unusualness, and treating macro discovery as a possible next direction. I disagree with several parts of its proposed experiment:

- **Separate replay from compression.** Its first treatment changes proposal generation, selection and the action library together. Start with replay alone; otherwise a gain or failure cannot identify which addition mattered.
- **Replay is training exposure.** If sleep supplies five-call examples, five-call questions are no longer never-trained types. The draft here quarantines all composite relation-10 questions, including during replay, and treats c=6..8 separately from practiced lengths.
- **A transformer success is informative.** The parallel report’s proposed requirement that the transformer stay below 15% would turn a strong control into an obstacle to the claim. We should measure the replay benefit within both architectures. Lake and Baroni and the corrected toy baseline also contradict a blanket description of transformers as unable to execute rules.
- **A learned lookup is not an independent correctness certificate.** It can make consistent errors. Using an explicit interpreter for training-world rewards is defensible if disclosed and isolated from held-out types; calling learned self-consistency verification does not make labels disappear.
- **Do not smuggle in a stopping rule.** Giving a macro a loop-until-done meaning requires specifying how it knows completion. Counting repeated fixed traces does not itself discover that rule. Likewise, the three-call ceiling does not establish a fixed-gain neuromodulation failure; a gain head is an untested alternative, not my runner-up.
- **LINK has a direction.** Reversing an association does not make a LINK fact symmetric, and reverse lookup may have multiple answers. A reversal probe needs a separately defined task; it is not a ready-made valid question in this world.

My source review also verifies Schacter and Addis (2007), whose details the parallel report left unresolved, and includes the 2024 DMN stimulation result, the 2025 compositional replay model and the 2026 cognitive-map study. These additions do not settle the general neuroscience account. I did not adopt or claim to verify the parallel report’s other citations merely because it listed them.
