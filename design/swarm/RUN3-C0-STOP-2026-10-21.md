## What this shows and does not show

- **Shown:** in a fresh process, as each real train runs, S0's FLOP calibration inside `train_d` peaks at 14.28 GiB
  allocated, above the 14 GiB guard. S0's first real training step needs 5.67 GiB. So cause (2) of the run-2
  record is confirmed, and cause (3), leftovers in the C0 process, is not what tripped it. Every registered S0 train runs
  this calibration first, so each would abort at its first step check.
- **Shown in the code:** under a seconds budget the FLOP fit steers nothing. The curriculum and the stop follow
  training seconds, and the FLOP budget is set out of reach (1e30). The fit is only FLOP accounting, but
  `MiniTrainer.train` refuses to start without one (`premonition/train.py:310-311`, `392-393`).
- **Not shown:** why the calibration needs 2.5× a training step's memory. One plausible reason is FlopCounterMode
  keeping the forward graph and its own bookkeeping for whole 55,000-token batches; that is untested.
- **Not shown:** anything about the swarm's accuracy. S0 is not slow: the steps-in-T ratio in run 2 was about 0.85.

## Design options (for the coordinator's review; none taken)

1. Calibrate on smaller pieces of the head batches. The fit is linear in tokens and question-loops, so a few small
   batches give the same kind of fit. This changes only the reported FLOPs, not training.
2. Reset the CUDA peak after calibration in `train_d`, so the guard covers training steps only. Calibration is an
   accounting pass, and 14.3 GiB is safe on a 24 GB Linux card. **This narrows the guard's scope,** which the coordinator
   has so far kept unchanged.
3. Calibrate D1-sized costs once in C0 and pass a fit to the S0 trains. Not equivalent: S0's per-question cost
   differs.
4. Stop the screen here.
