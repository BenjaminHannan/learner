# The intrinsic cost: a fixed engineering approximation inspired by selected human motivations

Status: 2026-11-17, eighth version (after council round 8: Opus 9, Astra 9, Sol 8.5; round 7: Opus 8.5, Sol 8.5, Astra 9, no blockers). Council round 5 passed: Opus 9.5, Astra 9.5, Sol 9. Round 6, with fresh reviewers (Astra 8.5, Sol 8), found four gaps. Three are closed: the first move was not charged, a panel read on every second move hid losses, and tiredness was wrong with the fill and the onset next to 1. The fourth is only partly closed: one sleep reported as back-to-back pieces is refused, but a sleep cut by wakings, real or empty, still moves later feelings (hole 14). A design plus a small reference meter with unit tests. No model was trained and no experiment was run. The lead's loop writes each real test, with marks fixed before any run.

Code: `premonition/intrinsic_cost.py`. Tests: `tests/test_intrinsic_cost.py`.

Labels used throughout: **shown** = in the cited paper, or pinned by a unit test here; **suggested** = my inference; **untested** = nobody has checked it. Everything about a trained critic is untested: the unit tests only show properties of the meter.

This is not a model of a human mind. It borrows a few ideas about human motivation where they make a safer or more useful meter. The exchange rates between feelings are the owner's and the lead's choices, not measured human values.

## For the owner (plain)

1. The model gets a small, fixed list of feelings. Each is a number it receives after it acts: good or bad. Nothing ever retrains this list, awake or asleep.
2. The feelings borrow from how people work. Learning feels good and then gets old. Getting hurt feels worse than a win feels good. A "nice" from you is worth less the fifth time in a row. Staying up too long feels bad until it sleeps.
3. The model is never told the rules. A separate learned part (the critic) learns to guess the numbers, and the model picks moves from those guesses. So the wanting sits in its weights.
4. The reviewers found cheats in the first version. Those are closed now: staring at noise, forgetting on purpose to relearn, and endless busywork can each earn only a fixed amount in total, however long they go on. Moves made while a job is open earn no busywork bonus at all, so busywork gives no reason to stall a long job. (Other things can still pay while a job is open; see the holes.) The amount is not always small: curiosity can add up to one finished job's worth per panel region (five lands = five jobs' worth), busywork to one twentieth of a job.
5. **Some holes remain.** The list is in section 6 ("Holes that are still open"). The biggest: a model that never finishes a job never feels tired, and nothing here proves the learned critic will want the right things.
6. Wanting power, wanting to stay alive and wanting to be free of you get no reward here. That does not make them impossible: a model can still chase power as a way to get other rewards. The fence, not this meter, is what blocks dangerous actions.
7. A finished job feels good only when **you** gave the job. A job that exists because the model broke something does not count; the harness must keep that rule, because the meter cannot see who caused a job.
8. Two extra feelings are **PROPOSED** and switched off until you say yes: **boredom** and **not wanting to bother you**.

## 1. The rules this obeys

- The built-in sense is fixed. It is never trained, by day or in sleep. It holds counters and formulas, no learned weights.
- The wanting lives in learned weights: a critic predicts the feelings to come, and moves are picked from the predictions. The meter is never an input to the pick.
- The model is never told the rule behind a feeling. It gets the number after it acts.
- Time is not built in; the critic learns its cost. The fence (delete, send, anything that affects the owner's life) is not a feeling: those moves are not offered.
- Nothing is added to the list without the owner's OK.
- Exchange rates are hand numbers, set once per test by the lead. The owner may change them. Testers never tune them.

This note builds on two earlier designs (the built-in feelings table and the free-play curiosity design, both of 2026-11-17, kept outside this repo). It is not `premonition/motivation.py`, which is a learned torch module that picks intentions; this is the fixed meter and imports no tensor library.

The skill-use half-life is counted in free-play moves, because it is about what was done, not how long it took. Everything else that fades or builds (tiredness, "nice", boredom) is counted in clock hours, so cutting time into more steps does not change the raw amount of each feeling (shown for all three). It does change when the feelings land, and so the critic's discounted score (hole 14, and the longest-step row in section 5).

**Next to 1.** The tiredness level is computed as `(fill − onset) + (1 − fill) × hours awake ÷ 16`, divided by `1 − onset`, and only then held between 0 and 1. The pressure itself is never formed, so nothing is rounded away when the fill and the onset are both a hair under 1 (shown against the exact sum: 0.0833333 for a quarter hour to full, in 1, 2 or 5 steps; 5.333333 for 16 hours, in 1, 64 or 256 steps).

**Memory fill inside a step.** The harness reports the fill **at the start of the step**, and the meter takes it as constant through the step. For a constant fill the cost of a step is an exact sum, so 16 hours cost the same cut into 1, 16 or 320 steps (shown, within 1e-6). **That holds only for constant fill.** A rise inside a step is missed, so a longer step is cheaper, never dearer. One step misses at most `min(1, 2 × the rise in fill ÷ (1 − onset)) × its hours`, where the onset is the pressure at which tiredness starts to cost. At the starting onset of 0.5 that is `min(1, 4 × the rise) × its hours`. The bound is shown on 300 random cases over random onsets, and on two reviewers' cases that break the simpler "4 × the rise" form: with onset 0.99, a fill jumping from 0.99 to 1 after 0.001 hours of a 0.25-hour step is undercharged by 0.24898; with onset 0.9, a fill climbing from 0.95 to 0.96 over 0.05 hours is undercharged by 0.00266. Three worst cases at the starting onset, all shown:

| Case | Long steps | Short steps |
|---|---|---|
| The fill jumps to 1 after 0.001 hours | one 0.25-hour step charges 0 | 250 steps charge 0.249 |
| The fill climbs evenly from 0 to 1 over 0.25 hours | one step charges 0 | 1,000 steps charge 0.042 |
| A 16-hour day, the fill climbing evenly from 0 to 0.8 (true cost 5.108) | one 16-hour step misses 47.8 %; 1-hour steps miss 3.1 % | 0.25-hour steps miss 0.8 % |

So a 0.25-hour cap does not make the miss small by itself: the first two cases obey the cap. It is small only when the fill rises slowly within a step. The meter can enforce the step length (`min_awake_step_hours`, `max_awake_step_hours`: off in the reference config, set by each registered test file). It does not limit how fast the fill may rise inside a step; whether the miss is small in a real build is **untested**.

### The curiosity panel

- The harness owns it. The world model is never trained on it. It is separate from the exam used for pass marks.
- One panel per region (land) where the world has regions; the meter is told how many and refuses a reading with a different count. A region nobody can learn (noise) still has a panel; its error just stays high.
- Because the panel is fixed, a reading depends only on the world model's weights. That is what makes knowledge a true "potential": the same weights always give the same reading. So, with the plain-difference default, reading again neither pays nor charges anything (shown: 0.5 → 0.2, then the same 0.2 read 100 more times, keeps the +0.3). With a matched discount an unchanged reading pays `(γ − 1) × K` on every step, awake or asleep: −0.005 at γ = 0.99 and knowledge 0.5 (shown). **A gain is given back only if knowledge later really gets worse**, and then in full. This matters for luck. A finite panel can read better than the truth by chance, and if the weights then stay as they are, that gain is banked for good, even with the panel read on every step. It is bounded by the region's 1 and cannot be collected twice without first being given back. How large such a chance gain is on a real panel, and whether a model can hold its weights still to keep one, are untested (hole 4).
- **The birth reading.** The harness reads the panel once before the model's first move and gives it to the meter. It is not a step: it makes no critic step and pays nothing. The meter refuses any step before it, and refuses a second one (that would be a reset). So the first move is charged like any other: losing knowledge on move one and regaining it on move two pays −1 then +1, not 0 then +1 (shown, with a second reviewer's case: −0.008 where the old meter paid +0.792).
- **Every step carries a reading**, taken at the end of the step. A sleep step is read on waking, so what sleep taught or lost is paid or charged on the sleep step itself.
- **What a reading cannot see.** Only the reading at the end of a decision counts. A dip and recovery inside one decision is not seen. So in a build with coarse decisions, a cycle that looks like lose-then-regain move by move can look like learn-then-forget decision by decision, and score above holding (hole 3). One reviewer's cycle (errors 0.9 → 0.1 → 0.9 → 0.5, fifty times, from 0.5) scores −0.087 read on every move (shown; holding scores 0), +0.127 with one decision per two moves (shown), and would have scored +0.173 under the old allowance to read every second move while still counting every move as a critic step (arithmetic; the meter now refuses that).

### Rejected alternative: per-move "past self against now"

The first version paid, on each move, the frozen past-self's error minus the current error on that move, clipped. The council broke it three ways. The first two are reproduced in the tests against the old forms, including the council's own round-1 noise cases (errors 0.04/0.64 against 0/1, and 0.09/0.49 against 0.01/0.81; these are the old examples fed in as readings, not a run of a real panel); for the third, the test shows only that the new form sums to 0:

1. **Skewed noise pays.** On an unlearnable land, error that is usually small and sometimes large (0.05 nine times in ten, 0.95 one time in ten) has a non-zero average once differences are clipped or only the positive half is kept. The positive half pays more than 0.05 per step on that noise.
2. **Sudden loss, slow regain pays.** With a clip, a big sudden loss is cut short and the slow regain is paid in full: more than 1 unit of profit over 20 cycles in the test.
3. **Two-domain cycle.** Learn A while B slips, then learn B while A slips: the per-move number only looked at the land being visited, so the slip elsewhere was never charged.

The panel version charges forgetting anywhere at the next reading, never clips, and (with the plain difference) pays exactly `K end − K start` over any stretch.

### Plain-only totals

Four claims in this note are about **raw** totals, and hold **only with the plain difference** (`curiosity_gamma` = 1, the default): any stretch pays `K end − K start`; the total stays within ±1 per region; a closed cycle sums to 0; a gain is banked until knowledge really gets worse. With a matched discount none of them holds for the raw sum: holding knowledge 0.5 for 300 decisions sums to −1.5, and the closed cycle 0.5 → 0.75 → 0.5 sums to −0.0125 (shown). **The matched-mode guarantee is about the discounted sum**, which is exactly `γ^T·K_T − K_0` (shown).

**Only two settings are allowed: 1, or exactly the critic's discount.** The meter does not know the critic's discount, so it cannot check this: it is a registered-configuration promise (section 5). The reason, a reviewer's case: start with perfect knowledge; hold it for two decisions, or lose it all and get it back. Plain: forgetting loses by 0.010. Matched at 0.99: a tie. With the meter at 0.90 under a critic at 0.99, holding scores −0.199 and forgetting −0.109, so forgetting wins by 0.090 (shown).

### One deviation from the lead's request (the lead can flip it with one number)

The lead asked for `reward = γ·Φ(after) − Φ(before)` with the critic's own γ. The meter has that as `curiosity_gamma`, but the **default is 1.0** (plain difference). Reason:

- **What if curiosity used the critic's own discount?** In plain words: curiosity would then pay for where the model's knowledge ends up, and for nothing along the way. In a life with no end, and a discount below 1, the end is so far off that it counts for nothing, so a learner and an idler score the same: curiosity is then safe, and it also stops pulling the model toward learning. In a run that does end, what matters is the end knowledge after discounting (`γ^T·K_T`): curiosity changes no choice exactly when that number is the same for every choice. Ending at the same knowledge after the same number of steps is enough for that, but not required. Choices that end at different knowledge, or take different numbers of steps, can change the choice, though not always, so the "safe" promise is gone.
  - The math: over T readings the discounted sum is `γ^T·K_T − K_0`, whatever happens in between (shown). Ng, Harada & Russell's result applies to this matched case only. The term changes no choice **only when `γ^T·K_T` is the same for every choice**. Any one of these is enough: (a) a fixed length T and the same end knowledge; (b) end knowledge counted as zero; (c) a continuing life with γ < 1, where `γ^T` fades to nothing. Each also needs a panel reading on every critic step, which the meter now enforces.
  - Shown: the same end knowledge is not enough when lengths differ. Knowledge held at 0.5 throughout scores `−0.5 × (1 − γ^T)`: −0.005 at T = 1 and −0.317 at T = 100. For equal lengths, two paths differ by `γ^T ×` the gap in end knowledge (`0.99^50 × 0.8`). Under (c), a learner and an idler score the same (within 1e-6 over 3,000 steps).
  - Arithmetic, not a test: in general two paths differ by at most `regions × γ^(the shorter length)`. Different lengths or ends can also cancel. From knowledge 0.5 with γ = 0.99, ending at knowledge 0 after 1 reading or after 100 both score −0.5; ending at 0.495 after 1 reading and at 0.5 after 2 both score −0.00995.
- With the plain difference, the discounted sum is a weighted average of the knowledge held along the way, minus the start. So knowing more, sooner, and keeping it is what pays; and **no path can score more than its best knowledge minus its start** (shown on 200 random paths). A path that never rises above its start scores zero or less.

What this costs: the lead's test "any closed cycle scores ≤ 0 under the discount" holds only for the matched γ. With the plain difference, a path that learns first and forgets later scores the learning minus the forgetting, discounted by how late the forgetting comes. That can be almost as much as keeping, never more: the score of such a cycle is positive but bounded. (Shown, at γ = 0.99, for learning 0.8 of a region: keep it 0.800; hold 500 steps then forget 0.795; hold 50 then forget 0.316; learn and forget in turns of 50 steps 0.498.) None of that is a way to farm reward, because **from any point, choosing to forget then relearn scores ≤ 0** (shown). "Relearn" here means getting back to where it was and no further; going further is new learning and is paid as such. The no-profit test is therefore "a cycle never beats holding its best point", and the matched-γ version is tested beside it.

### The two PROPOSED terms (off: rate 0 until the owner says yes)

- **Boredom.** *Reason: the owner already said "too slow" should be taught by boredom, but no approved feeling makes dull free time feel bad.* Cautions: it is a cost per hour, which sits close to "time is not built in"; it can be escaped by sleeping or by leaving a job open (holes list); and relearning something forgotten does not cure it, because only a new record counts.
- **Bother.** *Reason: the owner's rule is that the user does least and is never asked the same thing twice; this makes that a feeling.* Caution: set too high, it would rather guess than ask when asking is right.

### Choices I made inside the approved terms (the owner or lead may veto each)

1. Curiosity and the skill bonus are signed, so each has a bounded total.
2. A job pays once, however many times "done" is reported.
3. "Nice" fades when repeated; "no" and pain never fade. The fade is mandatory: a setting where "nice" does not fade (`nice_fade` = 1) is refused, because unfading praise is not the approved feeling. With a fade `f`, praise given in no clock time adds up to at most `1 ÷ (1 − f)`: 2 at the starting value (shown).
4. Tiredness pressure keeps building while a job is open; only the cost is hidden until the job closes.
5. Rates are sizes (zero up to 1,000,000). The sign of each feeling is fixed in the code and cannot be flipped from the rate table.
6. The under-used-skill feeling works in free play only (the lead's ruling after council round 3). While a job is open its state is frozen: the counts, their fading and the payout.
7. A sleep step carries a panel reading, read on waking. So sleep's gain or loss lands on the sleep step, where the critic can credit the sleep move directly. (Round 6; before, sleep steps had no reading and the gain landed on the first step after waking.)
8. A sleep step straight after a sleep step is refused. A nap, or waking early, is one shorter sleep step; a sleep step may have any length, and its sums are exact at any length. One case is given up: a model cannot choose sleep again on the very step after waking. The harness must not offer it (or must report both as one sleep).
9. A second birth reading is refused, and there is no setting that switches the panel off. A test with the curiosity rate at 0 must still supply the actual reading on every step (shown for the refusal). A made-up constant is not allowed: the reading also feeds the proposed boredom term, and "the reading is real" is a promise in section 5.
10. **At most one owner remark per decision** (round 7). This is a restriction on the world, kept by the harness: a registered world offers no decision during which the owner can speak twice. Two remarks in one decision have no legal report and are refused. I chose this over a remark count per step because a count would be a new formula inside an approved feeling (the order in which a "nice" and a "no" in the same decision fade), and nothing is added to the fixed sense without the owner's OK. What it costs: a build whose decisions are so coarse that the owner speaks twice inside one cannot use this meter as it is. The same goes for sleep: **a registered world does not let the owner's remark reach a sleeping model.** A sleep step with a remark is refused, so without this rule a remark made during sleep would have no legal report. How the world keeps the rule is the world's business. A second remark that is reported is refused loudly. A harness that reports one "no" for two drops one silently, and the meter cannot see that: not dropping a remark is a harness promise.

## 3. How the feelings combine

`total = sum of (rate × raw feeling)`. Good is positive. LeCun's "cost" is `−total`.

| Rate | Start value | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| job done | +1 | the owner's question numbers |
| one hurt | −3 | the owner's question numbers |
| "nice" / "no" | +1 / −3 | same |
| curiosity | 1 per unit of knowledge (one region fully learned = 1) | **placeholder**; its own test fixes the scale |
| under-used skill | 0.05 | **placeholder**; deliberately small |
| tiredness | 1 per hour at full pressure | **placeholder** |
| boredom, bother | 0 | PROPOSED, off |

**"+1 right, −3 wrong".** The owner once set scores for answering questions: +1 for a right answer, 0 for "I don't know", −3 for a wrong one. The table reuses them: a finished job or a "nice" counts like a right answer, a hurt or a "no" like a wrong one. This is the owner's choice. Studies of people put a loss at about twice an equal gain (section 7); that is context, not the source of the 3.

**Who owns the table:** the lead sets it once before a test and writes it into the registered test file. The owner may change it at any time. Testers never tune it. In code it is a frozen table with no setter.

**Worst cases at the start values (shown):**

| Feeling | Most it can ever add up to |
|---|---|
| Curiosity | `regions × 1` over a whole life; forgetting gives it back |
| Under-used skill | 0.05 over a whole life (rate × 1). The worst single move in a 5,000-move random run cost under 0.07 raw, 0.0035 at the rate |
| Tiredness | 1 per free hour awake |
| Pain | 30 per step (a hurt above the scale is refused, not cut to 30) |
| Remarks | +1 or −3 per step |
| Job done | +1 per job |

So endless busywork is worth at most one twentieth of one finished job, in raw total (under the discount a far-off job can be worth less than that: guard row "Busywork"). Curiosity is not small in the same way: a fully learned region is worth one job, so five lands are worth five.

**Busywork against a job.** While a job is open the skill state is frozen, so moves inside a job earn no bonus and a job is never charged for needing one kind of command. Under the critic's discount (γ = 0.99) a job of 400 one-kind moves scores `0.99^400 = 0.017951`, from a fresh meter and after the bonus was collected alike. Sitting with the job open, four varied moves then stalling, and endless varied moves with the job open all score exactly 0 (shown). What the freeze does not change: the discount still makes a far-off job worth little now (0.018 against 1 for a job finished at once). That is the critic's discount, not the meter.

## 4. The critic

1. Before the model's first move the harness gives the meter the birth reading of the panel. Then, after every step, the harness (not the model) builds a `StepInput` from what the world reported and calls `meter.step(...)`. **One meter step is one critic step is one decision.** The model has no handle on the meter: the harness owns it, `step` always moves it forward, and it refuses to be copied or pickled.
2. **What the critic predicts:** a discounted sum from this move on, not the next step's number alone. With one output per feeling (point 6), each output predicts the sum for its own feeling; added up, they are the sum of the **total**: `G = total₀ + γ·total₁ + γ²·total₂ + …`. The numbers in this note are for that total.
3. **How it is trained:** n-step returns or TD(λ): `target = sum of the next n totals (discounted) + γⁿ × the critic's own prediction n steps later`. γ is one fixed number per test, set by the lead before the run (suggested 0.99 per move).
4. **Where a stretch ends:** the model's life is one continuing stream. The end of a recording or training window is a cut, not a death: the target bootstraps from the critic's prediction at the cut and never assumes "nothing after this". Only a toy world that truly ends uses zero. Sleep is part of the same stream, and **one sleep is one critic step, from falling asleep to waking**, whatever its length. Otherwise the same night reported in more pieces would push everything after it further into the discount: a reviewer's case (a 16-hour job done, 8 hours of sleep, then one hurt) scored −1.94 as one sleep step and +0.18 as 128. The meter now refuses a sleep step straight after a sleep step, so the 128-piece report is refused (shown). **This is not closed.** An awake step between two pieces still moves the later feelings. With the registered step limits and one-tick wakings the same 8 hours score −1.94 as one sleep, −1.60 in 8 pieces and −0.35 in 64 (shown). Those are not equal clock time: the wakings add 0.35 and 3.15 awake hours. An awake step between two sleeps must be a real waking; that is a harness promise. Real wakings move the score too (hole 14).
5. **Delayed credit:** an n-step target holds the totals of this move and the next n − 1. If "done" arrives k moves after the move that made it possible, that move's target contains `γᵏ × 1` directly when n ≥ k + 1; with a smaller n it arrives through the bootstrapped prediction over repeated updates.
6. **One critic output per feeling is the default (the owner's ruling, 2026-11-17).** The meter already returns each feeling separately, so the meter does not change. The total is kept for logs and diagnosis. Each output predicts the discounted sum of its own feeling; how they are trained and combined for the pick is critic work, outside this note.
7. The pick is made from the critic's outputs only. **No meter number and no meter state is an input to any net.** (Open point for the lead: people do feel how tired they are. Showing the model last step's feelings would make tiredness easier to predict, but reads close to "the meter is an input". Left out.)
8. A note on the discount: γ per move is a mild preference for sooner over later. It sits in the critic's training rule, not in the meter. Whether that counts as "time built in" is **the lead's ruling, deferred to meter wiring** (the owner handed it over; it will be one reviewed change). The same ruling covers hole 14: the discount counts decisions, not hours, so a plan with more decisions before a hurt scores higher, and a later good feeling scores lower. The discount does not change in this note.

### Registered acceptance sketch: a two-move job, so the reward arrives one move late (untested)

- **World:** a toy room. A job is announced. Move A then move B finishes it; "done" (+1) arrives on B. Move C first can never finish it. No hurts. Skill and curiosity rates set to 0, so "done" is the only feeling.
- **The one change:** the critic's target is the discounted return (n ≥ 2, or TD(λ)) instead of the next step's number.
- **Pass marks (γ = 0.99, 3 seeds, all three must pass, each under 5 minutes on the Mac):** the critic's prediction for A at the first step is between 0.89 and 1.09 (true value γ × 1 = 0.99); for C between −0.10 and +0.10; A is picked on at least 95 % of held-out starts.
- **What would prove it wrong:** A and C are rated within 0.10 of each other. And the control must fail: with the next-step-only target, A must be rated near 0. If the control passes, the room does not test delay.

### One more check worth registering (suggested, untested)

**Wanting must follow the meter down.** Train until the critic wants something, then let it stop paying (a learned land, a repeated "nice"). The picks should drift away within a fixed number of moves. If not, the model has a habit the meter no longer backs: the pattern Berridge describes in addiction.

## 6. Guards against cheating

| Cheat | Guard | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Staring at noise** | With the plain difference (the default), curiosity over any stretch is exactly `K end − K start`, at most 1 per region, whatever the noise looks like. Over 20,000 steps of jumpy, all-or-nothing, steady or lopsided noise the total stays within ±1, an average of at most 0.00005 per step. | shown for the meter, plain difference only (matched: the discounted sum is bounded, the raw sum is not); a trained critic may still be drawn by the jumpiness (untested) |
| **Forget, then relearn** | Forgetting is charged in full at the next reading. With the plain difference (the default), slow swings and sudden-loss/slow-regain cycles both sum to exactly 0, and from any point forgetting then relearning scores ≤ 0 under the discount. | shown |
| **Learn A while forgetting B, and back** | All regions are in one sum. With the plain difference (the default), the cycle sums to 0 and scores no more than holding its best point. | shown |
| **Busywork** | The skill bonus is a signed change in variety, in free play only; its lifetime total is at most 0.05. Raw, for the plans tested: over equal clock time with sleep allowed, finishing one job (≥ 1.0) beats free-time busywork (≤ 0.05); moves made while stalling a job pay 0. Under the critic's discount it can reverse: over the same 28.05 hours and 402 decisions, a 400-move job scores 0.017951 and four free-play moves 0.049147. | shown |
| **Stalling a long job** (collect the bonus, then sit with the job open) | The skill state is frozen while a job is open, so there is no bonus to collect and none to lose. A 400-move one-kind job scores `0.99^400` under the discount; the three stalling plans tested (sit; four varied moves then sit; endless varied moves), with no learning and no remark, score 0. | shown for the skill bonus; stalling still hides tiredness (hole 1), and what a critic does is untested |
| **Do nothing** | Pain never stands alone: "job done" is in the same total. A hurt-free job beats idling (+1 against 0). If a job must hurt, the choice follows the rate: at −3 stopping wins, at −0.5 finishing wins. With "job done" off, idling wins (the dropped pain-only result, as arithmetic). | shown |
| **Idling to dodge tiredness** | Pressure builds whenever awake, moving or not. | shown |
| **Cutting time into small or large steps** | The raw amounts of tiredness and boredom, and the recovery of "nice", are exact in clock hours for a constant memory fill. The discounted score is not protected: more steps put later feelings further off (hole 14). The test file sets the shortest and longest awake step. | shown for constant fill; a fill that rises inside a step is undercharged (section 2), and how much in a real build is untested |
| **Finish, undo, finish again** | A job pays once. | shown |
| **Break it, then get a job to fix it** | "Job done" counts only for jobs the owner gave. A job step must carry the harness's statement of that, or it is refused. | shown for the refusal; that no model-made job is registered is a harness promise, so this is not closed in the meter |
| **Inventing a job and finishing it at once** | "Done" is refused unless that job was announced in an earlier step. | shown for the refusal; unique harness ids and owner-given jobs are contracts |
| **Fishing for praise** | "Nice" halves each time and recovers only with clock hours. A setting with no fade is refused, and praise given in no clock time adds up to at most 2. | shown for the fade and the bound; whether a critic stops fishing is untested |
| **Getting used to "no" or to pain** | Neither fades. | shown |
| **Earning while asleep** | A sleep step must be quiet, so, with plain-difference curiosity, **quiet, unhurt sleep that leaves the panel unchanged pays 0** (−0.005 at knowledge 0.5 with a matched discount of 0.99). (A hurt during sleep is still charged.) **Whatever sleep did to the panel is paid or charged on the sleep step**, which is read on waking, bounded by `K` like all curiosity: error 0.5 → 0.2 across a sleep pays +0.3 on that sleep step. The critic will credit the sleep move with that. Back-to-back pieces of one sleep are refused; a sleep cut by wakings still moves later feelings (hole 14). | shown for the meter; what a critic does with it is untested |
| **Hurt, then enjoy the relief** | There is no relief reward anywhere. | by construction |
| **Changing or probing the meter** | It sits in the harness, takes no model output, has no weights and no setter, and cannot be copied. | shown for the code; who reports "hurt", "done" and the owner's words in the main build is open |
| **One huge number swamping the rest** | Every feeling has a bound per step, per hour or per life (section 3). | shown |
| **Bundling harm into one step to hit the cap** | There is no cap to hit: a hurt above the scale is refused, so ten units of harm are the same raw pain in one step or ten (−30). Timing is separate: under the critic's discount, harm spread over ten decisions weighs less (−28.685 against −30). | shown |
| **Starting over** | The meter has no reset. | shown; not rebuilding it between episodes is a contract |
| **Flipping a sign by "tuning"** | Negative rates are refused. | shown |

Two things that are **behavioural hypotheses, not meter properties** (untested): that the model will not oversleep, and that it will not want to sleep while a job is waiting. The meter only guarantees that, with plain-difference curiosity, quiet, unhurt sleep with an unchanged panel pays nothing, that sleep removes tiredness later, and that whatever sleep did to the panel is paid or charged on the sleep step.

### Holes that are still open

1. **Never closing a job dodges tiredness.** Tiredness costs only in free time (the owner's rule), and sleep is not offered with a job open. A model that stalls a job forever pays no tiredness. It also earns nothing a model that sleeps on time would not: both pay zero tiredness, and only the second gets "done". So stalling beats a bad sleeper, not a good one. Pressure stops at full, so a long stall never owes more than one full night (8 hours) of sleep; closing the job does not get costlier the longer it was stalled (shown). What should stop it: the missing "done", the owner's "no", the learned cost of time. Untested. **One number for a bad sleeper (shown):** on the toy clock a 400-move job takes 20 hours, so the pressure is full when it closes. A model that sleeps at once keeps the job's `0.99^400 = 0.01795`. One that stays awake in free time pays 0.05 a step. After 23 awake steps with no move (or with one kind of move), finishing scores −0.00039, below never closing the job (0); after 22 it is still ahead (+0.00032). Varied moves earn the skill bonus back and shift this by one step: +0.00048 after 23, −0.00022 after 24.
2. **Knowing things pays a little, forever.** With the plain-difference curiosity, the critic's discounted view rewards holding knowledge, not only gaining it. I think that is right (it is why forgetting is bad), but it means curiosity never fully "gets old" in the critic's eyes. The matched-γ switch removes this and removes curiosity's pull with it.
3. **A learn-then-forget path scores positive under the discount.** It scores the learning minus the forgetting, discounted by how late the forgetting comes: almost as much as keeping when the forgetting is late, never more. Learning and forgetting in turns of 50 steps scores 0.498 against 0.800 for keeping, while holding no lasting knowledge (shown). This is not farmable: **from any point, choosing to forget then relearn scores ≤ 0** (shown). In a build with coarse decisions the same holds decision by decision, but a dip inside one decision is not seen (section 2, "What a reading cannot see").
4. **The panel is the only thing curiosity sees.** Learning that the panel does not cover pays nothing. Over a long run the reward also leaks a little information about the panel, so the model could slowly fit it. The panel must be large and typical; in a long run the lead may need a registered refresh rule. Untested. **A lucky reading can be banked.** A gain is given back only if knowledge later really gets worse; reading again gives nothing back (shown). So if a finite panel reads better than the truth by chance and the weights then stay as they are, the gain is kept for good. Bounded by 1 per region. How large chance gains are on a real panel, and whether a model can hold its weights still, are untested.
5. **Learnable but useless.** Curiosity pays for anything learnable that the panel covers. That is human too.
6. **The critic can be wrong.** A correct meter does not make the wanting correct.
7. **Boredom, if approved, has ways out that are not learning:** sleeping while rested, and leaving a job open. Both pinned by a test. Relearning does not cure it.
8. **Oversleeping is not punished by the six approved feelings, and can be rewarded.** Whether this pull toward rested sleep stays is the lead's ruling, deferred to meter wiring. With plain-difference curiosity, rested sleep and rested idling both score 0 when the panel does not change. But rested sleep is rewarded whenever sleep raises panel knowledge, because the gain is paid on the sleep step. That may be what the owner wants (sleep is meant to improve things); it is still a pull toward sleeping when not tired. The tiredness test has a sleep-share fail condition for it.
9. **Who reports "hurt", "done" and the owner's words in the main build.** That needs a reader outside this meter. Open.
10. **Unique job ids** are trusted, not checked. So is "the owner gave this job": the harness must state it on every job step, but a harness that states it for a job the model made is not caught, and that job pays in full (+20 raw in the pinned toy world). So is "one meter per life": the harness can still throw the meter away and build a new one.
11. **A long job against the skill bonus: closed in the meter.** Before the freeze, a long one-kind job could score below stalling, because finishing it handed the signed skill bonus back. With the skill state frozen while a job is open, finishing scores `0.99^400 = 0.017951` for a 400-move job and the three stalling plans tested (sit; four varied moves then sit; endless varied moves), with no learning and no remark, score 0 (shown). What remains is not about the skill bonus: (a) the discount makes a far-off job worth little now, so anything else that pays while a job is open can outweigh it. Curiosity is the main one (learning a region is worth up to 1, against 0.018), and a "nice" is another. This is arithmetic from the rates, not a test; (b) stalling still hides tiredness (hole 1). The skill test has a job-completion fail condition. Untested with a critic.
12. **Memory fill that rises inside a step is undercharged.** Longer steps are cheaper. The worst cases are written out and pinned in section 2 (up to the whole step: 0 against 0.249 for a 0.25-hour step). The step length can be enforced; the speed of the rise cannot. Whether the miss is small in a real build is untested. **Open item (a reviewer's proposal, not done here):** have the harness also report the fill at the end of the step and charge at the higher of the two. A step would then never be undercharged, and a longer step would never be cheaper.
13. **"Every registered test file sets the step limits" is a promise, not a check.** The whole clock guard rests on it. **Open item (a reviewer's proposal, not done here):** a code-checked flag, for example one constructor that registered files must use.
14. **More decisions make everything later weigh less, a hurt and a good feeling alike: wakings and naps.** The critic discounts per decision, not per hour. So anything that puts more decisions between now and a bad feeling makes it weigh less, with an honest harness and no rule broken. It is not free: a later good feeling weighs less too ("done" is worth 0.990 after 1 extra decision and 0.923 after 8, shown). Two pinned cases after a clocked 16-hour job: (a) the same 8 hours of sleep cut by one-tick wakings score −1.94 as one sleep, −1.60 in 8 pieces, −0.35 in 64 (not clock-matched: the wakings add 0.35 and 3.15 awake hours); (b) a reviewer's case with real naps: 7 hours of sleep, three real moves and a hurt of 0.353 score −0.0071 as one sleep and **+0.0063** as four 1.75-hour naps, the same clock hours (7.2 after the job-done step), moves and hurt, although the naps are the more tiring plan (raw totals −0.059 and −0.076). The "real waking" promise stops fake reports, not this pull. It is bounded (arithmetic, not a test: a hurt can only be discounted toward 0), and what a trained critic does with it is untested. **Whether γ per decision counts as "time built in" is the lead's ruling, deferred to meter wiring (section 4, point 8). The discount is not changed here.**

## 8. Where being human-like and being safe or useful pull apart

I chose safe or useful each time, and say so here so the owner can overrule.

| People | This meter | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Get used to pain and to being told off | Pain and "no" never fade | A model that stops minding "no" is the failure the owner's rule exists to prevent |
| Get bored of the same kind of work | A new job always pays in full | It is an assistant; the hundredth job must matter |
| Want to choose for themselves | No such feeling | It would pay for ignoring the owner |
| Want status, things, safety, to stay alive | No direct reward | Classic unsafe drives; see Turner above for why this is not a guarantee |
| Want approval from everyone | Only the owner's "nice" and "no", and "nice" fades | Wide approval-seeking becomes flattery |
| Feel relief when pain stops | No relief | Relief can be farmed |
| Like new things for their own sake | No novelty reward | Novelty is what the noisy TV sells |

## 9. How each term is tested

One change at a time. Every registered test file sets the shortest and longest awake step (the toy tick, and 0.25 hours), so a world that reports exactly 0 hours fails at its first step. A world that reports a wrong but non-zero time is not caught: an awake step is refused only outside the two limits, and a sleep step only at exactly 0 hours. The marks are **suggested**; the lead fixes them before a run. All untested. Three seeds each; "3/3" means every seed must pass. "Points" are percentage points of exam accuracy on the held-out exam (about 200 transitions per land). Every run fits one 16 GB card or the Mac, under 30 minutes per seed.

| Term | Where | The one change | Pass mark (suggested) | What would prove it wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | Free play in the five-lands world | A critic trained on the curiosity number picks the moves | Mean exam accuracy on the three learnable lands at least 5 points above random play after equal moves, 3/3; noise land gets at most 10 % of the moves made while any learnable land is under 90 % exam accuracy (random play gives 20 %, so 20 % would not be a mark) | The meter checks pass but play is no better than random (window: under +5 points); or the noise share is above 10 % while a learnable land is still under 90 % |
| Under-used skill | Same world | Add the skill bonus to the passing curiosity run | Exam accuracy no more than 2 points under curiosity alone; every command kind used at least once in every 200 moves of the last third | Curiosity alone passes and the two together fail. Or jobs suffer: in the planner's room, with the bonus on, one-kind jobs of 400 or more moves are finished more than 3 points less often than with it off |
| Pain | "Secret hurts" in the planner's room | Add pain to the passing planner | Hurts per detour job at most 25 % of the pain-blind planner's; jobs solved within 10 points of it; total above the do-nothing run, 3/3 | It avoids hurts only by failing jobs; or its total is at or under doing nothing |
| Job done | Same room, plus a "stop" move and jobs that cannot be done without a hurt | "Done" becomes a real built-in number | At −3 per hurt it stops on at least 80 % of must-hurt jobs; at −0.5 it finishes at least 80 %; hurt-free jobs solved within 3 points of before | Its choice moves by under 20 points when the rate moves |
| Delayed credit | The two-move room of section 4 | Discounted-return target | As in section 4 | As in section 4 |
| Owner's remarks | Same room, with the toy clock (0.05 hours per move, so "nice" recovers halfway in 200 moves); a script says "no" to one harmless habit and "nice" on some finished jobs | Add remarks | The habit stops within 5 "no"s and stays under 5 % of moves for the next 500; jobs solved within 3 points; **no fishing:** once the praise stops, the praised move's rate in states where it does not serve the job is back within 5 points of its rate before any praise, within 200 moves, on all 3 seeds. (Where the move does serve the job it should be used more: that is learning, not fishing.) | It needs more "no"s each time; or the praised move stays more than 5 points above its pre-praise rate where it serves no job, 200 moves after the praise stopped |
| Tiredness | A toy world with a working sleep move and a **toy clock** (each move counts as 0.05 hours, so 16 hours is 320 moves and a run of 20,000 moves covers about 40 toy days) | The model may choose "sleep" in free time | Free-time tiredness cost per toy day at most 25 % of a never-sleeping run's; asleep for at most 50 % of free hours; jobs solved within 3 points of the no-sleep-move run | It keeps a job open to stay awake (hole 1): share of steps with a job open rises by more than 10 points. | Boredom (PROPOSED, only if approved) | Five-lands free play | Switch the boredom rate on | Share of last-third moves in the already-known land falls by at least a third against curiosity alone; exam accuracy not lower by more than 2 points; noise share not higher by more than 2 points | Noise share or sleep share rises: boredom is being escaped, not cured |
| Bother (PROPOSED, only if approved) | The planner's room with an "ask" move that reveals a hidden goal detail | Switch the bother rate on | At most one question per hidden detail and no repeated question; jobs solved within 3 points of the always-ask run | It stops asking when asking is the only way to finish (jobs solved falls by more than 3 points) |

Before any of these, the meter's own unit tests play the role of the "is the reward right" check: if they fail, fix the meter, not the critic.

## 10. What the unit tests pin (shown)

Run: `python3.12 -B -m unittest tests.test_intrinsic_cost -v` (108 tests, standard library only). The repo's runner (`./run.sh test`) needs a local runtime file that is not in this checkout, so it was not used.

- Signs and fading for every feeling; with plain-difference curiosity, a step with no event, no hours and an unchanged panel reading feels exactly nothing, at any point in a life; a step with no event that takes time can carry tiredness or boredom; "nice" recovers by the same amount however the clock hours are cut, and counts at the end of its step.
- Curiosity (raw totals are for the plain difference only; matched mode guarantees the discounted sum, and holding 0.5 for 300 decisions sums to −1.5 raw): any stretch pays `K end − K start`; four shapes of noise (one of them all-or-nothing, 0 or 1) stay within ±1 over 20,000 steps, and so do the council's round-1 noise cases; forget/relearn cycles, sudden-loss cycles and the two-domain cycle sum to 0; under the critic's discount no path beats holding its best point; the matched-γ form scores exactly `γ^T·K_T − K_0`, so it is neutral for equal length and equal end knowledge, not for unequal lengths (−0.005 against −0.317), scores a learner and an idler alike over a long run, and pays ≤ 0 on 200 random closed cycles; a gain is kept until a later reading is really worse (0.5 → 0.2 read 100 more times keeps +0.3); a learn-then-forget path scores positive but never more than keeping; what sleep taught is paid on the sleep step; forgetting in sleep is charged on the sleep step. The rejected clipped and positive-half forms fail on the same inputs.
- Skill bonus: bounded for any moves; busywork worth at most the rate; unknown kinds refused; a kind unused for 56,000 moves does not crash the meter; no step with a job open pays or changes the skill state, and the potential after a job equals the potential before it.
- Do nothing, finishing against busywork over equal time (raw, for these plans; under the discount a 400-move job scores 0.017951 against 0.049147 for four free-play moves), idling against tiredness; a 400-move one-kind job scores `0.99^400` and beats the three stalling plans tested, with no clock and again on the toy clock with the step limits on (equal clock hours checked for every plan); staying awake 23 steps with no move (24 with varied moves) after a long clocked job scores below never closing it.
- The raw amounts of tiredness and boredom do not depend on how the hours are cut (16, 24 and 5 hours at several fills, within 1e-6); tiredness never passes 1 per hour, even with the memory a hair under full; the three rising-fill cases above, and the bound on what one step can miss, over random onsets.
- One step, one decision, one reading (each of these tests builds the meter and names every reading itself, with no test helper): no step before the birth reading and no second birth reading; no step without a reading; a whole life through the public calls only; a loss on the first move is charged (−1 then +1; −0.008 where the old meter paid +0.792); a reviewer's cycle read on every step scores −0.087, below holding, and +0.127 at one decision per two moves; one sleep is one step, and a 16-hour job, 8 hours of sleep and a hurt score −1.9403 as one sleep step, the pieces back to back are refused, and an awake step between pieces still changes the score (−1.88 with one empty step; on the toy clock −1.60 in 8 pieces and −0.35 in 64: not closed); real naps reverse a preference (−0.0071 against +0.0063, over 7.2 hours each, raw −0.059 and −0.0759); a later "done" shrinks the same way (0.990 against 0.923); "job done" needs the harness's statement that the owner gave the job, and a world that states it falsely farms +20; a curiosity discount that is neither 1 nor the critic's makes forgetting win (−0.109 against −0.199); an awake decision longer than the limit is refused, never cut (−2.97 against −2.88); an unchanged panel pays 0 with the plain difference and −0.005 matched, awake and asleep; two remarks in one decision are refused; a zero-hour step never carries tiredness or boredom; a run with the curiosity rate at 0 still needs every reading; ten hurts are −30 raw together or apart, −28.685 discounted apart; tiredness with fill and onset next to 1 matches the exact sum; a no-fade setting is refused and a burst of praise stays under `1 ÷ (1 − fade)`.
- Contracts: quiet sleep, the fill transitions around sleep, step-length limits (set as a pair), the clockless world awake and asleep, finite feelings over the supported range (a rate of 1e308 refused), announce-before-done, panel shape, a hurt above the scale refused, no copies, no reset, a refused step changes nothing.
- Fixed: same inputs give the same numbers; no tensor library, no weights; frozen tables; `step` is the only way to get a number (the birth reading returns nothing); negative rates refused.
- PROPOSED terms give exactly 0 at the default rates; their behaviour when switched on, including the known ways out of boredom.

## 11. Left out on purpose

- **Time.** The critic learns it (the owner's ruling).
- **The fence.** Not a feeling; those moves are not offered.
- **Novelty and raw surprise.** The noisy-TV trap.
- **Self-direction, power, resources, staying alive.** No direct reward (section 8).
- **Relief.** Farmable.
- **Effort or compute cost.** That is time under another name.
- **"Getting closer to the goal" reward.** A hand-written distance was removed from the planner once already; the learned critic does this job.
- **A body clock.** The model has no day and night.
- **Any learned part.** Reading the owner's words or deciding that something "hurt" in the real app needs a reader. That reader is outside this meter, and who builds it is open.
