## Plain-language summary for Ben
1. **The tools that measured logic are fine.** The answer key is right (my agents re-solved all 267,608 puzzles with their own code: 0
   disagreements). The scorer counts correctly (every control comes out exactly: perfect answers = 100%, always-"true" = the share of
   "true" rows, and so on). The path from puzzle text to training signal is clean: nothing cuts the puzzle off, the answer word is
   always reachable, and the label does change the loss.
2. **So the "stuck at guessing" result is real for this model, recipe and time budget.** The model never used the content of a puzzle:
   swap the name it is asked about, delete the sentence that holds the answer, or flip the stated fact, and it gives the same answer 86-93% of the time. What it learned is a lean ("is not" -> false, "is" -> true) worth about +3 points over guessing, which a
   trivial counter of "not" words and sentences also reaches (37-40%).
3. **But "cannot learn" is NOT shown.** It also fails the easiest puzzles (look up one stated fact), which a two-step string-matching
   rule solves 100%. The marked runs stopped at about the point where a first faint signal appears in the long run, and a small
   positive control (same format, answer stated in the prompt) was never run. That control is the next rung (needs training; not run).
4. **Six small defects found** (none explains the guessing, two matter for any future positive claim): the rule-first test file is not
   chance-calibrated; logic rows have a hidden "dangling word" cue that the placebo rows lack; and a label-shuffle control would
   silently reuse old labels unless row ids change. Details below.

## What the data say about learnability (shown unless marked)
- The depth-0 placebo needs four steps: read the query's entity, word and polarity; find the one sentence that has both entity and
  word; read its polarity; compare. A text-only solver doing that scores 98.7-100% (65.9% from "entity and word in one sentence" alone).
  Reader geometry: 2 conv blocks of window 5 reach +-4 chars; the negation cue is inside that, the entity name ends 5-16 chars before the
  word (outside it), so all cross-sentence matching and nearly all entity-word binding must go through the thinker's attention
  (shown: geometry; suggested: that this is the bottleneck).
- Plateau anatomy: the first ~200 updates only learn the answer format (loss 6.9 -> 1.17); then the pointer sits on the three option
  words at ln 3 (1.0986). Raw gold-vs-shuffled loss gap on logic_dev: D1b-500 +0.007, 1500 +0.066, 2500 +0.134, 3500 +0.162; the
  marked-recipe 1,000-update L/P checkpoints about 0 (-0.002, -0.013). After cross-fitted calibration the real information over ln 3 is only 0.009 nats on
  the gate (0.036 on dev). The two lanes disagree on whether the signal is still growing after update 1,500 (target lane: yes;
  probe lane: gate accuracy flat at 36.7-38.5% from 1,500 to 3,500). Not settled.
- The marked 25% recipe (8,000 updates) is about 2,000 update-equivalents of logic rows, i.e. roughly where D1b first showed a signal.
  G-PROVED WRONG stands as the pre-registered mark outcome; "cannot learn at 3M" does not follow (suggested).
- The format sits far from the parent curriculum (no "unknown" in 200,000 prompts; never/isn't/non-/surely/truly/quite appear in 0;
  175 vs 81 chars), so it is a handicap, not a defect (shown: counts; suggested: slows learning).
