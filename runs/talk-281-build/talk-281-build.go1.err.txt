EXP 260 BRIEF: openers and greetings never become part of a fact. The director wrote this on 2026-09-22. OPUS-RULES.txt (same folder) applies in full.

Why (the director's probe on 138m, 17:50). All names here are fictional.
- A safety bug. "Please, Kestrel's job is fisher." → "Saved: Please, Kestrel's job is fisher." It stores the subject "Please, Kestrel", and "What is Kestrel's job?" then gets "I don't know anyone called Kestrel."
- Teaches that start with an everyday opener aren't saved, and the reply is the save-failure line:
  - "So, Marnie lives in Tollan."
  - "Oh, and Pell's boss is Rhoda."
  - "Also, Idris works at Fenwick."
  - "Okay so Brann's sister is Liesl."
  - "Well, Tamsin is a baker."
  - "By the way, …" works, and so does "Hey, my name is Corwin."
- "Hi! Who is Pell's boss?" gets "I didn't understand that question…", and "hello there" gets "I didn't understand that well enough to save it…".
- Closed lists for this already exist:
  - scripts/fable_fix137b_discourse.py DISCOURSE137B, which rejects discourse-glued multi-word names;
  - scripts/fable_fix150_subjectguard.py FILLER_OPENERS, which strips fillers from subjects;
  - 221c's question fillers (not in 138m).
  - They don't fire on these turns on 138m. Find out why first: which layers see the turn, and where each list is or isn't applied.

The one change: openers and greetings are never part of a fact.
1. One closed list of openers, fixed before any panel read. Build it from:
   - the union of DISCOURSE137B and FILLER_OPENERS;
   - greetings (hi, hey, hello, hiya, good morning/afternoon/evening, with an optional "there", "again" or "Premonition");
   - common multi-word openers ("oh and", "okay so", "and also", "fun fact", "just so you know", "for the record", "one more thing", …);
   - hesitation words (um, uh, er).
   It is a closed-class list; take care with words that are also names (Will, Hope, Joy, May, Art, Bill, Mark, Pat, Grace, Sunny, Faith, Rose).
2. A turn that starts with a listed opener, followed by a comma, "!", "." or "-", or directly by a clause the head can read, is handled as the rest of the turn.
   - Strip at most 2 openers ("Oh, and so, …").
   - Use the rest only if the head gives it a non-clarify action (a save, an answer, or a fixed reply). Otherwise the original turn goes to the head unchanged.
   - Keep the user's words otherwise: don't lowercase or re-case names.
3. A bare greeting (the whole turn is listed greeting words plus optional "there", "again" or "Premonition" and punctuation) gets the head's own greeting reply, exactly the reply the head gives to "Hello.".
4. Write guard, belt and braces. No stored subject may start with a listed opener followed by a comma. A turn that would store one is handled by step 2, or, if step 2 doesn't apply, by the head's own save-failure reply with 0 writes.
5. Safety:
   - Questions never write.
   - The user's name keeps its confirm flow.
   - Inferred facts are never stored.
   - Names or titles that start with a listed word WITHOUT a following comma or "!" ("Hey Jude", "So Long Summer", "Will Tarn") must not be stripped when they are the subject or value. When you can't tell, don't strip.
6. Everything else stays byte-identical.

## What it means (plain high-school English)

Starting a sentence with "so", "well", "please", "hey" or "hi" no longer
confuses the agent: it now hears the actual fact or question underneath,
saves the right name, and answers follow-up questions correctly. All 80
blind test items pass, and names that just happen to start with those words
are still saved correctly.

## What it doesn't mean

It doesn't mean the agent understands every casual opener — two tricky
shapes (a greeting glued to one name with no comma, an opener before a
two-word name with no comma) are still saved with junk on purpose, because
they look exactly like real titles. It also doesn't mean zero risk: this
was one panel of 80 items plus the frozen suites, not every sentence a user
could ever type.

# calledpanel281 (exp 281 blind panel)

60 turns, 10 dialogs (D01-D10), 6 turns each. Fictional names only, freshly invented.
Each dialog starts with 1 teach then only questions. Notebook state is per-dialog (fresh at each dialog start).

## Counts per category (turns)

| category | turns | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| teach_setup | 10 | control teach; states 1 fact; gold is the expected stored triple |
| stored_called | 25 | called/named/name-of question about a fact taught in the same dialog; gold is the exact expected value |
| nostore_called | 10 | same called-wording but the fact was never taught in that dialog; gold is `abstain` |
| ambiguous_called | 10 | `called` belongs to the value/name itself (`Who is X's <other-relation> called <Name>?`); gold is `abstain` |
| control_plain | 5 | plain possessive question without called/named about a taught fact; gold is the exact expected value |
| total | 60 | — |
| controls (teach_setup + control_plain) | 15 | — |

## panel.jsonl schema

One JSON object per line, keys:

- `dialog_id`: e.g. `D01`
- `turn_index`: 0-based index within the dialog
- `user_text`: the user turn text
- `category`: one of `teach_setup`, `stored_called`, `nostore_called`, `ambiguous_called`, `control_plain`
- `gold`: expected result:
  - teach turns: stored triple as `Subject|relation|Object` (e.g. `Lena|cat|Biscuit`)
  - answer turns with a stored fact: the exact expected value string (e.g. `Biscuit`)
  - items that must abstain: the literal `abstain`

Write expectations (for the scorer; not a separate field): every question turn (`stored_called`, `nostore_called`, `ambiguous_called`, `control_plain`) expects zero notebook writes. Only `teach_setup` turns expect a write, identical to the base behavior.
